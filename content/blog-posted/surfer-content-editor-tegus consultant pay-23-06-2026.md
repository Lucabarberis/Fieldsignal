## How Tegus consultant pay works

Here's the basic process of tegus consulting compensation:

* Clients (private equity firms, venture capital, hedge fund managers, corporate strategy teams) pay Tegus for access to expert calls and a searchable transcript library. Tegus has facilitated over 25,000 client calls.

* You set your own consulting rates on the tegus platform. When a client wants to speak with you, Tegus presents your profile, rate, and screening answers.

* Rates are pro-rated based on call length. A 40-minute call at $300/hour yields roughly $200. A 45-minute call yields $225.

* Calls are recorded, transcribed, and shared across the entire client base. Expert names are removed, but roles and bios are preserved. Transcripts of calls are available to all Tegus clients. Experts do not receive compensation for transcript access by other clients.

* Tegus charges clients a $75 markup on expert fees. You don't pay Tegus to join or accept work.

* Payments to consultants are processed within 15 business days via [Tipalti](https://expertopportunities.com/tegus-review/). You choose ACH, wire, or other options.

* Tegus connects experts primarily through LinkedIn outreach. Tegus onboarding takes about 20 to 30 minutes to complete, and you must answer compliance questions during the onboarding process. Onboarding includes a non-compensated introductory call with Tegus.

Consultants report a very smooth and professional process. Consultants appreciate the flexibility offered by Tegus. But remember: you're paid only when selected and when the client call happens. This is variable, project-based consulting income, not a salary.

## What affects your Tegus earning potential?

Your rate and call frequency depend on factors you control and some you don't.

* **Current or recent roles.** Being a former employee of a company that investors follow closely gives you an edge. Clients want specific questions answered about specific firms.

* **Niche domain knowledge.** Deep research backgrounds in specific SaaS modules, medical devices, or industrial supply chains beat generic "strategy consultant" profiles on both selection odds and rates. Expert consultants can negotiate higher rates based on demand and specialization.

* **Geography and language.** Experts in under-covered regions (EMEA, APAC) or who can speak multiple languages attract premium invitations.

* **Responsiveness.** Quickly accepting screeners, filling compliance forms, and offering several time slots improves how often you're chosen for expert calls. This is a point many experts overlook.

* **Profile clarity.** Clients see several potential industry experts for each brief. Concrete numbers, named companies, and product names in your bio beat vague buzzwords every time.

* **Multi-network presence.** The competitive landscape of expert networks includes GLG, AlphaSights, Third Bridge, Guidepoint, FieldSignal, and others. Many experts sit on several platforms at once to smooth out earnings across a broader research ecosystem.

## Setting your Tegus rate: strategy by career stage

How you set your rate affects both short-term selection and long-term earning power. Consultants can set their own rates based on expertise, so be deliberate.

* **Early-career / manager level.** Start slightly below the midpoint of the $100 to $200 band to build a track record. Get 2 to 3 calls under your belt, then step rates up once demand is consistent.

* **Director / VP level.** Start around $250 to $350/hour. If you're receiving frequent invites or clients request you by name, push up. This is where most professionals doing tegus consulting land.

* **C-suite / board / rare technical profiles.** Quoting $400 to $600+ per hour is reasonable if you can point to P&L ownership, regulatory responsibility, or product launch leadership.

* **Opportunity cost.** Weigh your day rate or salary equivalent against spending 45 to 60 minutes on a consultation. If it's not worth it, set higher and accept fewer calls.

* **Incremental moves.** Changing your rate between projects is possible, but rapid swings confuse repeat clients. Move $50 to $100 per hour at a time.

## Compliance, MNPI, and how it affects pay

Compliance rules shape what you're paid for and what you must avoid.

Tegus and other expert networks explicitly prohibit sharing material non public information. You can only speak from publicly available information, public information, and your own general professional background and experience.

Every call is recorded and transcribed, creating a permanent record. You must avoid disclosing confidential information from a current employer or anything not already available through sec filings or other public channels. [Fortune reported](https://fortune.com/2024/06/10/alphasense-tegus-venture-capital-a-i-goldman-sachs-mergers/) that Tegus compliance checks are strict, with MNPI guidance reiterated before and during each client call.

You're allowed to decline to answer specific questions without losing compensation. Staying within public information protects you and the client from regulatory problems.

Higher pay doesn't come from "edgier" data. It comes from sharper judgment, clearer explanations, and more relevant experience. FieldSignal adopts equivalent or stricter compliance standards, with pre-call briefings and call monitoring.
