# AlphaSense vs Tegus: Research Workflow Compared

AlphaSense and Tegus served different research needs before AlphaSense acquired Tegus for $930 million in July 2024. AlphaSense was built for AI-driven document search across filings, broker research, and news. Tegus was built for expert interview transcripts and private company intelligence. Now they're one platform, and the real question is whether that combined offering fits your workflow and budget.

Below is a practical comparison of how each workflow operates, where each excels, and what alternatives exist if you don't need a six-figure annual contract.

## AlphaSense vs Tegus: Key Differences

The core difference is secondary research versus primary research.

* AlphaSense's platform focused on ai driven market intelligence: searching, summarizing, and analyzing public documents like earnings calls, equity research, company filings, trade journals, and news at scale.

* Tegus specialized in expert transcripts and qualitative data from former employees, executives, and industry insiders. Tegus provides expert research on over 35,000 companies, with a cleaner UI designed for deep linear reading of custom interview content.

* AlphaSense acquired Tegus to create a unified financial and market research platform. The deal valued AlphaSense at over $4 billion, with AlphaSense's valuation boosted by new investors including SoftBank Vision Fund and Alphabet's CapitalG in an additional funding round co led by those firms.

* AlphaSense raised $650 million in additional funding to support the acquisition and accelerate growth.

The combined platform now offers both document analysis and expert calls in one interface. That's a significant leap forward for teams that need both. But it also means you're paying for both, whether you use them or not.

## Data Sources, Financial Data, and Content Access

### AlphaSense Content Workflow

AlphaSense covers over 35,000 public and private companies through its search platform. Its content sets include:

* SEC and global regulatory filings

* Earnings call transcripts

* Broker research and equity research reports

* News, trade journals, and ESG disclosures

* Internal content through an Ingestion API for proprietary documents

AlphaSense offers extensive quantitative financial data across these sources. Automated content ingestion and tagging make discovery faster than manual archival lookups. The platform holds over 500 million external documents, all machine-tagged and indexed.

Strong coverage of public companies and macro research themes. If your workflow revolves around financial data and public market analysis, this is where AlphaSense wins.

### Tegus Content Workflow

Tegus is renowned for high-quality, investor-led expert interview transcripts. The library spans 35,000+ public and private companies, with roughly 240,000 transcripts growing at about 8,000 per month.

* Primary research through expert calls with former employees, suppliers, and specialized buyers

* Private company intelligence not found in public filings

* Tegus maintains a larger database of conversations with specialized buyers and executives

* Qualitative insights covering product feedback, leadership quality, customer sentiment, and unreported risk

Tegus is preferred for deep-dive research into niche industry dynamics. If you need private company data or insider perspectives for due diligence, Tegus content capabilities are stronger than anything in AlphaSense's original document library. AlphaSense said it would add Tegus employees, and those Tegus employees would help scale the transcript library and strengthen financial data and workflow tools.

## AI Search and Analysis Capabilities

### AlphaSense Research Process

AlphaSense's NLP is built to analyze large volumes of unstructured text. The research process works like this:

1. Enter a natural language query. The search returns cited insights from documents, transcripts, and financial data.

2. Smart synonyms expand your query automatically, catching related terms you might miss.

3. AI-powered synthesis delivers insights over the course of an analysis in near real time, not just as a static summary across hundreds of documents.

4. Trend analysis tools let you track how topics evolve across multiple time periods.

5. Table Explorer extracts structured data from filings for spreadsheet-based analysis.

AlphaSense's generative AI tools support faster decision making by automating in-depth analysis creation. Features like Generative Grid enable multi-company comparison across themes. Integration with Excel and PowerPoint supports quantitative workflow tools directly.

**Winner for speed and breadth:** AlphaSense. You get answers in seconds across millions of documents.

### Tegus Research Process

Tegus's search process is built around human insight rather than document volume:

1. Search transcripts by company, topic, expert role, or time period.

2. Filter by former employee tenure, geography, seniority, or functional area.

3. Schedule custom expert calls with industry insiders for fresh perspectives.

4. Synthesize qualitative data across multiple expert perspectives to validate hypotheses.

Tegus provides a cleaner UI designed for deep linear reading of custom interview content. That matters when you're spending hours reading through expert transcripts during due diligence.

**Winner for depth and qualitative insight:** Tegus. You get nuance that no document search can replicate.

## Pricing and Accessibility

Neither platform publishes pricing. Both require enterprise-level annual contracts.

* Subscription tiers typically labeled Standard, Premium, and Enterprise

* Pricing depends on seat count, content access level, and add-ons like expert calls

* Per-seat cost drops at higher seat counts, but minimums remain high

* Expert call credits, API access, and internal content integration are often add-on costs

The combined platform likely maintains enterprise pricing. AlphaSense's valuation reached $4 billion after funding, and the company's ARR grew from roughly $200 million in April 2024 to over $500 million by late 2025. That growth comes from enterprise clients, not small teams.

**The accessibility problem:** If you're a mid-market PE fund, a boutique consulting firm, or a founder doing pre-investment research, six-figure annual retainers don't make sense. You're paying for content sets and workflow tools you won't use. Existing investors like Viking Global Investors, Goldman Sachs Alternatives, MSD Partners, and Alkeon Capital back AlphaSense's enterprise model, but that model isn't built for you.

## Market Intelligence Integration and Workflow Features

The combined platform offers a broad set of tools:

* **Deep Research:** AI agent that builds reports automatically across documents and transcripts

* **Generative Search:** Natural language queries with cited results

* **Workflow Agents:** Automate recurring monitoring and analysis tasks

* **Financial modeling:** Via Canalyst (acquired separately), enabling data and workflow tools for quantitative analysis

* **Table Explorer:** Extract structured financial data from filings

* **Expert Calls:** Schedule interviews, manage transcripts, set interview guides

* **Internal Content:** Upload and search proprietary documents alongside public data

AlphaSense features generative AI tools for business-grade insights, reflecting its broader product innovation direction. The platform consolidates what previously required separate subscriptions: one for document intelligence, one for expert research, one for financial modeling.

The trade-off is a steep learning curve. Rapid feature releases mean frequent UI changes. Users report high onboarding costs. Managing both document and expert content workflows demands skill, and not every team has the bandwidth to remain focused on mastering both.
