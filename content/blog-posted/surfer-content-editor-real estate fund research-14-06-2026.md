# Real Estate Fund Research: Underwriting Methods

Real estate fund research is the process of evaluating whether a commercial real estate fund will deliver the risk-adjusted returns it promises. Underwriting methods are the analytical frameworks that make that evaluation possible. If you're a PE/VC associate, corporate M&A analyst, or boutique consultant evaluating a manager's next fund, this is the work that matters most.

## Overview: How Real Estate Fund Research Uses Underwriting

Underwriting in this context isn't about approving residential mortgages. It's about sizing projected income, expenses, financing, and exit value for commercial real estate investing decisions at the asset and fund level. Underwriting assesses risk and potential returns of investments, and only about 5% of potential investments pass underwriting. The practice originated from the insurance industry in the 17th century, but today it's the backbone of credible real estate investment analysis. Underwriters evaluate borrower creditworthiness and property value, and that assessment is crucial for financial stability in commercial real estate.

The three primary types of real estate funds are REITs, private funds, and debt/mortgage funds. Public REITs are traded on major exchanges and offer high liquidity. Public REITs are also heavily regulated investment vehicles. Private funds typically have longer lock-up periods of 5 to 10 years, and liquidity risk varies between public and private funds relative to holding periods. ## Underwriting Methods In Real Estate Fund Research

Underwriting methods are the frameworks and inputs used to decide whether a commercial real estate fund or deal meets return hurdles like IRR, MOIC, or a stated hurdle rate. The major approaches used in real estate funds:

1. **Discounted cash flow (DCF)**: Projects cash flows over the hold period, discounts them back at a required return. Handles irregular cash flows like lease-up or capital events.

2. **Direct capitalization**: Value equals NOI divided by market cap rate. Fast, but only works for stabilized assets with strong comps.

3. **Comparable sales and rent comps**: Anchors assumptions in observable market transactions. Lagging data is the main weakness.

4. **Scenario and sensitivity analysis**: Varies key levers like exit cap expansion, rent growth, occupancy, and financing terms across base, adverse, and upside cases.

Institutional-quality managers standardize their underwriting process across deals to compare risk-adjusted returns consistently. Fund-level research looks at repeatability of the manager's underwriting, not a single model. LP memos often show polished models, so external research should [test the underwriting logic](https://apers.app/learn/financial-modeling/valuation/dcf-vs-direct-capitalization-when-to-use?utm_source=openai) with independent experts who've seen the same assets or markets.

## Core Components Of Commercial Real Estate Underwriting

Every serious underwriting model revolves around income, expenses, financing, and exit assumptions, regardless of whether you're looking at office, industrial, multifamily, retail, or specialized property types like data centers. Underwriting is iterative and should be updated between LOI, PSA, and closing as new findings arrive.

### Income And Rent Growth Assumptions

Underwriters model base rent, escalations, reimbursements (NNN, gross, modified gross), and other income like parking or signage. Typical assumptions include 2 to 3 percent annual rent growth in stabilized multifamily, while 2019 to 2023 last-mile industrial in markets like Dallas or Inland Empire saw double-digit growth. Rental income projections must account for loss-to-lease, vacancy, credit loss, free rent, and lease terms.

Key questions for managers:

* Which rent comp datasets did you use? CoStar, RCA, local brokers?

* How do in-place rents compare to effective market rents?

* What vacancy and concession assumptions did you use vs historical submarket data?

### Operating Expenses And Property Condition

Real estate underwriting must separate controllable operating expenses (repairs, payroll, marketing) from non-controllable (taxes, insurance, utilities). Property condition reports (PCA/PNA) should feed directly into CapEx and reserve line items, especially for 1980s and 1990s vintage assets needing roof replacements or HVAC overhauls.

Post-2020 realities changed the expense picture. Insurance premiums for multifamily have [jumped roughly 75% since 2019](https://blog.kardin.com/blog/cre-insurance-budget-rebuild-2026?hs_amp=true&utm_source=openai), now accounting for about 8% of operating expenses. The insurance industry's repricing of risk in coastal and storm-exposed markets hit deal economics hard. Market and economic risks impact property demand based on regional and property-type fundamentals.

Ask former property managers: do the sponsor's expense ratios match what you've actually seen in this metro?

### Capital Expenditures And Business Plan

CapEx in commercial real estate covers non-recurring costs that extend useful life or improve NOI: roof replacement, common-area renovations, HVAC upgrades, dock door additions. Value-add and opportunistic funds underwrite CapEx-heavy plans with staged timelines, contingency buffers of 10 to 15 percent, and projected rent premiums.

Example: a 5-year industrial fund underwriting dock door additions and office build-outs in years 1 to 3, expecting rent step-ups by year 4. The risk is in permitting delays, contractor availability, and construction material inflation, which has run 30 to 40 percent higher since 2020 in many markets. Aligning CapEx schedules with local realities is where many business plans fall apart.

### Financing Structure And Interest Rate Assumptions

Leverage, interest rate type (fixed vs floating), loan term, amortization, and covenants all feed into the underwriting model. Typical LTVs for stabilized core assets range roughly 60 to 70 percent. Value-add or bridge lending may push to 75 to 80 percent. High leverage over 65% can amplify gains but increase vulnerability to interest rate fluctuations. The debt service coverage ratio minimum for stabilized assets is typically [1.20 to 1.25x](https://www.radcre.ai/resources/loan-sizing?utm_source=openai), with riskier deals requiring 1.30x or higher.

Many 2021 to 2022 vintages underwrote floating rate debt and short-term interest rate caps. In hindsight, those look risky. Stress testing should cover refinance risk at maturity, cap expiration, and covenant headroom under different NOI and occupancy scenarios. Lenders now often underwrite assuming rates 100 to 200 basis points higher than current.

### Exit Cap Rates And Valuation

Exit cap rates typically assume some expansion from entry cap rates to reflect uncertainty. CBRE reported 2024 cap rates at roughly [5.2% industrial, 5.3% multifamily, 6.4% office, and 6.4% retail](https://www.cbre.com/insights/viewpoints/a-multi-perspective-view-on-cap-rates?utm_source=openai). Property valuation at exit is the single largest variable in most models.

Concrete example: underwriting a 50 to 100 basis point cap rate expansion for core-plus multifamily bought in 2019 to 2021 vs a smaller spread for long-lease industrial. Fund researchers should benchmark a manager's exit cap assumptions against historical NCREIF, RCA, or Green Street data. Ask experts: does current buyer demand and debt market conditions justify the sponsor's assumed cap rate at a 2028 to 2030 exit?


## From Asset-Level Underwriting To Fund-Level Research

Investors care less about a single deal and more about how a manager underwrites across a portfolio and over a full fund life. Analysts roll up property-level models into fund-level cash flows, management fees, carried interest, and net IRR/MOIC. Internal rate of return is a common metric for private funds measuring expected total return. Many of the manager-evaluation patterns here mirror those covered in our broader [private equity research guide](/blog/private-equity-research-a-practical-guide).

Research core areas include fund structure, manager track record, portfolio composition, fee structures, and macroeconomic environment. Key factors to examine include asset class, geographic focus, and the leverage used by the fund. Real estate funds often carry substantial fees that erode returns. Management fees are typically around 1.5% to 2% of assets under management, and performance fees often involve a cut of 20% of profits after returning initial capital. Investment objectives should align with the strategy's risk profile.

### Evaluating Historical Underwriting Accuracy

Compare original underwriting models to realized outcomes on rent growth, occupancy, operating expenses, CapEx timing, and exit pricing. Create simple attribution: which part of the underwriting drove over- or under-performance? Historical operating performance is analyzed in market assessments to identify patterns.

Nareit research for 1998 to 2023 found that value-add and opportunistic funds over core [delivered under 1% annual net excess return](https://www.reit.com/news/blog/market-commentary/private-value-addopportunistic-real-estate-net-total-return-spreads?utm_source=openai). Consistently aggressive rent growth assumptions or chronic underestimation of CapEx is a red flag. Former asset managers from the same firm or competitors are key expert sources to validate whether issues were structural or isolated. That's where a strong track record matters, and where background checks on a manager's prior funds pay off.

### Comparing Commercial Real Estate Investment Strategies

Underwriting methods differ across core, core-plus, value-add, and opportunistic strategies. Core open-end funds focus on stabilized office and multifamily with conservative leverage. Closed-end value-add funds target secondary market industrial repositioning with higher leverage and more leasing risk. Fund research should check whether underwriting assumptions match the stated strategy. More aggressive strategies require more granular sensitivity analysis and stress testing from investors and consultants. Market volatility affects each strategy differently, and accredited investors should understand the potential risks before committing capital.

## Integrating Due Diligence With The Underwriting Process

Underwriting is the hypothesis. Due diligence is the verification step before capital is committed. Due diligence checks financial, legal, and physical property details. Due diligence typically spans 30 to 90 days for commercial real estate. A thorough due diligence checklist prevents costly investment mistakes, and due diligence confirms underwriting assumptions after signing a contract. Investors can negotiate terms if due diligence reveals issues. These are critical diligence steps and due diligence steps that protect against risk factors.

For fund investors, diligence also includes assessing the GP's track record, reporting quality, governance, and asset management capabilities. Investment involves verifying that the GP's practices actually support the assumptions in their models, helping you make informed decisions.

### Financial, Tenant, And Lease Due Diligence

Validate rent rolls, T-12 financials, tenant credit, lease clauses (options, abatements), and recovery structures before closing. Review the tenant mix and credit history of anchor tenants. Common mismatches include assuming full recoveries in a multi-tenant office where leases cap CAM pass-throughs.

Questions to ask: What's the estoppel collection rate? How many leases were reviewed only in summary form? Were third-party financial audits completed? Does the purchase price reflect confirmed income or projected income?

### Property Condition, Environmental, And Market Due Diligence

PCAs, Phase I/Phase II environmental assessments, and seismic reports inform updates to CapEx, insurance, and risk premiums. Older multifamily with deferred maintenance or industrial assets in flood zones directly affect lender terms and collateral value. Natural disasters in exposed regions can reshape insurance costs overnight. Property condition findings must feed back into the final underwriting, not just sit in a file.

Market analysis includes economic and demographic evaluations, property inspections, and assessment of competitive positioning within local markets. Future estimates of property performance and market trends for metros like Phoenix, Atlanta, or Austin should be validated against new supply pipelines. These factors determine whether investment decisions are sound.
