# Tegus vs GLG: Pricing, Coverage, When to Use Which

Tegus gives you a searchable library of 200,000+ expert call transcripts with predictable subscription pricing. GLG gives you live access to over 1 million experts globally with premium per-hour rates. The right pick depends on whether you need archival research at scale or custom, real-time expert consultations.

Both platforms serve investment professionals, corporate strategy teams, and consulting firms. But they solve different problems at different price points. Here's how they compare.

## Tegus vs GLG: Key Differences

The core difference is content model vs. service model.

* Tegus is built around an extensive transcript library. You search prior expert interviews, spot market trends, and build conviction before making a single call. It's a self-service investment research platform, now part of AlphaSense.

* GLG is built around live expert consultations. You describe what you need, a research manager finds the right person, and you get on a call. It's a full-service global expert network.

Your choice affects research speed, cost structure, and how you source specialized knowledge. Tegus favors teams that do high-volume transcript-driven research in sectors like TMT and healthcare. GLG favors teams that need bespoke expert matching across broad industries and geographies.

Both are legitimate expert network platforms. They just optimize for different parts of the research process.

## Research Approach and Content Access

Tegus delivers insights you can access immediately. GLG delivers insights you need to schedule.

* Tegus prioritizes speed and volume. You gain instant access to thousands of pre recorded expert interviews without waiting for a call.

* GLG prioritizes depth and specificity. You get a hand-picked expert who answers your exact questions in real time.

The trade-off is clear: Tegus is faster for broad pattern recognition. GLG is better when you need custom research on a narrow topic.

### Tegus: Transcript Library and Self-Service Research

Tegus has 200,000+ expert transcripts covering 25,000+ companies, both public and private. The library grows by roughly 8,000 transcripts per month.

Transcripts come from investor-led expert interviews, AI-led channel checks, and competitive analysis calls. Experts are typically former executives, operators, customers, and suppliers with firsthand knowledge.

The technology platform includes ai powered search, sentiment filters, generative summaries, and side-by-side transcript comparisons. You can extract actionable intelligence from the library without scheduling a single call.

Tegus is favored by private equity and hedge funds for due diligence, especially in TMT, healthcare research, and high-growth sectors. After AlphaSense acquired Tegus in July 2024 for roughly $930 million, the platform now integrates with AlphaSense's broader data offering. AlphaSense sources information from over 10,000 content sources, and Generative Search helps users extract insights from millions of sources. Smart Summaries provide instant earnings insights during earnings season.

Tegus also offers access to over 50,000 expert call transcripts through additional content tiers, and the transcript library is the core product for most subscribers.

### GLG: Live Expert Consultations and Full-Service Support

GLG connects clients with over 1 million experts globally across 150+ countries. Expert consultations typically occur via phone and video calls, with dedicated research managers handling expert matching, scheduling, and compliance.

The network includes C-suite executives, academics, former government officials, and operational specialists. GLG supports a broader spectrum of industries including consulting and professional services, making it useful for management consulting firms, corporate research teams, and institutional investors.

Engagement formats go beyond calls. GLG offers surveys, panels, roundtables, expert witness work, and moderated events. GLG provides integrated scheduling and consultation recording services, reducing logistics overhead for large teams.

GLG is known for stringent enterprise compliance standards. Its compliance infrastructure is mature, particularly for regulated sectors like life sciences, financial services, and government policy research.

## Pricing Models and Cost Structure

Pricing is where these two platforms diverge most sharply. Here's a direct comparison:

| Pricing Component     | Tegus                                                                                    | GLG                                                                               |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Model**             | Subscription for transcript library + per-call fees for live calls                       | Credit-based or per-hour fees + annual platform/membership costs                  |
| **Expert call rates** | $350-$1,000 per hour for expert consultations                                            | $400-$1,200+ per hour based on expertise                                          |
| **Transcript access** | Included in subscription (tiered by seats/modules)                                       | Not a core offering; pay per call or project                                      |
| **Annual cost range** | \~$25,000-$125,000+ depending on team size and modules                                   | Higher; enterprise retainers plus per-call costs                                  |
| **Transparency**      | High. Expert fee + transcription cost visible before call. No markup on expert honoraria | Lower. Credit-based pricing with multipliers, minimum commitments, opaque markups |
| **Volume discounts**  | Subscription absorbs high-volume usage                                                   | Volume commitments can reduce expert consultation costs by 15-40%                 |
Expert network pricing matters because it determines how many calls you can actually afford. The expert network market surpassed USD 2.50 billion in 2024 and is growing at a 9% annual rate, which means more options and more pressure on transparent pricing.

### Tegus Pricing Structure

Tegus uses a subscription model for transcript library access. Annual contracts are custom-negotiated but typically fall in these ranges:

* Small teams (1-3 users): $25,000-$50,000 per year

* Mid-sized teams (5-10 users): $75,000-$125,000 per year

* Enterprise: custom pricing

For live expert calls, Tegus charges $350-$1,000 per hour for expert consultations. The average expert fee runs around $450 per hour. You see the expert cost plus transcription cost upfront, with no hidden markups.

This structure makes Tegus cost-effective for investment research teams and private equity firms that consume high volumes of transcripts. The per-insight cost drops as usage increases.

### GLG Pricing Structure

GLG uses a credit-based system with annual retainers or membership fees. Expert call rates run $400-$1,200+ per hour based on expertise, seniority, and domain complexity.

For context, AlphaSights rates range from $700-$1,800+ per hour, and Third Bridge charges $350-$1,000+ per hour for consultations. GLG sits in the upper-middle of traditional expert networks on pricing.

Pricing transparency is a known issue. Credit systems, multipliers, and minimum commitments make it difficult to predict total spend. Enterprise clients with large budgets absorb this. Smaller teams get burned.

## Industry Coverage and Expert Quality

Expert networks provide access to over 1 million experts globally across every major sector. But coverage depth varies significantly between Tegus and GLG.

### Tegus Industry Focus

Tegus is strongest in technology, media, telecom, healthcare, and consumer sectors. The transcript library skews toward public company executives, investor perspectives, and high-growth private companies.

Channel Checks (AI-led interviews with customers, suppliers, and vendors) add coverage in energy, industrials, and supply chain research. These are useful for competitive intelligence and tracking emerging trends.

Gaps exist in traditional heavy industries like oil and gas, manufacturing, and very local or emerging markets. If your due diligence targets are outside TMT or healthcare, the transcript library may have thinner coverage. Geographic concentration leans North American.

### GLG Industry Breadth

GLG covers virtually every industry across 150+ countries. The network includes industry experts from regulated sectors like medical devices, pharma, government policy, and legal. Expert consultations help validate assumptions and uncover risks in these domains.

For global research, GLG's geographic reach is a competitive advantage. Local market expertise, multi-language capability, and on-the-ground specialists make it the better choice for cross-border market research and market intelligence projects.

Guidepoint operates in over 200 industries, providing another reference point. But GLG's strength in compliance-sensitive and regulated research is hard to match among expert network companies.

## Technology Platform and User Experience

Tegus (via AlphaSense) offers a self-service platform with ai powered search, natural language processing, sentiment analysis, and workflow integration. You search across the transcript library, filter by expert type, generate summaries, and compare transcripts side by side. AI-powered search tools enable instant access to relevant experts and content. The platform doubles as a financial data and broker research tool post-acquisition.

GLG's client platform (myGLG) focuses on project management, scheduling, and engagement coordination. It supports rapid expert matching, streaming expert content, and integration of consultation recordings into your workflow. The platform is optimized for real-time project logistics rather than content browsing.

For research teams that prioritize self-directed exploration and competitive analysis, Tegus has stronger content discovery features. For teams that need full-service coordination, especially across multiple networks or engagement formats, GLG's platform handles the logistics better.

Both platforms support virtual calls and mobile access. Tegus is primarily browser-based for researchers. GLG has a mobile app for experts and project management tools for clients.

## Use Case Considerations

Expert networks are essential for investment research and corporate strategy. But the right platform depends on how you work.

**Choose Tegus when:**

* You're doing high-volume transcript research across TMT, healthcare, or consumer sectors

* You want predictable annual spend with subscription pricing

* Your team includes analysts who can self-serve, searching prior expert interviews for valuable insights

* You need archival data to track market trends over time

* Your consulting projects or due diligence work benefits from pre-built content

**Choose GLG when:**

* You need custom sourcing for a niche subject matter expert on short notice

* Your research targets regulated industries: life sciences, pharma, government, legal

* You require expert witness support, risk management consultations, or board-level expertise

* Your firm operates globally and needs local market expertise across multiple geographies

* Compliance and accountability standards are non-negotiable for your organization

Expert consultations can validate assumptions during market entry. Expert networks facilitate due diligence for market entry decisions. Expert networks help identify market opportunities before competitors do. The question is whether you need that validation from archived transcripts or from a live conversation.

**Team size matters.** Small to mid-market research teams often find Tegus subscription pricing feasible. GLG works better for enterprise clients and large asset managers who have the budget for frequent calls and need access to senior experts with deep research experience. AlphaSights focuses on senior executives with 15+ years experience, offering another option for high-seniority needs.

## Tegus vs GLG: Which Should You Choose?

Tegus wins on cost predictability, transcript volume, and self-service research speed. If you're an investment firm focused on TMT or healthcare, running frequent competitive analysis, and want to minimize per-insight cost, Tegus is the better fit.

GLG wins on expert breadth, global coverage, custom research depth, and compliance infrastructure. If you need bespoke expert consultations, operate across regulated industries, or require professional services support for complex engagements, GLG delivers more.

AI technology is transforming expert network operations and insights delivery. Both platforms are investing in AI. But the business model difference remains: Tegus monetizes content at scale, GLG monetizes expert access per engagement. Expert networks provide rapid access to specialized market insights, and both platforms do this differently.

Decision factors:

1. **Sector focus.** Does the transcript library cover your sectors, or do you need experts from industries Tegus doesn't cover well?

2. **Research volume.** Are you doing dozens of calls per quarter (Tegus subscription pays off) or a handful of high-stakes consultations (GLG's premium is justified)?

3. **Budget.** Can you afford $400-$1,200+ per hour plus platform fees, or do you need predictable annual spend?

4. **Team structure.** Do you have analysts who can self-serve, or do you need full-service project management?

Third Bridge offers over 40,000 interview transcripts, providing a middle-ground option that bridge combines expert calls with a growing content library. Multiple expert networks may be worth evaluating depending on your specific needs.
