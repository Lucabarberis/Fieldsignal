## The Pet Care Market in 2026: What You Actually Need to Know

Before you get into methods, you need the baseline numbers. Here's what matters:

* Pet food products will dominate the market with a 52.60% share in 2025. Food is the largest bucket, but it behaves differently from discretionary categories like toys or pet walking accessories.

* Advanced veterinary care is the fastest-growing sector in the pet care market. U.S. veterinary services hit roughly [$41.26 billion in 2024](https://petbusinessprofessor.com/petmarket/petmarketsegments/2024-u-s-pet-food-spending-40-04bdown-%E2%86%935-46b/), up \~15.7% year-over-year.

* Pet services are projected to grow at a 10% compound annual growth rate.

* E-commerce platforms and online subscription services are leading the distribution market for pet products. Yet the offline distribution channel will still dominate with a 73.00% market share in 2025.

* The pet care market is transitioning toward premium nutrition and holistic preventive care.

* Households owning pets in the U.S. increased from 82 million in 2023 to 94 million by 2025.

* Pet food prices surged 18.1% over the past 5 years, pressuring volumes even as pet ownership grew.

* Cat ownership is witnessing the fastest growth globally due to urbanization and lower maintenance costs.

* The global pet care market is projected to reach nearly [$500 billion by 2034](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/pet-care-market).

The structural market trends you need to track: pet humanization, premiumization of pet nutrition, growth of pet insurance (still under 4% penetration in the U.S.), and the accelerating shift to e commerce and subscription purchasing for pet food and supplies.

Mature markets like the U.S. and Western Europe show slowing top-line growth but increasing spend per pet. Faster-growing asia pacific markets like China, India, and southeast asia are driven by rising disposable income, urbanization, and first-time pet ownership. Regional differences in format (wet food vs dry), price sensitivity, and preferred channels make "copy-paste" strategies unreliable. That's why targeted pet market research matters.


## What Makes Pet Care Market Research Different from Other CPG Verticals

Pet care behaves like a mix of FMCG, healthcare, and emotionally driven luxury. Standard CPG playbooks are incomplete here.

Decision-making involves two layers: the pet's health and preferences (species, age, pet type, dietary needs) and the owner's budget, ethics, and convenience. You must probe both functional outcomes and emotional drivers in interviews and surveys. Pet market research helps understand consumer purchasing decisions at both levels.

84% of US dog owners view their dogs as children. Pet ownership is increasingly viewed as pet parenting. Many owners treat dogs and cats as family members, which increases willingness to trade up on food quality, veterinary care, and supplements even when overall household budgets are tight. In 2025, pet parents spent an average of USD 1,135 on health-related expenses. At the same time, 48% of pet owners set a budget for pet spending in 2025, showing the tension between emotional attachment and financial discipline.

Veterinary professionals and groomers are "invisible" influencers who shape brand adoption for food, flea/tick products, and pet wellness products. They should be included in expert interviews, not just consumers.

Viral social media trends (TikTok-driven pet treats or harnesses) can cause short-term spikes. Your research process should distinguish durable behavior from one-off fads using time-series questions and expert triangulation. Social listening helps but doesn't replace structured primary research.

Pet care regulation, claims standards, and import rules differ sharply by market. Expert consultations on compliance and labeling are often more valuable than generic consumer surveys, especially for manufacturers entering new regions.

## Core Pet Care Market Research Questions Before You Spend a Dollar

Before committing capital to any pet sector bet, answer these questions. Use them to build your research brief:

1. What is the current and forecast pet care market size in your target country and category (e.g. dry dog food in the U.S., cat litter in Japan) between 2024 and 2030?

2. How are penetration rates and spend per pet changing, by species and income band? How does this differ for dog owners vs cat owners?

3. What functional jobs are pet owners trying to solve (gut health, odor control, anxiety, pet wellness), and what products and services are they currently using?

4. What pushes them to trade up to premium products, trade down, or switch brands?

5. Which 5 to 10 brands command shelf space and mindshare in your segment, and what's their real market positioning and differentiation?

6. What do former employees say about competitor product roadmap priorities, margins, and channel strategy? What's their product portfolio focus?

7. How are online vs offline shares shifting in your specific category? 65% of US pet owners shop online for pet food to save time. Does that hold in your target segment?

8. Which e commerce partners (Chewy, Amazon, JD, Shopee, Tmall, DTC) matter most in 2026, and on what terms?

9. Where is profit actually captured in this value chain, from ingredient suppliers to brands to vets and pet retailers?

10. What structural constraints exist, like vet distributor lock-ins, exclusive contracts, or regulatory hurdles?

Surveys and focus groups are essential for pet market research, but only if they're designed to answer questions like these. Turn this list into a 1 to 2 page research brief that defines scope, markets, and decision deadlines before you commission any external work.

## How to Conduct Market Research in Pet Care: A 3-Stage Practical Workflow

Most effective pet care market research projects follow three stages: 1) frame the decision and scope, 2) collect targeted data, 3) turn findings into an investment or product decision. You can compress this into 2 to 3 weeks for a pre-investment view or expand to 8 to 10 weeks for a full market entry plan. The structure stays the same.

### Stage 1: Frame the Bet and Research Scope

Stage 1 is a 1 to 3 day exercise. Define the decision, stake size, and timelines, then reverse-engineer what you need to learn.

Phrase the decision crisply. Examples: "Should we invest USD 10 to 15 million into a growth-stage U.S. premium dry dog food brand in 2026?" or "Should we launch our European cat litter DTC into asia pacific in 2027?"

Write down 3 to 5 hypotheses your research will try to falsify. For example: "Offline vet clinics still control new product recommendation for pet food in U.S. suburbs" or "Asia pacific cat owners are more price-sensitive but have faster e commerce adoption."

Map the pet care value chain relevant to your bet: ingredient suppliers, co-packers, brand owners, vets, independent pet retailers, supermarkets, online platforms. Know which roles to target for interviews.

Define what's out of scope. Excluding small mammals, reptiles, or grooming services keeps your research time and budget focused.

### Stage 2: Collect Pet Care Data That Actually Changes Your Decision

Mix secondary industry analysis with tailored [primary research](/blog/primary-research-methods-examples-and-b2b-use-cases). Generic reports that competitors also own won't give you an edge.

Secondary sources to use:

* The american pet products association (APPA) National Pet Owners Survey for U.S. pet ownership and spend data

* Fortune business insights and similar firms for global market value estimates

* Company filings for major players. Chewy generated $11.2 billion in revenue in 2023. Petco's revenue reached $6.3 billion in 2023. PetSmart operates 1,522 stores across the US as of June 2024. These filings reveal channel economics.

* Any industry report that slices pet care by region, product sales, and channel

Primary research you should prioritize:

1. Expert interviews with former executives at pet food brands, big box pet retailers, vet groups, or e commerce platforms. These reveal real channel margins, how trade spend works, and why certain claims or packaging formats win shelf space.

2. Short, targeted surveys of pet owners focusing on the specific species and income bands you care about. Capture purchase triggers, price sensitivity, and brand switching, not vanity satisfaction scores. Limit questionnaires to 10 to 15 minutes.

3. Vet or groomer interviews for insight into recommendation behavior and how they influence product sales for pet health and pet wellness categories.

63% of US households owned pets in 2024. There was a 43.5% increase in pet-owning households among Gen Z from 2023 to 2024. These are the kinds of demand signals you validate through primary research, not just accept from a report.

### Stage 3: Turn Findings into Clear, Actionable Insights

Stage 3 is where you synthesize into a decision memo. Not a 200-slide deck. Tie pet care data back to your original investment or product decision.

Quantify the opportunity by segment using market size and market growth. For example: premium dry dog food in the U.S. growing at mid-single-digit CAGR vs double-digit growth for pet insurance in select asia pacific markets.

Structure the memo into four sections:

1. What's true about this market that matters for us

2. What's uncertain or risky

3. What we'll do if we invest or launch

4. What would change our mind within 12 months

Translate qualitative themes into actions: shifting spend toward vet-recommended formats, building bundles for e commerce, or avoiding over-crowded sub-segments where expert interviews confirmed margin compression.

## By Segment: How Pet Care Market Research Differs Across Categories

"Pet care" is not a single market. Your approach to market intelligence must flex by category.

* Pet food and pet treats: Research should focus on pet nutrition beliefs, brand trust, format preferences (dry, wet food, raw, fresh, premium food), and recurring purchase patterns in subscription e commerce contexts. The pet food sector is the largest by revenue but faces increased spending pressure from inflation and trade-down behavior among price-sensitive owners.

* Veterinary services and pet health products: Focus on regulation, vet practice economics, insurer relationships, and owner willingness to pay for preventative vs acute care. Pet owners spent an average of $1,135 on health-related expenses in 2025. Pet insurance adoption is growing but still small. These are growing interest areas for investors.

* Pet services (grooming, boarding, training, pet walking): Very local. Labor availability, real estate, and online discovery via Google reviews and social media matter more than global brand power. Local expert interviews are key.

* Accessories and discretionary spend (toys, apparel, smart devices): Test elasticity and fad risk. Products that rely on influencer or social media virality need careful well being and durability assessment before you commit.

Choose 1 to 2 priority segments and design research for those economics. A one-size-fits-all questionnaire across the entire pet market won't cut it.

## Regional Pet Care Market Focus: U.S. vs Asia Pacific

Most readers will either be adding to U.S. exposure or evaluating asia pacific expansion. You need different research questions for each.

**U.S.**: 63% of US households owned pets in 2024, making it a mature market. 84% of US dog owners view their dogs as children. Demand is shifting toward premium food, wellness trends, and pet health products. Omnichannel strategies that blend Chewy, Amazon, big box stores like PetSmart, and vet clinics matter. U.S. research should prioritize understanding pet insurance market growth, inflation-driven trade-down behavior, and the balance between brick-and-mortar and e commerce subscriptions. 48% of pet owners now set a budget for pet spending, reflecting the tension between emotional attachment and cost consciousness. Large breeds and specific pet type segments have different economics worth isolating.

**Asia Pacific**: The [asia pacific pet care market is projected to grow at a CAGR of 7.24%](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/asia-pacific-pet-care-market) from 2026 to 2034. China's urban pet market reached roughly $43 to 45 billion in 2025, with [online channels capturing nearly 60% of sales](https://en.cipscom.com/IndustryNews/25434.htm). In southeast asia, 61% of Vietnamese cat owners prefer mix-feeding their pets. Pet ownership norms, species mix, preferred food formats, and regulatory regimes differ sharply across the region. Country-level granularity is not optional.

For asia pacific, run a 2-track research plan: 1) expert calls with local pet care executives, vets, and platform category managers, and 2) targeted surveys of urban pet owners in Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities to understand price tiers and channel choice.


## Primary Research Tactics That Work in Pet Care

High-quality primary research is what separates a differentiated thesis from everyone reading the same syndicated industry report.

Structure expert interviews as 45 to 60 minute calls with former senior staff from pet food brands, specialty retailers, vet groups, or platforms like Chewy. Question guides should focus on economics, operations, and demand signals, not general opinions.

Run focused pet owner surveys screened by pet type (dogs vs cats), income band, and channel usage (offline, e commerce, subscription). Capture frequency, basket composition, and brand switching. In 2025, pet parents spent an average of USD 1,135 on health-related expenses. Probe where that money actually went.

Panel calls with 2 to 3 experts for 60 to 90 minutes give you fast triangulation on issues like private-label competition, retailer power dynamics, or regulatory shifts in pet food labeling and animal welfare standards.
