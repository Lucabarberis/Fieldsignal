## What Is Financial Due Diligence? (And How It Differs From an Audit)

Financial due diligence is a transaction-specific investigation of a target’s financial data and business model to validate the purchase price, confirm value, assess risks, and shape deal terms. Financial due diligence assesses a company’s financial health and risks, identifies hidden liabilities and validates financial statements.

It examines:

* Revenue quality, including recurring revenue streams, one-time revenue, customer concentration, and revenue trends.

* Cash flow, cash flow reports, cash flow statements, cash flow patterns, capex, and liquidity.

* Balance sheet items, debt obligations, deferred revenue, goodwill, and working capital.

* Off-balance-sheet items, including leases, guarantees, customer prepayments, and related-party balances.

* Tax compliance, tax filings, and exposure to future penalties or audits.

* Historical financial statements, audited financial statements, income statements, and financial records.

| Area    | Financial Due Diligence                                                  | Audit                                                               |
|---------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Purpose | Validate price, risk, financial stability, and future cash generation    | Verify compliance with regulatory standards and accuracy of records |
| Scope   | Forecasts, QoE, unit economics, hidden liabilities, potential risks      | Historical accuracy and accounting compliance                       |
| Timing  | During business transactions, investments, and M&A                       | Regular assessments, often annual                                   |
| Output  | Diligence report, adjusted EBITDA, risk assessment, deal recommendations | Audit opinion and certified financial statements                    |
| Users   | Acquiring company, PE, VC, corp dev, lenders                             | Boards, regulators, lenders, owners                                 |
Audits look backward. Due diligence is transaction-specific while audits focus on regular assessments. Financial audits are often legally required under certain criteria.

Diligence focuses on sustainability of reported profits, cash conversion, financial forecasts, and future cash flow under realistic market conditions. Buyers use findings to re-cut valuation, adjust earn-outs, change covenants in the sales and purchase agreement, or walk away.
