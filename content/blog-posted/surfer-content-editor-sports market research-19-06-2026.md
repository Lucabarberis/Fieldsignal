## Key use cases for sports market research

Most projects fall into repeatable patterns. Understanding fan behavior helps tailor marketing campaigns, and audience segmentation analyzes demographic and behavioral data to identify fans. Market research identifies untapped market segments, reduces risks associated with sponsorship and product development, and helps validate product development. Sponsorship valuation uses metrics to establish the monetary worth of partnerships, and voluntary sponsorship and advertising valuation are crucial for partnership efficiency. Competitive analysis evaluates competitors' market share and strengths.

* **New market entry**: Launching a team in Las Vegas by 2027. Sub-questions: fan base size, local media habits, price sensitivity, competitor presence.

* **Media rights valuation**: League renewing a deal for 2028-2033. Sub-questions: linear vs streaming splits, younger viewer behaviors, comparable rights fees. [U.S. media rights hit roughly $30.5B in 2025](https://thedesk.net/2025/08/ampere-analysis-sports-media-rights-us/).

* **Sponsorship evaluation**: Selecting a jersey sponsor in MLS or a presenting sponsor for a college bowl game. Sub-questions: category saturation, activation cost, [brand fit](/blog/brand-market-research-methods-and-vendor-selection).

* **Sporting goods expansion**: Pickleball or padel gear in 2025-2028. Sub-questions: DTC vs wholesale margins, consumer demand, competitive maps.

* **Fanbase sizing**: Segmenting sports fans by spending, not just social media follows. Sub-questions: paid vs free engagement, churn rates, propensity to pay.

* **Due diligence**: Acquiring sports teams, media assets, or rights-holders. Sub-questions: revenue breakdown, growth vs peers, regulatory exposure.

Examples span U.S. (NFL, NCAA, MLS) and non-U.S. markets (Premier League, LaLiga, Indian Premier League).

## Primary research methods for the sports industry

Once secondary data is exhausted, you move to primary market research. Common research methodologies include surveys and questionnaires. Focus groups facilitate discussions to understand consumer attitudes and behaviors. Social listening captures fan sentiment on social media platforms, useful for digital marketing and advertising review.

* **Expert interviews**: Talk to former league rights executives about 2026-2030 media rights renewals. Best for depth on competitive tactics.

* **Fan/consumer surveys**: Test price sensitivity for 2027 season tickets or measure willingness to pay for streaming. Collect information from communities of fans directly.

* **Panel calls**: Mini-roundtables with former ticketing leads from NBA or NCAA to discuss tier pricing and track trends.

* **Mystery shopping**: Audit sponsorship sales processes or stadium membership funnels.

* **Internal data benchmarking**: Compare your team's CRM and attendance data against peer analysis.

Constraints: data privacy with students and college athletes, compliance when contacting current employees, NDA expectations around non-public terms.

Standard research sequence:

1. Start with 5-8 expert interviews to surface hypotheses

2. Refine hypotheses from those interviews

3. Run a survey if you need quantitative validation

4. Build a simple financial model comparing base, optimistic, and pessimistic scenarios

## Using expert networks for sports market research

Expert networks are the fastest way to talk to former insiders from leagues, sports teams, betting operators, and brands that buy sponsorships. An expert network is a vendor that recruits and vets former employees, customers, and partners, then sets up paid 45-60 minute calls under compliance rules.

Sports-specific expert types you might want:

* Ex-media rights negotiators at networks or leagues

* Former team ticketing and CRM heads

* Sponsorship directors from brands that ran Olympic or World Cup campaigns around major sporting events

* Retired athletic directors from D-I schools with access to winning strategies and resources

* Former executives at sporting goods manufacturers in running, soccer, or golf

Major networks like GLG, AlphaSights, Third Bridge, Guidepoint, Tegus, and others dominate the top end but often require opaque retainers. That prices out smaller funds, developing companies, and operators with sub-$50K project budgets.

## Designing high-impact sports market research: practical steps

Good research design is about a few decisions you make before spending the first dollar. This effort drives success or failure.

1. Define one primary decision (e.g., "Do we back a minority stake in an MLS expansion club by Q4 2026?")

2. Lock 3-5 must-answer questions: media rights renewal risk, sponsorship category saturation, fanbase size in a specific DMA

3. Map what's already known from secondary data, industry reports, and links to public filings

4. Pick 2-3 primary methods (expert calls, survey, panel)

5. Set budget and deadline

6. Choose vendors for each piece

## Common mistakes in sports industry research (and how to avoid them)

Most wasted budget comes from repeated errors. Keep these tags on your review checklist:

* Assuming pro sports economics apply to college sports. Fix: treat them as separate industries with different management structures.

* Confusing social media followers with paying fans. Fix: segment by spending and behaviors, not clicks.

* Accepting league-issued projections without independent checks. Fix: pair league data with former rights-holder interviews.

* Relying only on public sponsorship case studies for future planning. Fix: run 3-5 expert calls with people who negotiated those deals.

* Skipping compliance review for current-employee calls. Fix: route all expert work through a compliant vendor.

* Over-surveying season ticket holders while ignoring casual fans. Fix: sample across engagement tiers to understand the full window of consumer behavior.

* Ignoring media and distribution shifts between 2020 and 2026, including the impact of the pandemic on attendance and viewing habits. Fix: use [current viewership data](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/state-of-us-sports-viewing-2025) and streaming trends as your baseline.

## When to invest in sports market analytics vs one-off research

Sports market analytics means ongoing tracking: attendance, ARPU per seat, sponsorship inventory fill rate, rights fee escalators, and digital engagement across professional leagues. Data analytics analyzes historical sales and attendance trends to inform these dashboards. One-off strategic research is tied to a specific decision date.

Analytics tools make sense when a club is optimizing 2027-2029 ticket pricing, a venue operator is tracking F&B conversion by game, or a streaming service needs to measure churn from sports content loss.

Expert interviews and targeted research are better when you're deciding whether to pursue a minority stake in a European club, evaluating a [sports betting B2B software investment](/blog/technology-market-research-methods-for-software-buyers), or entering a new sporting goods category like padel in the U.S.

Use analytics for ongoing performance. Use FieldSignal-style projects for the moments when you need to commit capital or change strategy.
