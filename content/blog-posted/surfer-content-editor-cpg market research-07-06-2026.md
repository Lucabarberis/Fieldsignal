## Frequently Asked Questions About CPG Expert Research

### How quickly can I get CPG experts for time-sensitive investment decisions?

If the scope is clear, expert sourcing can begin the same day. Many expert networks can produce matches in 24 to 48 hours, and boutique searches can move faster when the expert profile already exists.

Speed improves when you define the category, target channel, decision question, and expert profile upfront.

### What compliance protections exist for sensitive competitive intelligence gathering?

A proper expert research process includes conflict checks, MNPI screening, NDA review, call guidelines, recording consent where applicable, and secure transcript handling.

FieldSignal screens experts so they speak from professional experience, not confidential information. That matters when you're comparing pricing, market share, retailer behavior, consumer packaged goods brands, and category performance.

### How do I ensure expert quality without paying tier-1 network premiums?

Narrow the profile.

Don't ask for a generic CPG executive. Ask for a former category manager in frozen food, an ex-club channel sales lead, a former mass retail buyer, or a brand manager who owned pricing and promotion for your category.

You can also run multiple shorter calls across different roles to compare signals. This improves data quality and reduces the risk of over-weighting one opinion.

### Can boutique networks handle global CPG research across multiple markets?

Yes, for selected markets and categories. Global work depends on geography, language, seniority, and category rarity.

For broad multi-country studies, large networks or firms such as Kantar, NielsenIQ, Hanover Research, Pollfish, and Touchstone Research may be useful alongside expert interviews. Touchstone Research operates in over 60 international markets, while Pollfish offers rapid, mobile-first surveys to a global audience.

### What's the difference between expert interviews and traditional CPG market research surveys?

Expert interviews explain why something is happening. Surveys measure how often it's happening.

Expert interviews are best for qualitative research, competitive dynamics, retailer behavior, pricing logic, supply chain issues, and brand strategy. Surveys, online surveys, quantitative surveys, and conjoint analysis are best for purchase intent, consumer segmentation, concept testing, and statistically valid measurement.

The strongest CPG research often combines qualitative and quantitative methods. Use expert interviews to frame the business questions, then use quantitative research to size the opportunity.
