## How Third Bridge consultant pay is structured

Third Bridge consultant pay is a simple hourly rate, pro-rated by the minute. There are no benefits, no guaranteed hours, and no non-compete with other expert networks. Third Bridge pays consultants based on time spent in consultations.

Here's the typical process:

1. You receive and accept a project or screening invite.

2. You confirm your hourly rate.

3. The client selects you.

4. The call happens, typically lasting one hour or more, sometimes 30 or 90 minutes.

5. You submit your payment application. Consultants must apply for payment within two business days.

6. Third Bridge pays you via bank transfer within a defined window, [officially up to 30 days](https://careers.thirdbridge.com/en-us/about-us/compliance/policies/expert-terms-and-conditions), though many experts report faster turnaround.

You're paid only for actual call time, not prep work, emails, or materials. Factor your preparation into your rate.

What counts as a paid consultation: 1:1 calls, some follow-up calls, and sometimes participation in Forum or panel interviews, each with separate compensation rules. Third Bridge doesn't typically pay retainers. The work is ad-hoc and tied to specific project demand in your niche.

## Factors that influence your Third Bridge consulting rate

The biggest driver of pay is how directly your experience matches the client's subject matter. Exact company knowledge, an exact buyer role, and very recent tenure command a higher rate every time. Rates can be adjusted based on demand for your specific expertise.

Key variables:

* **Seniority and decision-making authority.** Did you control budgets, sign contracts, or set strategy? That's what investors want to discuss.

* **Niche depth.** Reimbursement policy in one country is worth more than "generic healthcare" knowledge. A specific industry focus commands top dollar.

* **Scarcity.** If few other experts in Third Bridge's database or in other expert networks cover your area, your supply advantage pushes your rate up.

* **Urgency.** Short-notice calls for private equity or corporate diligence questions often pay a premium. High demand plus low supply equals fair compensation for your time.

Recent, post-2022 experience on a target company or product is usually priced higher than experience from a decade ago. If you're repeatedly selected quickly for Third Bridge calls, that's a sign you can test higher rates. If you're rarely picked, consider lowering your rate or tightening your profile focus.

A concrete example: a former 2023 to 2025 procurement director at a Fortune 500 industrial firm will often clear more per hour than a 2012 to 2016 manager in the same category, because the market cares about current supplier and pricing dynamics.

## Setting your Third Bridge consulting rate: practical guidance

Third Bridge usually asks you to propose a rate. It's in their interest to keep your rate acceptable to clients while maintaining their own margin. Here's a simple method to set your initial rate:

1. Benchmark against your base salary. Aim for 2x to 4x your hourly equivalent.

2. Check what peers report for other expert networks.

3. Start slightly above your comfort level and adjust after your first 3 to 5 calls.

Starting points:

* Mid-level manager at a public company: test $200 to $250 per hour.

* Senior director or VP at a recognizable brand: test $300 to $500 per hour. Experienced advisors can command rates of $300 to $500 per hour consistently.

* Former C-suite at a scaled company: test $600 to $900 per hour.

If Third Bridge suggests lowering your requested rate, ask whether the client has flexibility or offer a slightly lower rate in exchange for a guaranteed minimum duration. You're free to set different rates across different expert networks and to revisit your Third Bridge rate annually as your seniority, position, and market demand increase.

## Compliance, confidential information, and pay-related restrictions

Your Third Bridge compensation doesn't change the strict rule: you can't share confidential information, material nonpublic information, or trade secrets. The rate doesn't matter. Specialists must maintain strict compliance during calls. Third Bridge emphasizes regulatory compliance to avoid insider trading.

Specific no-go categories:

* Unreleased financials or material changes not yet public.

* Unannounced M&A, fundraising, or investment decisions.

* Upcoming product launches or internal pricing and bids.

* Any data covered by employment agreements or NDAs.

Third Bridge's compliance framework protects against confidential information breaches. Their compliance team typically screens consultants and projects, provides written guidelines, and may join or record calls. But ultimately, consultants are responsible for ensuring compliance during calls. Consultants must not disclose confidential or protected information, and they must report any conflicts of interest immediately.

Some employers restrict or ban outside paid consulting entirely. Verify your employment contracts and internal policies before agreeing to any Third Bridge or other expert network calls. Check with your employer's legal or compliance requirements in advance.
