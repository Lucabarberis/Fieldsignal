# How to Become Expert Network Consultant: A Career Guide

You don't need a new degree, a certification, or a career change. If you've spent years working inside a specific company, product line, or market, you already have what expert networks pay for. This guide walks you through exactly how to turn that experience into paid consulting calls.

## What an Expert Network Consultant Actually Does

"Expert network consultant" isn't a full-time job title. It means you get paid for 30 to 60 minute phone or video calls with investors, management consultants, and corporate strategy teams through expert networks. Those networks act as matchmakers, connecting professionals helping decision-makers with subject matter experts who have firsthand experience in a specific domain.

The major players in the expert network industry include GLG (Gerson Lehrman Group), AlphaSights, Third Bridge, Guidepoint, Tegus, and boutique options like FieldSignal. Expert networks connect clients with industry professionals for insights they can't get from broker research, 10-Ks, or news articles, one of the key benefits of using these networks. They provide timely insights in changing environments where public data lags behind what's actually happening on the ground.

Typical use cases where clients need your knowledge:

* Market entry checks (channels, incumbents, pricing dynamics in a target geography)

* Competitive intelligence (vendor comparisons, win/loss patterns, technology shifts)

* Customer sentiment studies (why buyers switch, stay, or churn)

* Leadership assessment (evaluating management teams pre-acquisition)

* Product roadmap feedback (former users or product leads offering candid takes)

Expert networks provide one-on-one expert consultations, but that's not the only format. You might join survey panels, small group panels, moderated workshops, or longer advisory engagements. Expert calls are a standard tool for client consultations, and expert consultations can be scheduled within 24 to 48 hours in many cases. Expert networks also offer qualitative and quantitative research options, and clients can access expert insights through surveys and focus groups.

Expert network consulting is usually a side income stream. Most experts do this alongside a full-time role, fractional work, or as part of a post-exit career. It's not meant to replace your salary. It's meant to monetize what you already know.

## Are You Qualified To Be an Expert Network Consultant?

If you can talk concretely for 30 minutes about a company, product, or market you've worked in, you're viable. That's the bar. You don't need to be a CEO or a key opinion leader with a speaking circuit. Participating in expert network platforms can provide valuable consultation opportunities for a wide range of business professionals.

Typical expert profiles include:

* Former managers, product leaders, sales directors, and operations heads

* Engineers, clinicians, procurement leads, and regulatory specialists

* Former executives from target companies in active deal pipelines

* Mid-level operators with direct access to customers, vendors, or internal systems

In expert network terms, "expert" means 2 to 5+ years in a narrow domain with direct decision-making responsibility or first-hand exposure to specific vendors, technologies, or regulations. Expert network consulting involves strategic consulting skills and technical knowledge, but it doesn't require academic credentials alone.

Sector examples of high-value experts:

* Life sciences: oncology sales across EU5, 2019 to 2024

* SaaS: US mid-market sales for product-led growth tools

* Manufacturing: Tier-1 automotive supplier in Mexico since 2018

* Medical devices: hospital procurement for surgical robotics

What usually disqualifies you: no direct experience in the topic (only academic exposure), or a current role that creates clear conflicts. If you're on a public company's deal team and someone asks about that company's unannounced pipeline, that's a hard stop. Gaining practical experience is essential for handling complex scenarios in expert network consulting.

* **Think of your profile as a search engine.** Every keyword you add increases the chance that a network's sourcing teams flag you for a relevant brief.

## Step 4: Prepare for Your First Expert Network Call

Expert network consulting lives or dies by your first 2 to 3 calls. A strong start leads to repeat invites. A mediocre one means you won't hear back.

Here's how a typical 60-minute call with a PE associate or strategy manager runs:

1. 5 minutes: your background summary

2. 45 minutes: structured Q&A

3. 10 minutes: follow-up questions and clarifications

**Before the call:**

* Request an agenda or question list in advance when the network allows it. Most networks will share at least a project description.

* Spend 30 to 60 minutes reviewing public information: the target company's website, recent press releases, industry overviews, and any relevant SEC filings. This ensures you don't waste call time restating what's already visible.

**What to prepare:**

* Timelines of changes you witnessed (2019 to 2024 market shifts, product launches, leadership transitions)

* Approximate figures that are public or generalized (market sizing ranges, competitive rankings)

* Procurement steps, customer personas, and channel structures you directly managed or observed

**Compliance is non-negotiable.** You must never share material non-public information, confidential pipeline data, non-public pricing, or specific unannounced contracts. Networks like FieldSignal, GLG, and others train you on this before your first call. Expert networks implement rigorous compliance standards, and you're expected to follow them from day one.

## Step 5: Deliver Exceptional Value on Calls (Without Breaking Compliance)

The "expert" part is clarity and structure, not having secret data. Your job is to offer expert insights in a way clients can actually use for their business decisions.

* **Structure answers chronologically.** "In 2018 to 2020, we saw X. By 2021, the market shifted to Y. In 2023, the major change was Z." This translates well into transcripts and ai tools that clients use later.

* **Give concrete ranges, process steps, and frameworks.** "The typical sales cycle ran 4 to 6 months" is useful. "It varied" is not. Use approximations where exact numbers would breach confidentiality.

* **Handle "sharper" questions from funds carefully.** When something touches potential risk areas or MNPI, redirect: "I can't speak to that specific contract, but I can tell you how procurement decisions generally worked in that segment." Consultants must communicate technical issues clearly to non-technical stakeholders, and knowing how to decline gracefully actually builds trust with both the client and the network.

* **Why this matters more in 2026:** Expert network companies increasingly feed call transcripts into ai tools for summarization, search, and reuse. Well-organized, clearly spoken commentary becomes more valuable because it surfaces accurately in future searches. Poorly structured answers get buried. Expert networks provide timely insights during market changes, but only if the source material is clear.

## Step 6: Build a Repeatable Expert Network Income Stream

Set realistic expectations. Many experts start with 1 to 3 live calls per quarter, then scale to several per month as they build a proven track record across multiple networks.

* **Pay structure:** Per-hour honoraria typically range from the low hundreds to the low thousands USD. Seniority, niche scarcity, and geography drive rates. Rare specialists and former executives in active deal sectors earn more. The research process for high volume sectors like life sciences generates steady demand.

* **Keep a simple log** by date, topic, and questions asked. You'll get faster at future calls, and you'll identify your strongest "products" as an expert. This log becomes the foundation of your expert network consulting practice.

* **Expand formats over time.** Move from 1:1 expert calls into surveys, survey panels, small workshops, or longer advisory projects when available. Some networks offer ongoing engagements that pay monthly retainers.

* **Ask for feedback.** After initial projects, politely ask network associates what worked and what didn't. Update your expert network profiles with new responsibilities, exits, or certifications so you surface for different briefs. Knowledge sharing goes both ways.

* **The math works at scale.** Even at a cost effective rate of $400 per call, four calls per month across two or three networks adds up. Expert networks serve as a repeatable channel for professionals with in-demand expertise.

## Ethics, Compliance, and Long-Term Reputation

Your long-term viability as an expert network consultant depends on zero compliance incidents. One mistake can end your access to every network permanently. This is a potential risk that isn't theoretical.

Common rules across most expert networks:

* No material non-public information (MNPI)

* No confidential pipeline data or internal dashboards

* No specific unannounced contract terms

* No sharing documents from former employers without authorization

Networks brief you on conflict checks and restricted lists before every engagement. Treat any grey area as a hard stop. [GLG's compliance framework](https://glginsights.com/compliance/) includes annual training, identity verification, and employer conflict screening. Expect similar processes at other networks.

Keep your employer's external communication policies in mind. Avoid calls that would place you in direct competitive or contractual conflict with your current or recent roles. Client needs don't override your professional obligations.

Saying "I can't answer that" once or twice per call is normal. It protects everyone involved and actually signals to the client that you take compliance seriously.
