# Operational Due Diligence: A Buyer's Field Guide

Operational due diligence (ODD) is the analysis of how a target company actually runs. It covers people, processes, systems, and controls. It's separate from financial due diligence, and it's where most deal failures originate.

## What is Operational Due Diligence (ODD)?

Operational due diligence assesses a target company's operations, not just its financials. It examines day-to-day effectiveness, operational infrastructure, management capabilities, and processes. For PE, VC, and corporate buyers running the [full due diligence process](/blog/the-due-diligence-process-step-by-step-for-buyers) in 2024 through 2026, most blown deals and write-offs trace back to operational issues, not missed revenue forecasts.

About 25% of private equity deals fail to hit return targets because operational problems missed in diligence catch up later. MSCI's research shows that buyouts ending in write-offs see EBITDA decline by a median 18% below entry levels by year three, while revenue drops only around 3%. The problem is margin erosion, not topline failure.

Whether you call it diligence operational, operational due, or operational due diligence ODD, you're referring to the same core review: how the company makes, delivers, and supports what it sells, and whether that can hold up under new ownership.

Consider a 2025 mid-market software acquisition where the financials showed strong ARR. But billing operations were fragile, with manual contract-to-invoice handoffs creating multi-week delays and millions in unrealized revenue. The numbers looked clean. The operations weren't. ODD would have flagged that gap before the LOI.

Private equity firms use ODD to ensure expected investment returns. It's now standard for institutional investors and serious corporate buyers. It identifies operational strengths, weaknesses, risks, and potential value creation. It's not optional.

## Operational vs Financial Due Diligence

[Financial due diligence](/blog/financial-due-diligence-a-buyer-s-guide-to-getting-it-right) measures overall financial health and reviews past performance. ODD looks forward to assess whether that performance is repeatable and scalable.

| Dimension    | Financial Due Diligence      | Operational Due Diligence                   |
|--------------|------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Time horizon | Past 3-5 years               | Future 3-5 years                            |
| Focus        | Financial statements, inputs | Operational processes, systems, people      |
| Key owners   | CFO, finance team            | COO, operations, technology, HR             |
| Outputs      | Quality of earnings report   | Operational risk map, value creation levers |
ODD focuses on operational efficiency rather than financial performance alone. Issues like weak controls, single-threaded processes, undocumented intellectual capital, and fragile internal procedures don't appear in a financial data room. They're core to ODD.

Sophisticated buyers run both streams in parallel with a shared risk register and integrated diligence process. Findings from both feed into valuation, contract terms, and post merger integration planning.

## Core Objectives of the Operational Due Diligence Process

ODD is a forward-looking risk and value assessment. It identifies operational risks and opportunities for improvement. The objectives vary by acquirer's goals and industry, but the foundation is consistent.

* Identify red flags early: key-person risk, tech debt, process fragility.

* Assess scalability: can operations sustain future growth without unforeseen investments?

* Test the business model: margins, churn, and growth mechanisms in actual form.

* Quantify value creation levers: where operational improvements move margins.

* Stress-test the company's business plan: are assumptions realistic given current capacity?

* Map potential risks to financial impact for decision making.

The due diligence process should connect directly to the investment thesis. For private equity funds, findings feed the 100-day plan. For corporate acquirers, they shape post acquisition integration priorities. ODD helps validate a target company's business plan, not just describe it.

## What's Assessed in Operational Due Diligence

Here are the main workstreams that repeat across most deals. Key areas of ODD include operational infrastructure, management capabilities, and processes.

* **Business model and market position.** This overlaps with commercial due diligence but goes deeper into operational proof points: actual churn mechanics, service levels delivered versus promised, unit economics in practice, and commercial viability at scale.

* **Organizational structure and leadership.** Assessment of organizational structure and the management team is critical. Evaluate span of control, decision rights, succession gaps, and whether managers can execute without founder dependency.

* **Core operational processes and systems.** Production or services delivery flows, IT stack, integrations, cybersecurity posture. Technology evaluation in ODD focuses on system security and scalability. The ODD process includes evaluating technology and systems.

* **Supply chain and fulfillment.** Supply chain and logistics assessment identifies vulnerabilities and efficiency gaps. Review vendor dependences, single-source raw materials suppliers, lead times, and capacity constraints.

* **Data and documentation quality.** Clean data, accessible dashboards, audit trails, and documented SOPs. If critical process knowledge lives only in someone's head, that's a risk.

* **Risk management and compliance.** Operational risks relevant to 2024 through 2026 include data privacy regimes like GDPR and CCPA, third-party vendor risk, ESG exposure, and sanctions screening.

* **Human capital.** Human capital assessment is crucial in operational due diligence. Turnover rates, compensation structures, training gaps, and whether roles exist to support the growth plan.


## Operational Due Diligence in Private Equity and Funds (Manager-Focused ODD)

Institutional allocators also run ODD on fund managers themselves, separate from portfolio company diligence. This diligence operational review covers governance, middle and back office processes, valuation policies, liquidity management, and cybersecurity.

Many LPs treat operational due as a go or no-go gate. Serious operational findings can block a capital raising commitment even if past performance is strong. An emerging manager might be rejected because of weak segregation of duties in finance or valuation methodology gaps detected during the due diligence phase.

The typical ODD checklist for fund managers covers firm ownership, compliance framework, risk management, operations, technology, HR, and business continuity. Allocators increasingly use ongoing ODD monitoring throughout the deal cycle, not just point-in-time reviews at fund close. ODD is a continuous process, and it requires high levels of organization.

## The Field-Tested ODD Process: From First Look to Final Report

Here's a practical odd process a potential buyer can follow, from scoping through the final report. The ODD process is iterative and co-creative with management teams, not a one-way audit.

1. **Scoping.** Align ODD work with the deal thesis, timing constraints, and integration priorities. Your deal team should agree on which operational aspects matter most.

2. **Desktop review.** Pull org charts, SOPs, SLAs, system architecture diagrams, vendor contracts, and KPI decks from the past five years.

3. **Management Q&A.** Interview leadership across functions. Ask about constraints, near misses, resource gaps, and cost overruns. Evaluate the management team directly.

4. **Site or virtual walk-throughs.** On-site assessments verify if documented processes match actual operations. Don't skip this step for services or SaaS businesses either.

5. **Expert calls.** Use networks like FieldSignal to speak with former employees, customers, and suppliers. These calls fill gaps in the data room with unvarnished views of the company's operations.

6. **Synthesis and quantification.** Consolidate findings into a risk map. Estimate financial impact of each finding: CapEx to fix tech debt, margin erosion from process gaps, financial risks from compliance exposure. Risk mitigation planning is part of this step.

7. **Final report.** Final reporting includes developing a post-deal action plan based on ODD findings. Tie everything to valuation adjustments, escrows, covenants, and the [100-day plan](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/pdf/2026/ey-operational-diligence-2-0-a-forward-looking-ai-enabled-rethink-of-diligence-with-value-at-the-core-v1.pdf). No generic narratives.

## Key Focus Areas: Business Model, Market Position, and Scalability

ODD should test whether the existing business model and market position can actually support the buyer's growth case. ODD evaluates if operations can sustain future growth without unforeseen investments.

* **Unit economics.** Examine actual margins at customer level, renewal mechanics, cash flow conversion cycles, pricing discipline, and payment terms.

* **Market position.** Assess customer concentration, actual service levels, product reliability, and complaint data. Customer and supplier relationships matter more than pitch decks. Review supplier relationships for fragility.

* **Scalability.** Analyze capacity headroom, process automation levels, dependency on hero employees, and technology architecture limits.

In a 2024 SaaS acquisition, the buyer discovered the product architecture couldn't support 3x more users without major replatforming. That meant a multi-million CapEx increase that wasn't in the forecast. This is the kind of analysis that separates good diligence from surface-level review.

## Operational Due Diligence Checklist: What To Actually Ask For

This odd checklist gives a junior associate a starting point. Adapt it to sector, deal size, and timing. Don't reuse it blindly.

* **Organization and governance.** Org charts for 2022 through 2025, vacancy lists, succession plans, board minutes, management incentives.

* **Core operations.** SOP manuals, production flow diagrams, throughput KPIs, quality dashboards, sell side data packages.

* **Technology and data.** System inventory, uptime reports from 2023 through 2025, integration diagrams, incident logs, CRM-to-billing workflow maps.

* **HR and culture.** Turnover by function, compensation structures, training programs, culture survey results.

* **Legal and compliance.** Privacy certifications, cybersecurity audit reports, IP ownership records, vendor contract clauses.

* **External relationships.** Top 10 customer contracts, renewal history, vendor scorecards, supplier dependency reviews.

A good diligence checklist also specifies who owns each request, target dates, and priority level so the deal team can manage time pressure during the diligence phase. ## Common ODD Failure Modes and Red Flags

Many costly misses trace back to ODD that was rushed, over-delegated, or treated as a box-ticking exercise. Time constraints often pressure operational due diligence completion. Limited access to information hinders it further. Lack of expertise complicates diligence in unfamiliar industries, and inadequate technology limits effectiveness.

Common process failures include no clear scope, overreliance on management presentations, limited site time, and no independent expert input.

Red flags at the company level that should trigger deeper analysis:

* Key person dependencies where one or two people hold a critical process together.

* Manual mission-critical workflows run on spreadsheets.

* Weak access controls or outdated ERP systems with no documentation.

* Opaque vendor relationships with no backup suppliers.

* Underinvested cybersecurity with no recent audit or breach history.

* Regulatory exposure from noncompliance with data privacy or sanctions rules.

Each red flag maps to a financial impact. [MSCI's research](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/quick-take/before-the-write-off-fundamental-signals-and-valuation-lags-in-buyout) shows that margin decline drives roughly 59% of value loss in failed buyouts. Risk identification in ODD highlights potential operational red flags that could devalue the business. Connect every finding to valuation and deal terms.

## Operational Due Diligence in Practice: A Simple Example

A private equity fund evaluates a €50m revenue industrial components manufacturer with plants in Germany and Mexico. The strategy looks sound. Financial performance over the past five years is solid. But the deal team runs a full ODD.

The diligence checklist uncovers three problems. First, a single failure point in a key stamping machine with no backup and a 16-week lead time for replacement parts. Second, a fragile supplier relationship for specialty raw materials sourced from one vendor with no alternative. Third, undocumented tribal knowledge on the shop floor, where two senior operators hold all setup and calibration procedures in their heads.

Expert calls with former plant managers, arranged through FieldSignal, clarified realistic ramp-up timelines and CapEx needs. The revised CapEx budget increased by €2.1m. The earn-out structure shifted to account for operational changes required in the first year. The 100-day plan was rewritten to focus on maintenance investment, supplier diversification, and SOP documentation. The company unlocks value only after those gaps close. Operational due diligence turned what looked like a clean deal into a set of informed decisions about real risks.
