# Industry Analysis: Frameworks, Examples, and How to Apply Them

Industry analysis tells you whether a space is structurally worth entering, funding, buying, or avoiding. A big market size number isn’t enough.

You need to understand competitive forces, buyer power, supplier power, regulation, technology, and margins before you commit capital.

Investors don’t want a single “$10B TAM” slide. They want to know whether a particular industry can produce durable profits under real market conditions.

Think of industry analysis as climate, and market analysis as weather. Market research explains near-term customer behavior and market demand. Industry analysis explains the structure that drives long-term returns.

## What Is Industry Analysis? (And How It Fits Into Your Business Plan)

Industry analysis is a structured assessment of the competitive forces, regulations, technology, and economic context that drive long-term profitability in a given sector. Industry analysis evaluates competition and market dynamics, not just customer anecdotes.

A comprehensive industry analysis is a market assessment tool at the industry level. Effective industry analysis evaluates market size, growth rate, competitive forces, and trends. It also evaluates economic factors, political factors, legal factors, technological factors, environmental factors, and market factors.

Put it early in the business plan or investment memo, usually in the “Industry Overview” section before market sizing, market segmentation, target market detail, and go-to-market planning.

Investors use it to compare theses, for example outpatient behavioral health clinics versus veterinary roll-ups in 2026 North America. Investors evaluate capital intensity, pricing power, market share potential, growth potential, and expected exit multiples.

Good industry analysis connects directly to strategic decisions on:

* Capital needs and working capital

* Pricing strategies and lower prices risk

* Marketing strategies by market segment

* Exit multiples and market power

* Risk mitigation strategies

## Core Frameworks: How To Structure Your Industry Analysis

Don’t build your memo from scattered facts. Core components and methodologies provide the framework for industry analysis assessment.

The four most useful tools are porter's five forces, PESTEL, Industry Life Cycle Analysis, and Value Chain Analysis, also called chain analysis. SWOT Analysis evaluates strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats, and it’s useful after you’ve done the structural work.

| Framework                    | Use it when you need to know                                        |
|------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Porter’s Five Forces         | Whether industry forces support attractive margins                  |
| PESTEL                       | Which external macro environmental factors matter                   |
| Industry Life Cycle Analysis | Whether the sector is in introduction, growth, maturity, or decline |
| Value Chain Analysis         | Where margin and competitive advantage sit                          |
| SWOT analysis                | Internal factors and external risks for a company or target         |
You don’t need every model in every memo. For a fast screen, combine a competitive forces model with quick value chain analysis. For a seed-stage business plan, 3 to 5 pages can work. For a buyout IC deck, expect 10 to 20 pages.

### Porter’s Competitive Forces Model (Five Forces)

Porter's Five Forces analyzes five competitive forces in an industry. Porter's Five Forces model assesses competitive forces in an industry, and Porter's Five Forces model analyzes five competitive forces: rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, bargaining power of suppliers, and bargaining power of buyers.

Use it to test industry attractiveness.

Example, US telehealth after COVID:

* Rivalry: High, many platforms and specialty providers

* Buyer power: High, payers and employers control reimbursement

* Supplier power: Medium, clinicians and tech vendors matter

* New entrants: Medium, digital entry is easy, compliance is hard

* Substitutes: High, in-person and hybrid care remain strong

Teladoc’s FY 2025 revenue was about $2.5B, down roughly 2% year over year, according to [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/teladoc-health-offers-cautious-2026-outlook-it-shifts-telehealth-model?utm_source=openai). That’s a signal that market growth rate alone doesn’t prove profit durability.

Translate each force into questions:

* How concentrated are payers or customers?

* What are minimum efficient scale requirements?

* What regulatory environment blocks entrants?

* Where do discounts, churn, or price wars appear?

Founders should keep this visual. Use a table with force, rating, and one-line evidence.

### PESTEL: External Forces You Don’t Control

PESTEL Analysis examines political, economic, social, technological, environmental, and legal factors. PESTLE analysis examines political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental factors. PEST analysis evaluates political, economic, social, and technological factors.

Use PESTEL to identify external factors that affect market value. Broad Factor Analysis assesses external factors affecting market value.

Examples:

* EV charging in Europe depends on subsidies, grid buildout, government regulations, and environmental factors.

* US cannabis depends on federal legality, banking access, tax rules, and social acceptance.

* Ad-tech in 2026 depends on data privacy, legal factors, and technology trends.

* Construction services are exposed to interest rates and credit availability.

PESTEL findings should feed your model. Don’t leave them as commentary. If rate sensitivity is high, change volume, margin, and capex scenarios.

### Industry Life Cycle Analysis

Industry Life Cycle Analysis categorizes industries into introduction, growth, maturity, and decline stages. Industry Life Cycle Analysis categorizes industries into growth stages.

Each stage changes strategy:

* Introduction: high R&D, uncertain demand, many business idea tests

* Growth: high hiring, expanding products or services, entrant activity

* Maturity: slower growth, consolidation, margin focus

* Decline: exits, cost cuts, shrinking market demand

Plant-based foods moved from fast growth into slower maturity in several categories. The US retail plant-based food market was about $7.9B in 2025, while plant-based meat and seafood fell about 10% in dollar sales, according to [GFI](https://gfi.org/marketresearch/?utm_source=openai).

Generative AI tooling is earlier in growth, with heavy entrant activity, technology development, and market shifts.

PE often likes mature, cash-generative sectors. Growth equity and founders often want early to mid-growth. Show this with a simple S-curve and a 5 to 10 year outlook.

### Value Chain Analysis (Chain Analysis)

Value Chain Analysis identifies areas of competitive advantage in company operations. Value Chain Analysis examines specific activities where a company adds value to its product.

Map inputs, production, distribution, sales, after-sales, and primary and support activities. Supply Chain Dynamics includes relationships between raw material suppliers, manufacturers, and distributors.

In smartphones, chipmakers, OS providers, OEMs, carriers, retailers, and app stores capture different margin pools. In B2B software, cloud providers can take margin through infrastructure costs, while vendors keep more margin when gross retention and data control are strong.

This helps you choose where to play: asset-light software layer, service-heavy channel, or capital-heavy manufacturing.

## From Theory To Practice: A Simple, Repeatable Industry Analysis Process

The best work follows a repeatable process: define scope, gather data, collect primary research, apply frameworks, then turn findings into strategy.

FieldSignal usually slots into steps 2 and 3, where you need credible expert and customer input.

### Step 1: Define the Industry and the Question You’re Answering

Set boundaries: product scope, geography, customer segment, and time horizon.

Good scope: “US SMB payroll software, 2026 to 2031.”\
Bad scope: “HR tech.”

Good scope: “European mid-market MDR cybersecurity, 2026 to 2030.”\
Bad scope: “Cybersecurity.”

Ask one clear question: “Can a new entrant achieve 20% EBIT margins in five years?” Poor scoping mixes unrelated signals from the same industry and makes the work long but useless.

### Step 2: Collect Secondary Data and Build a Fact Base

Industry analysis requires collecting current industry reports. Use IBISWorld, Statista, broker research, SEC filings, central bank reports, trade associations, and industry statistics from 2020 to 2026.

Market Size and Growth includes determining current revenue and projected growth rates. Analyzing market size and growth is essential for industry analysis. Conducting industry analysis can reveal market growth rates.

Build a one-page sheet:

* Market size

* Market growth rate

* Historical data

* Typical EBITDA margins

* Top five players by market share

* Capital structure norms

* Recent market trends and industry trends

Don’t accept vendor numbers uncritically. Compare 2 to 3 sources and footnote gaps.

### Step 3: Gather Primary Insight (Experts, Customers, and Frontline Operators)

Secondary data is often outdated. It misses current discounting, contract terms, customer preferences, and frontline sales tactics.

Speak with:

* Former product leaders

* Ex-sales reps

* Channel partners

* Major buyers

* Suppliers

* Regulators or policy advisors when relevant

Ask:

1. Where is buyer bargaining power shifting?

2. Which part of the value chain gained margin since 2022?

3. What triggers customer switching?

4. Which competitors win and why?

5. What changed in contract terms?

6. Which technology trends changed buying criteria?

7. Where are the hidden risks?

### Step 4: Apply Frameworks and Quantify Competitive Forces

Now turn raw inputs into judgment. Rate each force high, medium, or low, then attach evidence.

Use concentration ratios, CR3, CR5, HHI, churn, contract duration, discount ranges, and supplier concentration. Avoid “somewhat competitive.” Say where pressure shows up.

Example: “Average contract discounts increased from 8% to 15% since 2021.” That’s useful. “Competition is rising” isn’t.

Data interpretation transforms raw data into actionable insights. Forecasting trends models future demand based on historical data and consumer shifts.

### Step 5: Turn Insights Into Strategy and an Actionable Business Plan

Convert the analysis into 3 to 5 implications.

Examples:

* Pricing power is fragile, so the thesis must rely on cost takeout.

* Market entry strategies should start with niche markets, not the full category.

* Effective market entry strategies can reduce competition risks.

* Choosing the right market entry mode is crucial for success.

* Market entry strategies can include joint ventures and franchising.

* Analyzing competitive forces aids in formulating market entry strategies.

Industry analysis guides resource allocation toward high-growth segments. It also helps you develop risk mitigation strategies before capital is committed.

## Deep Dive Into Competitive Forces: Rivalry, Buyers, Suppliers, Entrants, and Substitutes

Most strategic errors come from misreading one or two forces, especially buyer bargaining power and supplier bargaining power.

IC readers scrutinize this section because it drives risk, pricing power, and defensibility.

### Industry Rivalry

Rivalry is competition among existing companies. It shows up in discounting, marketing spend, feature arms races, and churn.

Track:

* Number of meaningful competitors

* HHI

* Price cuts since 2020

* Marketing spend as percent of revenue

* Churn rates

High rivalry examples include food delivery apps and consumer neobanks. Lower rivalry examples include regulated utilities and some niche industrial components.

High rivalry means you need a differentiated product, cost edge, or lower return expectations.

### Bargaining Power of Buyers

Buyer bargaining power affects pricing and profitability in industries. Bargaining power of buyers rises when customers are concentrated, products are standardized, switching costs are low, and prices are transparent.

Fortune 100 enterprises buying SaaS often demand discounts, security reviews, and custom terms. Measure this through top 10 customer concentration, average contract length, renewal rates, and discount bands.

In many B2B markets, buyer power is the most important input for pricing and margin assumptions.

### Bargaining Power of Suppliers

Supplier bargaining power is the ability of input providers to raise prices or reduce quality without losing business. High supplier power can increase production costs for businesses.

Indicators include few suppliers, proprietary technology, regulatory moats, and high switching costs.

Cloud hyperscalers such as AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud act as key suppliers to SaaS firms. Advanced chipmakers can shape AI and semiconductor economics. Supply shocks from 2020 to 2022 showed how logistics and semiconductor shortages can hit margins.

Responses include dual sourcing, long-term contracts, and vertical integration.

### Threat of New Entrants

The threat of new entrants increases competition intensity. It depends on capital needs, brand loyalty, network effects, licensing, patents, and minimum efficient scale.

Local coffee shops and small DTC brands have lower barriers. Regulated utilities and certified aerospace components have higher barriers.

Digital sectors can look easy to enter, but distribution control, data assets, and compliance can create real barriers.

### Threat of Substitutes

Substitutes increase competition by offering alternative choices. A substitute solves the same job through another category.

Remote work tools substitute for office space. Streaming substitutes for cable TV. Videoconferencing substitutes for business travel.

Measure substitute risk by relative price, convenience, and performance. High substitute risk caps pricing even when direct competition looks limited.

## Competitor and Competitive Analysis: From Industry-Level to Player-Level Insight

Industry analysis explains structure. Competitor analysis explains specific firms. Both belong next to each other in a business plan.

Competitive Landscape involves identifying major players and their market share. Once you understand competitive dynamics, map competitors, strategies, and market position.

SWOT analysis identifies strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. SWOT analysis evaluates strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. SWOT analysis evaluates internal strengths and weaknesses. SWOT analysis identifies external opportunities and threats. SWOT analysis helps formulate effective business strategies. SWOT analysis can be used to compare businesses within an industry. SWOT analysis summarizes four key industry forces.

### Identify and Classify Competitors

Separate direct competitors, indirect competitors, and emerging disruptors.

A B2B payments startup might compare:

* Legacy banks

* Vertical SaaS tools

* Big tech platforms

* Embedded finance providers

Use customer interviews and expert calls to find invisible competitors that don’t show up in search results. Classify by segment focus, geography, pricing model, and sales motion.

Keep the map narrow. Don’t list every company that touches the category.

### Competitive Positioning and Gap Analysis

Competitive positioning is how buyers see you relative to alternatives: price, feature set, service level, specialization, and trust.

Use a simple 2x2 chart, such as price versus feature breadth. Validate it with customer behavior, NPS, churn interviews, and win-loss calls.

Gap analysis identifies underserved needs. For investors, this section should end with a clear “where we win” statement backed by industry forces and competitive analysis.

## Customer Behavior, Data Interpretation, and Key Success Factors

Industry analysis is structural, but you still need customer behavior. Customer behavior connects macro forces to actual buying, renewal, churn, and pricing response.

Combine secondary data with direct feedback. FieldSignal can run targeted surveys or short panels to quantify consumer preferences and B2B customer preferences in a specific industry.

### Market Size, Growth, and Trend Interpretation

You need current market size, realistic growth rates, and segment-level detail.

Sanity-check CAGRs against COVID spikes, 2022 rate hikes, 2023 to 2024 tech spending slowdowns, and 2025 market conditions.

Use TAM, SAM, and SOM only after scope is tight. Trend analysis should cover 3 to 5 shifts, such as regulation, technology adoption, buyer consolidation, and market saturation.

These numbers must match the revenue build in your model.

### Customer Behavior, Preferences, and Switching Patterns

Ask how customers discover, evaluate, buy, renew, and churn.

Key questions:

* What triggers switching suppliers?

* How many vendors will customers maintain?

* How price-sensitive are buyers after cost pressure?

* Which products or services are “must have” versus optional?

Segment matters. SMB buyers act differently from enterprise buyers. Public sector buyers act differently from private buyers.

### Key Success Factors in the Industry

Key success factors are the few capabilities that separate winners from average firms.

Examples:

* Regulatory mastery in healthcare

* Distribution reach in CPG

* Low unit costs in logistics

* Data assets in SaaS

* Human resource management in labor-heavy services

Test whether your target is strong or weak on each factor. Align hiring, capital allocation, and partnerships to these factors.

## Putting It All Together: Examples of Industry Analysis in Action

Examples make frameworks practical. Here are three industries with different industry's dynamics.

### Example 1: Telehealth Services After the COVID Surge

US telehealth expanded rapidly in 2020 and 2021, then normalized as reimbursement and regulations changed.

Key forces:

* High rivalry among platforms

* High payer power

* Regulatory uncertainty

* Strong social acceptance

* Mature technology

Life-cycle stage: early maturity.

Strategic implication: don’t underwrite pure volume growth. Focus on payer relationships, specialty niches, compliance, and reimbursement durability.

### Example 2: B2B Cybersecurity Software

Cybersecurity has strong demand from ransomware, remote work, and regulation. Public cybersecurity company revenue was about $39.4B in 2025, with growth around 16.8%, according to [First Analysis](https://www.firstanalysis.com/research/cybersecurity-mar-2026/?utm_source=openai).

Key forces:

* Many entrants

* High switching costs for large enterprises

* Strong urgency from buyers

* Powerful cloud suppliers

* Technology developments changing standards

Value chain analysis shows better opportunities in managed detection, incident response, and specialized workflows than generic tools. The sector is attractive, but weak competitive positioning leads to commoditization.

### Example 3: Plant-Based Foods

Plant-based foods had rapid growth around 2018 to 2021, then slower retail growth and more shelf competition by 2024 to 2026.

Key forces:

* High rivalry

* Strong retailer bargaining power

* Private-label pressure

* Substitutes from meat, dairy, and whole foods

* Premium pricing risk

Industry analysis would flag retailer power and private label as margin risks. Category enthusiasm alone overestimated long-term profitability.
