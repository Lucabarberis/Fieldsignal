# Is Third Bridge Legit? An Honest Review

Third Bridge is one of the most searched expert network names by PE associates, strategy analysts, and founders trying to figure out if it's worth their time and money. Here's what we found after reviewing public data, expert feedback, and how it stacks up against alternatives.

## Quick answer: Is Third Bridge legit?

Yes. Third Bridge is a legitimate, global expert network used by serious investment firms. It's not a scam, not a fly-by-night marketplace, and not something you need to google endlessly to verify. The company is profiled on Bloomberg as a legitimate business, serves over 1,000 investment firms globally, and has a team of 1,200+ employees across seven offices.

Third Bridge connects hedge funds, private equity shops, mutual funds, and consultancies with subject matter experts to support investment decisions. It's considered one of the "big five" expert networks alongside GLG, AlphaSights, Guidepoint, and Tegus.

That said, it's not perfect for every use case. The main tradeoffs you'll find below: opaque pricing, occasional payment friction for experts, variable project flow, and a commercial model that favors large funds over smaller teams. If you're a large fund or global consultancy running hundreds of calls a year, Third Bridge is a strong option. If you're a lean corporate team, a boutique fund, or a founder, you'll likely find a lower cost, more flexible alternative like FieldSignal a better fit.

## What is Third Bridge and how does it work?

Third Bridge is a global expert network and investment research firm. It was founded in 2007 in London (originally called Cognolink) and now operates offices in New York, other major cities, and across four continents.

The core business model: clients submit a research brief, Third Bridge sources and screens experts, then arranges paid phone consultations. Third Bridge conducts over 10,000 interviews each year and clients can access 15,000+ previous interview transcripts through their library product. The company connects specialists to investors via phone consultations, covering areas like US healthcare provider due diligence or European SaaS churn analysis.

Third Bridge earns revenue by charging clients a fee per call, which basically includes a markup on the expert's honorarium. They also sell access to their transcript library and other research products. [Estimated annual revenue sits around $318 million](https://growjo.com/company/Third_Bridge_Group).

## Is Third Bridge safe and compliant to work with?

Third Bridge operates with a formal compliance framework similar to other large expert networks like GLG and AlphaSights. The firm adheres to strict global codes of ethics and conducts due diligence to manage risk.

Their compliance program covers three pillars: confidentiality agreements, training, and specialist screening. Experts can't share material non-public information, proprietary data, or anything about current employers. Experts require compliance and vetting processes to ensure quality and reliability in consultations. Third Bridge has a compliance program ensuring confidentiality during consultations, and they present [ISO 27001, SOC 2, and GDPR certifications](https://trust.thirdbridge.com/) as proof of their security posture.

For regulated investors, using a structured expert network is generally safer than ad hoc outreach. You still need to follow your own firm's policies, but the intermediary layer reduces legal exposure. ## What do experts say about working with Third Bridge?

Third Bridge has a mixed reputation with professionals highlighting payment speed and work availability issues, but the overall sentiment skews positive. On [Trustpilot, it scores roughly 4.5 out of 5 across 239 reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/thirdbridge.com).

**What people like:**

* Many experts enjoy working with Third Bridge's coordination team. Staff are described as professional, supportive, and engaging to interact with.

* Onboarding is easy once your profile is set up. The scheduling and screening process feels smooth and well-structured.

* Many experts report positive experiences with Third Bridge's payment processes. Consultants often receive prompt and transparent compensation, with payment processing that can take 2-3 days after consultations.

**What people don't like:**

* Some consultants report issues with not being compensated as agreed, particularly on survey-type assignments. [BBB reviews](https://www.bbb.org/us/ny/new-york/profile/financial-consultants/third-bridge-us-inc-0121-171114/customer-reviews) include instances of calls completed but payments delayed or missing.

* Repetitive screening questions and long qualification calls that don't always lead to paid work. It takes time, and some experts feel the workload of prescreening isn't worth it.

* Some consultants experience unprofessional questioning during interviews. You'll also hear experts say the flow of opportunities is inconsistent across months.

Overall, the things experts flag most often, trust, ethics, confidentiality, are strengths. That supports the "legit" conclusion. The friction is real but relatively rare compared to the volume of people who say it works fine.

## What types of Third Bridge consulting calls and projects exist?

Third Bridge clients find it essential for obtaining niche industry insights and market intelligence. Consulting projects include industry overviews and company deep dives. Here are the main categories:

1. **Industry overview calls.** Experts discuss market structure, key trends, and competitive dynamics in sectors like medtech, logistics, or fintech. You learn how the board-level view differs from what's in public filings.

2. **Company deep dives.** Calls focused on a specific target company's go-to-market, customer behavior, unit economics, or churn drivers. These are common in PE due diligence.

3. **Consulting with consultants.** Clients talk to former operators or management consultants who can synthesize broader patterns and benchmarks across a sector.

Calls usually last around 60 minutes. Billing is typically per minute of completed call time. Third Bridge also offers a transcript library where you can search and read recorded interviews on demand. Experts can't share confidential or inside information about current or very recent employers on any of these calls.

## Do you qualify as a Third Bridge expert and how do you set your rate?

If you have meaningful, recent experience in a specific company, product, or niche market, you can likely find a place on the network. Typical expert profiles include former mid-to-senior operators, experienced individual contributors, ex-consultants, and people who've held a job in specialized functions.

Third Bridge screens for depth of experience, recency and relevance to active investment theses, and ability to communicate clearly within compliance limits.

On rate setting: consultants earn $100 to $500 per hour at Third Bridge. Consultants set their hourly rates, which Third Bridge marks up before presenting to clients. Third Bridge pays consultants per minute of consultation time. Competitive rates are often negotiated by professionals who successfully match with clients.

Consultants report sporadic work opportunities with no guarantees. Don't treat this as a primary income source. It's supplemental. If your area is niche and not in high demand, you may not consult for months at a time.
