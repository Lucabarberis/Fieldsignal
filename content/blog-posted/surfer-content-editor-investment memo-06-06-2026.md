## What Is an Investment Memo? (Definition & Purpose)

An investment memo is a structured document that summarizes a deal, the investment rationale, key findings from the diligence process, potential returns, and a recommendation for an investment committee or partners’ meeting.

Investment memos synthesize market analysis and financial projections. Investment memos contain summaries of due diligence, risks, and potential returns. Investment memos help investors make informed decisions efficiently.

They’re used across:

* Venture capital, for example a Series A SaaS company or seed stage startup

* Private equity, for example a platform buyout or 2025 carve-out acquisition

* Corporate development, where strategic fit and integration costs matter

* Family offices and independent sponsors reviewing potential deals

Investment memos document due diligence for future reference. Investment memos provide a time-stamped record of assumptions made when approving an investment. Investment memos create a searchable archive of deal analysis, enhance accountability by documenting decisions, and help surface logical gaps in investment reasoning.

They aren’t CIMs, pitch decks, or fundraising strategy documents. A good memo is balanced analysis, with dissenting views, risk factors, and mitigation strategies included.

## Standard Investment Memo Structure (PE & VC)

* Executive Summary: One page with company name, sector, stage, revenue, valuation, check size, target ownership, recommendation, and top risks.

* Investment Thesis: Why this company, why now, why this price, and why the market opportunity supports the deal.

* Company Overview: Founding year, HQ, target company profile, product, customers, revenue model, and current stage.

* Market & Competitive Landscape: Market size, market landscape, competitive landscape, adoption drivers, and competitive analysis against direct and indirect competitors.

* Product & Business Model: Product workflow, pricing, retention, product market fit, value proposition, and product roadmap.

* Financial Analysis: Historical performance, financial health, financial projections, cash flow, unit economics, IRR, MOIC, and valuation cases.

* Management Team: CEO, CFO, CTO, CRO, founders, governance, existing investors, other investors, and track record.

* Risk Factors: Market, competitive, product, execution, financial, regulatory, and key-person risks.

* Deal Terms: Security, ownership, liquidation preference, covenants, debt, and follow-on capital needs.

* Recommendation: Invest, pass, or further diligence, with data-driven conclusions.

Most firms use a fixed structure so partners compare potential deals across vintages. Investment memos typically range from 10 to 25 pages long. Concise memos are preferable and should limit to 2 to 4 pages for early screening or fast partner review.

## Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Busy partners often read this page first, then decide whether the rest deserves time. Investment recommendations in memos should be backed by data-driven conclusions, not loose conviction.

Include:

* Company name, sector, stage, and round

* Revenue or ARR as of the latest quarter, such as Q1 2026

* YoY growth, burn, EBITDA, or cash flow

* Proposed investment amount, valuation, ownership, and fund exposure

* Target returns, such as 3.0x MOIC and 20 to 25% IRR over 5 to 7 years

* Decision requested: invest, pass, or further diligence

Write the investment thesis in 3 to 5 bullets:

* Why this particular startup or company wins

* Why the timing fits the investment landscape

* Why the price meets investor expectations

* Why the business model can scale

* Why the team can execute

A strong investment thesis connects company strengths to market opportunities. Investment memos should present a compelling investment thesis, and investment memos should clearly articulate the investment thesis, risks, and opportunities.

## Company, Market, and Competitive Landscape

Open with facts. Don’t start with adjectives.

Show:

* Founding year, HQ, legal entity, founders, and ownership

* Core product, target customers, and revenue model

* Business stage, such as Seed, Series B, growth equity, or platform buyout

* Market size using TAM, SAM, and SOM with sources and dates

* Adoption drivers, customer pain, regulatory change, and technology shifts from 2022 to 2026

* 5 to 10 named competitors, including incumbents and new entrants

Key components include an executive summary and market analysis. Key components in an investment memo include market analysis, team assessment, and financial health.

## Product, Business Model, and Traction

Describe what the customer does with the product. State which workflow it replaces and why customers pay.

Include:

* Pricing structure: subscription, usage-based, license plus maintenance, or services

* ACV, contract length, gross retention, net revenue retention, churn, and upsell paths

* Revenue or ARR progression from 2021 to 2025

* User counts, logo growth, cohort quality, and renewal behavior

* Key metrics that prove traction against peers

Good signals include rising retention, short payback, customer pull, and expansion revenue. Weak signals include flat cohorts, high churn, unclear buyer urgency, and product roadmap dependence.

For venture capital memos, product roadmap clarity matters. Venture capitalists want to know which 2026 to 2027 releases can change growth potential or unit economics. For a venture capital fund, the memo also has to demonstrate how a startup could become one of the successful companies that returns the fund.

## Financial Analysis & Unit Economics

This is where private equity and growth equity teams spend the most time. Venture capital uses lighter modeling, especially when a company has limited operating history.

Financial analysis assesses historical performance and projections. Include:

* 2022 to 2024 actuals and latest 12 months

* Revenue, gross margin, operating expenses, EBITDA, burn, and cash flow

* 5-year financial projections, usually 2026 to 2030

* Bottoms-up drivers: sales headcount, conversion rates, pricing, churn, and expansion

* CAC, payback period, LTV/CAC, gross margin by product line, contribution margin, and sales efficiency

Investment memos should justify valuation with financial projections derived from comparable analysis. Show entry valuation, exit multiple, IRR, MOIC, and base, upside, and downside cases.

Using data-driven storytelling is essential in writing an investment memo. Visuals like charts and graphs can enhance the digestibility of investment memos.

Common return targets in PE and growth equity often center on 20 to 25% IRR and 2.5x to 3.0x MOIC, though actual fund outcomes vary by vintage, sector, and exit market. Don’t assume multiple expansion. Over-optimistic projections can mislead investors.

## Management Team, Governance, and References

The management team section should prove execution capacity, not list biographies.

Show:

## Risk Factors, Mitigation, and Bear Case

Strong memos in 2024 to 2026 treat risk factors as central content. Investment memos must include an objective assessment of potential risks and mitigation strategies.

Risk factors identify potential financial and market risks. Classify risks this way:

| Risk type   | Example                    | Mitigation                           |
|-------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Market      | Adoption slows after 2025  | Validate buyer urgency through calls |
| Competitive | New entrant cuts price     | Test win/loss data                   |
| Product     | Roadmap slips              | Tie funding to milestones            |
| Execution   | Sales hiring misses plan   | Review hiring funnel                 |
| Financial   | Margin erosion             | Stress test CAC and pricing          |
| Regulatory  | Rule change affects demand | Interview compliance experts         |
| Key-person  | CEO dependency             | Assess bench strength                |
Insufficient risk analysis often leads to poor investment decisions. Failing to provide balanced analysis is a frequent error. Lack of thorough due diligence is a common mistake.

Write a bear case as a real scenario: growth slows, pricing compresses, churn rises, and the exit multiple falls. Include probability, impact, and mitigation. The memo should demonstrate deep due diligence and preparedness for follow-up questions.

## Deal Terms, Structure, and Exit Paths

* State security type: preferred equity, common equity, debt, convertible note, or structured equity.

* Include valuation, ownership percentage, liquidation preference, anti-dilution terms, pro rata rights, and board rights.

* For PE, show equity vs. debt mix, leverage ratios, covenants, seller notes, earn-outs, and cash flow coverage.

* Summarize syndicate composition, including co-investors, other investors, reputation, and follow-on capital capacity.

* List exit paths: strategic sale, sponsor-to-sponsor sale, or IPO.

* Name likely buyer categories and timing assumptions.

* End with how the structure aligns management, existing shareholders, and new investors.

## How Investment Memos Differ Across Fund Types

Different formats of memos may exist based on the business stage. The structure is similar, but emphasis changes.

| Fund type                   | Most space goes to                                 | Less space goes to          |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Early-stage venture capital | Team, market size, product vision                  | Detailed financial analysis |
| Growth equity               | Revenue quality, retention, path to profit         | Pure vision                 |
| Buyout PE                   | Cash flow, debt capacity, downside protection      | Early product theory        |
| Corporate development       | Strategic fit, integration costs, operating impact | IRR and MOIC alone          |
Seed rounds may use fewer than 10 pages. Large buyouts can exceed 30 pages with appendices. For understanding vc decision making, remember that vc firms underwrite uncertainty differently than buyout investors.

## Practical Investment Memo Template (Section-by-Section)

* Executive Summary, 1 page

  * Recommendation, company name, check size, valuation, ownership

  * Top 3 reasons to invest, top 3 risks

  * Base, upside, and downside return cases

* Investment Thesis, 1 to 2 pages

  * Why now, why this company, why this price

  * Market opportunity and investment rationale

  * Core assumptions to test

* Company & Market, 2 to 3 pages

  * Company history, customers, market size

  * Market analysis and adoption drivers

  * Expert call snippets

* Product & Business Model, 2 to 3 pages

  * Product workflow, pricing, retention

  * Business model and unit economics

  * Product roadmap and customer quotes

* Financial Analysis, 3 to 5 pages

  * Historical financials, projections, valuation

  * Comparable analysis and return cases

  * Sensitivity tables

* Team, 2 to 3 pages

  * Management team table

  * References and governance

  * Existing investors

* Risk Factors, 2 to 3 pages

  * Risk register

  * Bear case

  * Mitigation strategies

* Deal Terms, 1 to 2 pages

  * Security, ownership, rights

  * Debt, covenants, exit paths

  * Alignment across parties

* Recommendation, 1 page

  * Invest, pass, or further diligence

  * Conditions precedent

  * Open diligence items

## Workflow: From Due Diligence Notes to Finished Investment Memo

1. Capture initial call notes, including claims, metrics, and unanswered questions.

2. Run desk research on market, company, competitors, and valuation comps.

3. Schedule expert and customer calls through FieldSignal for diligence.

4. Build the financial model with historicals, projections, unit economics, and sensitivities.

5. Draft the investment thesis and assumption log.

6. Write the first memo draft with charts, tables, and short commentary.

7. Collect partner feedback and note dissent.

8. Finalize the IC-ready memo and presentation.

Keep an assumption log during the due diligence process. Convert it into risks, bear case items, and follow-up questions.

## Recommendation Page, IC Discussion, and Post-Mortems

The final page should be direct. Investment memos facilitate communication with stakeholders during approvals, so don’t bury the answer.

Include:

* Recommendation: invest, pass, or further diligence

* Proposed investment size and ownership

* Conditions precedent

* Remaining diligence items, owner, and deadline

* “How we’ll know we were wrong” indicators

* Final rationale tied to data

Investment memos require thorough due diligence and risk evaluation. An unclear investment thesis can confuse stakeholders. An effective investment memo balances thorough due diligence with concise storytelling. Investment memos must be concise and directly inform the reader.

Firms often revisit memos 2 to 5 years later to review winners and losses. The memo becomes evidence of what investors believed, what risks they accepted, and how well the firm made investment decisions.
