# Real Estate Market Research: Methods and Sector Vendors

Real estate market research is the process of collecting and interpreting real estate data on pricing, demand, supply, cap rates, rents, absorption, and external factors so you can make investment, acquisition, or development decisions. It helps real estate investors, PE/VC teams, corporate M&A analysts, brokers, agents, founders, buyers, and sellers avoid bad pricing, weak submarkets, and projects that won't lease.

This article covers residential and commercial real estate, but the focus is the commercial real estate market: office, industrial, retail properties, multifamily, shopping centers, data centers, and medical office.

## Real estate market research: fast definition and why it matters

As of 2026, rising interest rates, uneven office recovery, foreign investment impacts on both commercial and residential sectors, and strong industrial and multifamily demand make disciplined market analysis essential. Economic conditions affect real estate market performance, interest rates impact buyer affordability in real estate, and supply and demand significantly influence real estate prices.

Market research beats rules of thumb because:

* A real estate market can split sharply by neighborhoods, quality, and access to public transportation.

* High inventory indicates a buyer's market while low inventory suggests a seller's market.

* Low vacancy rates usually suggest high demand in real estate markets.

* Days on Market indicates market demand; low DOM indicates high demand.

* Real estate market analysis evaluates current property market values and helps determine property value.

* Effective real estate market research minimizes risk and identifies profitable investment opportunities.

## Core components of a real estate market study

A real estate market study is a checklist for one property type and geography. Market analysis includes studying demand, supply, and environment.

Check these data points before underwriting:

1. **Macro context:** Evaluate local economic health to ensure long-term tenant or buyer demand. Look for diverse employment opportunities and low unemployment rates for economic stability.

2. **Submarket definition:** Define the city, submarket, and micro-area.

3. **Demand drivers:** Population growth drives demand for housing in specific areas. Rapid population growth among young people increases affordable housing demand.

4. **Supply and pipeline:** High permit activity for new construction can indicate future oversupply.

5. **Pricing and rent:** Track rental prices, asking price, effective rent, concessions, and prices for similar properties.

6. **Vacancy and absorption:** Compare recent leasing against deliveries.

7. **Capital markets:** Track cap rates, lender terms, and potential returns. CRED iQ reported U.S. CRE cap rates rose from about 5.91% in Q1 2025 to 6.28% by year-end.

8. **Regulation:** Government regulations can alter real estate market dynamics. Local governance affects cost of ownership and the feasibility of projects.

For stabilized assets, focus on in-place rent, renewals, tenant quality, and exit cap rate. For development, focus on entitlement risk, construction cost, pipeline supply, and lease-up timing.

### Defining the target market and submarket

The first step is scope. “Texas multifamily” isn’t enough. “East Austin Class A/B multifamily within 3 miles of I-35 in 2026” is usable.

Segment by:

* Class A/B/C office.

* Garden vs mid-rise multifamily.

* Power centers vs neighborhood shopping centers.

* Bulk vs last-mile industrial.

Poor definitions distort value. Mixing trophy CBD office with older suburban stock hides the real market situation. Institutional reports often use different submarket boundaries, so standardize your own.

### Demand analysis: tenants, buyers, and users

Demand analysis turns demographic, economic, and user data into unit or square-foot demand. Use 2015–2025 population growth, household formation, income bands, census data, labor data, broker reports, and national association sources for 2026–2030 forecasts.

Segment users by sector:

* Office: tech, legal, finance, healthcare.

* Retail: grocery-anchored vs discretionary.

* Industrial: e-commerce, manufacturing, 3PLs.

* Multifamily: renters by necessity vs choice.

Remote work trends drive housing demand in peripheral areas, while remote and hybrid policies from 2020–2024 reduced some office footprints. E-commerce growth from 2018–2025 supported warehouse absorption.

### Supply, pipeline, and competing product

Count stock, vacancy, and deliveries. For example, track Phoenix multifamily deliveries from 2022–2026, then compare concessions and lease-up velocity.

Map the competition landscape because understanding the competition landscape is crucial for market entry strategies. Market competition analysis identifies strategies of other players in the market. Compare competing property by year built, amenities, parking, concessions, and access. Data collection is crucial for assessing market viability, and data collection is crucial for determining project viability.

Public data lags. Call local contractors, planners, leasing brokers, and former operators to verify permits and stalled projects.

### Pricing, rents, cap rates, and absorption

Comparative price analysis reveals property pricing trends. A comparative market analysis helps estimate fair market value by comparing similar properties, recent sales, rent, and occupancy.

| Effective rent | $X         | $Y             | Rent growth      |
| Vacancy        | X%         | Y%             | Downtime         |

If a Q1 2026 deal prices 30–40 bps tighter than recent comps, explain why. If you can't, don't invest.

### Regulatory, infrastructure, and environmental factors

Regulation, infrastructure, and climate can affect value as much as rent. Investigate proximity to schools and public transportation when evaluating property.

Check zoning, rent control, short-term rental rules, taxes, transit, highway work, flood zones, wildfire exposure, and power constraints. Minneapolis ended single-family-only zoning in 2019, and California ADU reforms increased housing supply. NAR analyzed eight successful office-to-housing conversions in 27 markets, and 27 markets saw significant office-to-housing conversions post-pandemic.

Validate local rules with attorneys, planners, or former permitting officials. Don't rely on old PDFs.

## Methods and data sources for real estate market research

Good real estate market analysis combines three methods: secondary research, quantitative tools, and primary insights. Secondary sources frame the estate market, paid datasets add precision, and expert calls explain why numbers moved.

### Secondary data: broker reports, associations, and public records

Start with CBRE, JLL, Cushman, Colliers, NAR commercial research, Federal Reserve data, local assessors, deeds, permits, and planning minutes. Commercial real estate reports cover 390 market factors driving transactions.

Secondary reports help identify opportunities, but they lag. Reporting results helps investors make informed decisions, yet reports rarely explain why tenants are leaving, landlords are discounting, or lenders are pulling back.

### Quantitative real estate data and analytics tools

Use transaction databases, leasing data, hotel tools, multifamily trackers, GIS, and BI tools for analyzing market trends. Location intelligence enhances decision-making in commercial real estate analysis.

Foot traffic data informs retail property demand assessments. Commercial real estate relies on foot traffic data for demand insights, and foot traffic data is crucial for evaluating retail property demand. Still, coverage gaps, black-box methods, and bad tags on new construction can reduce accuracy.

### Primary research: expert interviews, tenant surveys, and panels

Primary research gives you the “why.” Interview former leasing brokers, property managers, asset managers, lenders, tenants, and suppliers.

## Commercial real estate market sectors: how research differs by asset type

Commercial real estate isn’t one market. Each real estate sector has different factors, industries, buyers, sellers, tools, and success metrics.

### Office: flight to quality and hybrid work

Start with utilization, not historical averages. Older Class B/C CBD offices weakened from 2020–2025, while amenity-rich Class A in certain places like Austin, Miami, and Nashville held up better.

Check sublease space, effective rents, TI, term length, tenant concentration, ESG retrofit costs, and conversion feasibility.

### Industrial and logistics: e-commerce, reshoring, and data-driven site selection

Industrial research focuses on ports, highways, airports, labor, freight, and population centers. Inland Empire, Dallas–Fort Worth, and Atlanta saw strong absorption in recent years.

Check vacancy, clear height, dock ratios, trailer parking, new supply, and freight data.

### Multifamily: migration, affordability, and new supply

Multifamily analysis centers on household formation, income growth, rent-to-income ratios, and migration. Austin, Phoenix, and Tampa saw heavy deliveries from 2021–2025, creating longer lease-ups and more concessions in 2026.

Demographic data analysis predicts future housing demand through 2030.

### Retail properties and shopping centers: foot traffic and tenant mix

Retail research starts with visitation, trade area, anchor strength, daytime population, and tenant sales. Grocery-anchored and value retail performed better than weaker discretionary formats from 2019–2026.

For a 200,000 SF open-air center, test whether small shops have sustainable sales per square foot.

### Specialty sectors: data centers, medical office, and hotels

Data centers require power, fiber, latency, and permits. Northern Virginia and Dublin faced tight power markets.

Medical office depends on aging populations, outpatient care, hospital strategy, and reimbursement. Hotels require occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, and business, leisure, or group segmentation.

## Step-by-step process to run a real estate market analysis

Use this 7-step process for a live deal or thesis:

1. Define scope and asset type within 48 hours.

2. Compile secondary data within 3 days.

3. Map supply and competitors by day 4.

4. Build a preliminary model by day 5.

5. Test assumptions with expert calls over days 4–10.

6. Refine underwriting by day 10.

7. Document risks, upside, and decision.

For a Sun Belt self-storage project, this means narrowing MSAs, mapping permits, checking rent growth, calling operators, and deciding whether demand supports development.

### Scoping the research: questions, geography, and asset type

Turn “look at multifamily in Texas” into a specific project: North Dallas Class B garden assets built 2000–2015, value-add thesis, five-year hold, target return, and exit cap rate.

Don't run a 50-market study for one $20M deal.

### Structuring and running primary research with experts

Draft a hypothesis memo, write 10–15 questions, then segment experts: brokers, lenders, tenants, local developers, and former city officials.

Run 5–15 one-hour calls through FieldSignal over 7–10 days. Code transcripts for themes like rent softness, lender pullback, construction delays, and tenant expansion.

### Translating findings into models and investment memos

Update rent growth, vacancy, exit cap rate, lease-up timing, and construction costs. Use sensitivity tables because financing conditions from 2022–2025 were volatile.

Your memo should state the thesis, evidence, risks, upside, and whether the research supports buying, selling, building, or walking away.
