# Best Note-Taking Apps for Customer Interviews: 8 Tested

Customer interviews are only as useful as the notes you walk away with. The best note taking app for interviews captures insights in real time, keeps your data organized, and doesn't force you to choose between listening and writing. We tested eight apps across remote calls, in-person conversations, and phone interviews to find what actually works.

Note-taking apps are tailored to different workflows. What works for jotting down grocery lists won't hold up when you're conducting interviews for due diligence, customer discovery, or market validation. Customer interviews demand structured notes, speaker identification, fast follow ups, and the ability to share key insights with your team. Use cases for note-taking apps range from project management to research, and the gap between a general note app and a purpose-fit interview tool is wide.

AI note-takers automate transcription and summarization tasks that used to eat hours of post-interview work. AI note-takers can capture vital information from interviews and meetings. The question is which tool does it best for your specific setup.

Here's what we found.

## How We Chose the Best Customer Interview Apps

Top note-taking apps offer features like customization and collaboration. But not every feature matters equally when you're sitting across from a customer or listening to an expert on a video call.

We evaluated each app against seven criteria:

* **Recording and transcription accuracy.** How well does the app handle real time transcription during actual customer conversations? Does it identify speakers? What happens with background noise or accents?

* **Speed of insight extraction.** How fast can you move from a raw transcript to organized interview notes with themes, quotes, and action items? AI note-taking apps can generate summaries and action items automatically, but quality varies.

* **Team collaboration and sharing.** Can you share transcripts, tag teammates, leave comments? AI note-takers enhance collaboration by sharing notes easily, but the depth of collaboration features differs.

* **CRM and research tool integrations.** Does the app connect to Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack, or project management tools? Integration matters for customer success teams and sales organizations.

* **Privacy and compliance.** Encryption, data ownership, consent management. Critical if you're handling sensitive customer data or expert interviews under compliance agreements.

* **Ease of use during live interviews.** Mobile support, minimal friction, ability to tag and highlight in real time. You shouldn't need a tutorial to take notes during a call.

* **Value for money.** Free plan availability, paid plans pricing, per-user costs, and whether the free version is functional enough to evaluate.

AI note-taking tools improve productivity for students and professionals, but the tools that score highest here are the ones built for business interview workflows.

## Best 8 Note-Taking Apps for Customer Interviews

### 1. Otter.ai

Otter.ai is an ai note taker app built around live transcription and automated meeting notes. Founded as AISense in 2016, it uses machine learning to transcribe conversations, identify speakers, and generate ai summaries after calls. It made the list because it's one of the few tools that handles the full cycle from recording to organized notes without manual effort.

**Why It Stands Out:** Real time transcription with speaker identification. Otter.ai integrates with Zoom, Google Meet, and Slack for live transcription during virtual meetings. Its ai meeting assistant auto-joins scheduled calls via google calendar integration, records the conversation, and delivers a transcript with ai generated summaries within minutes.

**Best For:** Remote customer calls and structured interview follow ups. If you conduct most of your interviews over video conferencing software, Otter handles the note capture so you can focus on asking better questions.

**Key Strengths:**

* Live transcription with speaker labels

* AI summaries and key points extraction

* Auto-joins calls through calendar integration

* Searchable transcripts across all the notes in your workspace

Otter.ai's free plan is limited to 300 minutes per month, which covers roughly 5-10 interviews depending on length.

**Possible Limitations:** Accuracy drops with multiple speakers, strong accents, or poor audio quality. One [comparative study of transcription platforms](https://nzmj.org.nz/media/pages/journal/vol-138-no-1625/a-comparative-assessment-of-ai-and-manual-transcription-quality-in-health-data-insights-from-field-observations/6fc633e389-1762211509/7024.pdf) found that manual transcription produced roughly 17 errors on a 3-minute clip, while Otter produced around 84. That's a meaningful gap when important details matter. The tool also requires an internet connection, meaning it won't work well for in-field interviews with poor connectivity. A 2025 lawsuit alleged Otter recorded conversations [without all-party consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otter.ai), which is worth considering if you handle sensitive customer data.

AI note-takers provide searchable transcripts for easy access, and Otter does this well. But you'll want to review raw transcripts before treating them as final records.

### 2. Notion

Notion isn't a transcription tool. It's a workspace where you organize, structure, and analyze your interview notes after they're captured. It made this list because no other tool matches its flexibility for building a structured customer research program.

**Why It Stands Out:** Database-driven interview organization with custom templates. Notion's [template gallery](https://www.notion.com/templates/category/free-user-interviews-templates) includes interview agendas, feedback forms, and tracking databases. You can relate interviews to customer personas, product features, and research themes using linked databases. Notion excels at project management and team collaboration, and that extends to managing an ongoing interview process.

**Best For:** Systematic customer research programs with structured data collection. If you're running 20+ interviews across multiple segments, Notion helps you create notes, tag them, and surface patterns.

**Key Strengths:**

* Custom interview templates with pre-built structures

* Database views (table, kanban, gallery) for organizing interview notes by date, persona, or status

* Notion integrates with various apps like Slack and Google Drive for pulling in data from other tools

* Built-in collaboration with comments, permissions, and shared pages

Notion offers a free plan for personal use and costs $12/user/month for the Plus plan.

**Possible Limitations:** The learning curve is real. A [survey of 50+ Notion business templates](https://www.reddit.com/r/Notion/comments/1qiemub/research_i_tested_50_notion_business_templates/) found that 95% failed to deliver because they were empty databases or overly complex for mobile use. You'll need to invest time designing your own notes structure or adapting an existing one. Notion also has no built-in transcription, so you'll need to pair it with other tools like Otter or Fireflies to handle audio recordings.

### 3. Fireflies.ai

Fireflies.ai is a meeting bot that joins your calls, records, transcribes, and tags action items automatically. It's designed for teams that need their interview notes flowing directly into CRM records and project management systems.

**Why It Stands Out:** Deep CRM integrations and conversation intelligence. Fireflies.ai integrates with Slack and project management tools like Asana, Trello, Notion, and Monday.com. It supports [over a dozen CRMs](https://fireflies.ai/integrations/crm) including Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Pipedrive, and Zoho. For Dynamics 365 specifically, it can auto-create lead or contact objects when they don't exist. Fireflies.ai offers advanced search functionality for transcripts, letting you search across all past conversations by keyword, topic, or speaker.

**Best For:** Sales teams and customer success teams conducting customer discovery calls. If your interview process feeds directly into deal progression or account management, Fireflies saves hours of manual data entry.

AI note-takers can generate action items from conversations, and Fireflies does this well, auto-tagging tasks and pushing them to your project management tool of choice.

**Key Strengths:**

* Auto-logs transcripts, action items, and tasks into CRM records

* Conversation analytics with keyword and topic detection

* Automated follow ups pushed to project tools

* SOC 2 Type II and GDPR compliance

* Meeting data is not used to train external AI models

Fireflies.ai has a G2 rating of 4.5/5 based on over 500 reviews.

**Possible Limitations:** Cost scales with users and features. Integration setup (especially dialer + CRM) can take 5-10 business days per integration. The bot-joining approach can feel intrusive during sensitive customer conversations. For small teams, pricing can become restrictive on paid plans.

### 4. Microsoft OneNote

OneNote is a free-form digital canvas that ships with Office 365. It doesn't record or transcribe calls on its own, but it's a strong manual note taking tool for enterprise teams already inside the Microsoft ecosystem.

**Why It Stands Out:** Freeform canvas with enterprise security and compliance. You can mix typed text, handwriting, images, and sketches on a single page. OneNote uses Optical Character Recognition to search text in images and scanned documents, which helps when you're working with printed interview guides or whiteboard photos. Microsoft OneNote integrates with Google Calendar for automatic note creation, so you can have a new notes page ready before each interview.

**Best For:** Enterprise teams needing Office 365 integration and security. If your organization runs on microsoft teams and Office 365, OneNote fits without adding another vendor to your stack.

**Key Strengths:**

* Free with Office 365 subscription

* Flexible formatting (typed, handwritten, sketched)

* Enterprise-grade security under E5 licenses

* Cross-platform with a windows app and mobile apps

* OCR-powered search across attachments

**Possible Limitations:** No built-in transcription or ai features for calls. You'll need a separate real time transcription tool or manual note taking during interviews. Notebooks can become disorganized quickly without deliberate structure. It's a general-purpose tool, not specifically designed for the interview process.

### 5. Evernote

Evernote has been around longer than most of the tools on this list. It's a document management and search platform that handles large volumes of notes, PDFs, and scanned documents well.

**Why It Stands Out:** Powerful search across all notes and attachments. Evernote's full-text search spans note content, PDFs, and images (via OCR). If you've conducted hundreds of customer interviews over months or years, Evernote lets you find the exact moment a customer mentioned a specific pain point, even in an attached PDF.

**Best For:** Researchers managing large volumes of customer interview data. If your work involves keeping organized archives of interview notes across multiple projects or clients, Evernote's search is hard to beat.

**Key Strengths:**

* Advanced full-text search across thousands of notes

* [Enterprise security features](https://evernote.com/security/teams-features) including SSO, role-based access, and data recovery

* Cross-platform sync

* Document scanning and attachment handling

* Evernote encrypts data in transit and at rest

**Possible Limitations:** No native transcription of audio or video calls. You can save notes and record audio memos, but you won't get automatic transcripts. Expensive paid plans are required for advanced features. The limited free version restricts uploads and device access. Formatting and collaboration are less structured than Notion for team-based research.

### 6. Apple Notes

Apple Notes is the simplest tool on this list. It does fewer things than everything else here, but it does them with zero friction if you're inside the Apple ecosystem.

**Why It Stands Out:** Apple Notes integrates seamlessly across Apple devices with voice recording. You can start a note on your iPhone during an in-person interview, add voice notes, handwrite observations with an Apple Pencil on iPad, and review everything on your Mac later. Instant sync, no setup process required.

**Best For:** Apple users conducting in-person customer interviews. If you're doing face-to-face conversations and need to capture quick notes or voice memos without pulling out a laptop, Apple Notes works.

**Key Strengths:**

* Voice recordings attached directly to notes

* Handwriting support with Apple Pencil

* Instant sync across all Apple devices

* Privacy under Apple's encryption policies

* Scan documents and extract text with OCR

**Possible Limitations:** Apple ecosystem only. No windows app, no Android support. Limited collaboration features compared to Notion or Google Workspace tools. You can't build structured notes with databases or custom fields. For international teams that mix device platforms, this is a non-starter. If you're trying to create notes with structured data, you'll outgrow Apple Notes fast.

### 7. Obsidian

Obsidian is a markdown-based note taking app that stores everything locally on your device. It's free and open source for personal use, with paid options for sync and publishing. Obsidian is best for deeply linked personal knowledge bases.

**Why It Stands Out:** Links customer insights across interviews to reveal patterns. Bidirectional linking lets you connect a quote from Interview #3 to a theme identified in Interview #17. The [graph view](https://techrepublic.com/article/obsidian-review/) visualizes connections between notes, helping you spot patterns you'd miss in a linear note system. This is in depth analysis territory.

**Best For:** Research teams building comprehensive customer knowledge bases. If you're running a multi-month research program and need to surface themes across dozens of interviews, Obsidian's linking system is powerful.

**Key Strengths:**

* Knowledge graph visualization

* Powerful bidirectional linking between notes

* Obsidian uses local markdown files for privacy, giving you full control of your data

* Extensive plugin ecosystem for custom workflows

* No vendor lock-in since files are standard markdown

**Possible Limitations:** Steep learning curve. Initial setup to configure vaults, plugins, and workflows takes real time investment. No built-in transcription, live transcription, or audio capture. All interview content must be imported from other tools manually. You can't use it as a real time transcription tool during calls. It's not built for team collaboration out of the box.

AI note-takers can handle multiple languages for transcription, but Obsidian doesn't transcribe at all. You'll pair it with a transcription tool and use Obsidian purely for analysis and knowledge mapping.

### 8. Google Keep

Google Keep is for quick notes and nothing more. It's the digital equivalent of a physical notebook with sticky notes, but synced to your Google account.

**Why It Stands Out:** Fastest note capture with voice transcription. Google Keep lets you capture [voice notes that are automatically transcribed](https://support.google.com/keep/answer/2888246?co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS&hl=en) into text on mobile. Labels, color-coding, and reminders help you organize follow ups. It integrates with Google Workspace, so your notes connect to google docs and other apps you already use.

**Best For:** Quick customer feedback collection and follow-up reminders. If you need to capture a few key insights immediately after a conversation, Google Keep gets it done with zero friction.

**Key Strengths:**

* Voice notes with automatic transcription

* Location-based and time-based reminders

* Google Workspace integration (google docs, google meet, google calendar)

* OCR on images extracts text

* User friendly interface, zero setup process

**Possible Limitations:** Transcription quality is approximate. Users report accuracy drops for longer recordings and multi-speaker conversations. Formatting is minimal, so you can't build structured notes or databases. Not suitable for complex customer interviews where you need to capture lengthy transcripts. Collaboration features are basic. If you're doing serious research, Google Keep is a supplement, not a primary tool.

VoiceToNotes is another option in this quick-capture category. VoiceToNotes has over 1 million users according to platform data. VoiceToNotes is free and supports real-time transcription. VoiceToNotes.ai exports notes to all major platforms, making it a viable alternative if Google Keep's formatting limitations bother you.

## How to Choose the Right Interview App

### Choose Based on Interview Format

Your interview format determines which core features matter most.

**Remote video calls (Zoom, google meet, microsoft teams).** You need ai powered interview notes with automatic transcription. Otter.ai and Fireflies.ai are the clear picks. Both auto-join calls and handle the recording so you're free to focus on the conversation. Otter.ai integrates with Zoom and Google Meet for live transcription.

**Phone calls without video.** Fireflies supports dialer integrations, but setup takes time. For phone-based expert calls, you may need a separate audio recordings solution paired with a transcription tool.

**In-person interviews.** Apple Notes or a physical notebook. You want minimal friction. Pull out your phone, hit record, take quick notes. You're not going to have a bot joining an in-person meeting.

**Face-to-face with follow-up analysis.** Record with your phone's voice memo, then import into Obsidian or Notion for tagging and linking. This two-step workflow adds time but gives you the deepest analysis capability.

### Choose Based on Team Size and Collaboration Needs

**Solo researchers.** Otter.ai on its free plan or Google Keep for quick notes works fine. You don't need team collaboration features or CRM integrations. Save your own notes, review them, move on.

**Small teams (2-5 people).** Notion is the best balance of structure and collaboration. You can share interview databases, assign follow ups, and track research progress without per-seat costs eating your budget.

**Larger teams or enterprise.** Microsoft OneNote if you're already on Office 365. Fireflies.ai if your team needs CRM integration for sales-driven customer research. Enterprise security requirements typically narrow your options.

### Choose Based on Integration Requirements

**CRM integration for sales teams.** Fireflies.ai wins. It connects to Salesforce, HubSpot, Dynamics 365, and more. Transcripts, action items, and ai notes flow directly into deal records.

**Research tool integration for product teams.** Notion connects to Slack and Google Drive. Obsidian works with local files and exports to most formats. Superpowered deletes audio immediately after transcription for privacy while supporting integrations with Notion, Google Drive, and Salesforce.

**Enterprise security and compliance.** Evernote and OneNote both offer enterprise-grade security controls. Obsidian stores data locally, which sidesteps cloud privacy concerns entirely. Fireflies claims SOC 2 Type II compliance and states meeting data isn't used to train AI models.

## Which App Is Best for You?

Direct recommendations based on your role:

**Choose Otter.ai** if you conduct frequent remote customer calls and want ai powered transcription without managing a complex tool. It handles meeting notes, speaker identification, and ai summaries in one place. Just review the transcripts for accuracy before sharing.

**Choose Notion** if you run systematic customer research programs. It won't transcribe your calls, but it's the best place to organize all the notes from your interviews efficiently, link findings to themes, and share structured notes with your team.

**Choose Fireflies.ai** if you're on a sales or customer success team and need interview data flowing directly into your CRM. The automated follow ups and action item tagging save hours of manual work after each call.

**Choose Microsoft OneNote** if you're in an enterprise Office 365 environment and need a tool that doesn't require new vendor approval. It's free, flexible, and meets enterprise compliance requirements.

**Choose Obsidian** if you're building a long-term knowledge base from future interviews and want to discover patterns across dozens or hundreds of conversations. The setup time pays off when you're doing in depth analysis over months.

**Choose Google Keep** if you need quick customer feedback capture and reminders for follow ups. It's not for complex interviews, but it's the fastest way to save notes after a casual customer conversation.

For job interviews and hiring managers on hiring teams evaluating candidates, several of these tools apply. Otter.ai handles the transcription while Notion can track candidate evaluations. The same principles apply: match the tool to your decision making process.

## Final Thoughts

Good notes are the first step. What separates useful customer interviews from wasted time is what happens after, the analysis, pattern recognition, and action you take on the key insights you've gathered. AI features speed up transcription and summarization, but they don't replace the judgment needed to turn valuable insights from customer conversations into product decisions or investment theses.
