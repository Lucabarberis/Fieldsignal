## What is an expert network in practice?

An expert network is a firm that helps you gain access to specialized knowledge from subject matter experts who have relevant expertise in a company, market, technology, regulation, or buyer segment. In the 2000s, hedge funds used expert consultations mainly for channel checks. Today, private equity firms, growth equity, corporates, founders, consultants, institutional investors, and strategy firms use networks for primary research across various industries.

Firms like GLG, AlphaSights, Guidepoint, Third Bridge, and FieldSignal source, vet experts, schedule calls, run surveys, manage compliance, and help clients turn perspectives into insights. Most expert networks now operate with a global expert network footprint, even when they focus on niche sectors or regions.

* Former operators and former executives explain pricing, sales motion, churn, product quality, margins, and execution risk.

* Ex-customers, customers, and channel partners explain buying criteria, satisfaction, switching costs, and vendor gaps.

* Regulators, compliance leaders, doctors, medical researchers, and former regulatory officials help assess healthcare policy, drug development, medical devices, and regulated markets.

* Software engineers, enterprise architects, and former competitors help tech companies and investors evaluate emerging technologies, machine learning adoption, and competitor landscapes.

## Compliance and legal risk: how serious networks manage it

Compliance concerns are grounded in real history. The 2009–2011 US insider-trading investigations involving expert networks pushed the industry toward tighter controls, stricter documentation, and better training.

* Ensure all interactions follow strict MNPI, or Material Non-Public Information, prevention protocols when dealing with industry specialists who may be privy to sensitive data.

* Top expert networks implement rigorous compliance standards that limit the exchange of information, safeguarding both clients and experts from potential risk.

* Leading expert networks use a combination of AI-driven search, referrals, and direct outreach for sourcing and vetting experts, which includes background checks and conflict-of-interest screening.

* Expert networks adhere to ISO, GDPR, and industry-specific standards for data privacy and compliance, ensuring confidentiality agreements and compliance training are standard practices.

* Serious networks use restricted topic screening, call scripts, pre-call attestations, conflict checks, employer policy review, and documented approvals before each consultation.

* Reputable expert network companies reduce risk compared with informal back-channel LinkedIn outreach, which often lacks contracts, audit trails, training, and MNPI controls.

* GLG, Guidepoint, AlphaSights, Third Bridge, and FieldSignal maintain institutional-grade compliance infrastructure, even though pricing and positioning differ.

## Pricing models: retainers vs pay-per-use

Pricing is often the main friction point for smaller PE firms, boutique consultancies, and early-stage founders considering expert networks. Many teams need expert calls now, but don't want a six-figure annual contract before they know whether the provider can deliver.

* Annual retainers: best for high-volume users with predictable demand, but they can lock smaller teams into spend before match quality is proven.

* Call credit banks: you pre-purchase credits and draw them down as you schedule calls.

* Pay-per-call: you pay only for actual calls. Public pricing guides commonly place standard 60-minute calls around $300 to $1,500+, depending on seniority, geography, rarity, and industry.

* Project-based fees: useful for surveys, panels, structured interview programs, leadership assessment, and tailored market research.

* Opaque vs transparent pricing: opaque markups make it hard to plan research budgets for due diligence, strategy projects, and M&A work.

* FieldSignal's model: transparent pay-per-use pricing, pass-through call costs, and no mandatory annual retainer. That makes premium access available to firms priced out of GLG- or AlphaSights-scale commitments.

* Pilot first: start with 3–5 calls, compare relevance, turnaround, notes quality, compliance flow, and whether the insights helped build conviction.

## How to choose the right expert network for your next project

Use this checklist before you send an RFP. The goal isn't to find the biggest network. It's to find the right expert, fast, with clean compliance and usable output.

* Speed: how quickly can the provider return qualified expert profiles? Expect 24–72 hours for common profiles and longer for narrow technical roles.

* Vetting depth: does the provider verify background, recency in role, conflicts, and direct relevance, or just search a database?

* Expert quality: prioritize operator experience, recent responsibility, and a direct line of sight to your questions.

* Coverage: does the provider have global expert network reach in your sector, geography, and buyer segment?

* Transcript library: can you use past calls and structured notes to avoid repeating research?

* Compliance: are experts trained, screened, contracted, and restricted from discussing confidential data?

* Pricing: are fees, honoraria, markups, and minimums clear before work starts?

* Fit: if your work is niche or competitive intelligence-heavy, a boutique network like FieldSignal can outperform volume-first firms by custom sourcing former employees, customers, and channel partners.

Test providers with a live brief. Compare match quality side by side, not marketing claims.
