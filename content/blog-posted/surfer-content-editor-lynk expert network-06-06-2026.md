## Hong Kong Asia-Pacific Expert Network with Payment and Operational Challenges

Lynk, formerly known as Straits Network, was founded in 2015 by Peggy Choi, who had a private equity background. Lynk positions itself between traditional expert networks like GLG, AlphaSights, Third Bridge, Guidepoint, Coleman Research, and newer knowledge platforms built around faster matching, online workflows, and broad expert access.

The company’s pitch is simple: connect clients with the right experts quickly so teams can turn specialized knowledge into actionable insights. Expert networks facilitate access to specialized knowledge for strategic decision-making, and companies employ expert networks to gather qualitative data for market analysis, due diligence insights, competitive intelligence services, and market research.

Lynk offers three main services: Lynk Answers, Lynk Circles, and Lynk Infinity.

* **Lynk Answers** connects users to over 840,000 knowledge workers through expert interviews, consultations, and surveys.

* **Lynk Circles** launched in summer 2022 for organizing advisor networks.

* **Lynk Infinity**, an expert network aggregator, launched in February 2023.

Lynk connects over 840,000 knowledge workers globally, and Lynk has over 840,000 experts in its database. Public materials also cite a global network across more than 80 countries and a reported footprint of 8 global offices.

On the client side, Lynk collaborates with over 500 enterprises across 20 industries. Lynk serves 500+ enterprises across 20+ industries, and Lynk serves over 500 enterprises across 20 industries.

## How Lynk's Process Works

Lynk’s process follows the standard expert network model: find the expert, screen the expert, schedule the call, verify the work, and process payment.

### Step 1: Expert Recruitment via LinkedIn

Lynk identifies and approaches experts through LinkedIn, referrals, associations, partnerships, and other online channels. Experts may be called knowledge workers, knowledge partners, members, consultants, or subject-matter experts.

The outreach usually focuses on the expert’s background, sector expertise, current or former role, geography, and ability to answer a client’s research question. AI-driven matching improves the accuracy of connecting clients with relevant experts, and Lynk’s platform uses its knowledge system to match experts based on more than a job title.

Lynk offers flexible opportunities for knowledge workers. Lynk's platform allows knowledge workers to access various project engagements, including calls, surveys, and other paid research activities.

The promise is income for sharing knowledge. Experts often set or confirm a rate, then engage only if the project is relevant. In public posts, some experts mention compensation expectations around $250-400+ per consultation, although rates vary by person, market, seniority, and project type.

### Step 2: Client Consultation Setup

Once a client posts a project, Lynk searches its database and wider network for relevant experts. Matching criteria include industry, geography, customer exposure, years of experience, and specific knowledge tied to the client’s question.

The client may receive profiles, screen candidates, and select the right experts. Consultation formats include calls, interviews, focus groups, surveys, and other flexible engagement formats. Flexible engagement formats in expert networks include consultations and fractional roles, though fractional work depends on the provider and project design.

Preparation should include:

1. A clear research question.

2. A screened expert profile.

3. Compliance checks.

4. Confirmed payment and tax details.

5. A call guide that avoids confidential information.

### Step 3: Payment Processing Issues

Lynk’s written payment process has been described as a 60-75 working day payment verification process after a completed call or project. Payment depends on consultation verification, call duration confirmation, correct banking details, and payment method setup.

That timeline is already long compared with many expert expectations. The bigger issue is that public expert reports describe delays extending 6+ months, with some cases reported as 3 months, 6+ months, or over 1 year.

Payment methods referenced in Lynk materials include Payoneer and PayPal, with payments made in local currency or USD depending on the expert’s location. Errors in payment or tax information can cause delays, but many public complaints describe delays even after experts say their details were correct.

If you’re a client, this matters because unpaid experts churn. If experts stop trusting the network, the quality of the network drops, the best people ignore invitations, and your project takes longer.

## Major Red Flags with Lynk Operations

The main risk with Lynk isn’t the idea. The idea is sound. Expert networks exist because companies need fast access to knowledge they don’t have internally.

The red flags are operational.

* **Systemic non-payment complaints**: Public reports describe non-payment issues affecting large numbers of experts.

* **Long payment delays**: Reported delays range from 3 months to over 1 year.

* **Weak communication**: Experts describe support replies that say the issue is being checked internally, without actual resolution.

* **Broken payment dates**: Some consultants report repeated promised dates that pass without money arriving.

* **Fraud accusations**: Multiple unpaid consultants have used fraud language in public complaints. That doesn’t prove legal fraud, but it’s a serious trust signal.

* **Operational instability**: Public directory coverage has referenced layoffs and senior departures in 2022 and 2023.

* **Funding gap**: Operational issues appear despite reported funding above $30M.

The funding history is material. Lynk raised $1 million in 2015 from Wavemaker Partners. Lynk secured $4 million in Series A funding in April 2017.
