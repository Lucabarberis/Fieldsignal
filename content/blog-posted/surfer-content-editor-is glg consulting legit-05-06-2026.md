* Recruiters at GLG often use cold messaging strategies to reach potential experts. Those "GLG Consulting" outreach messages on LinkedIn are usually real GLG associate sourcing, not scams. But impersonation and sloppy recruiting practices have caused confusion.

## What Is GLG Consulting and How Does It Work?

GLG, formerly Gerson Lehrman Group, is one of the oldest and largest expert networks in the world. Founded in 1998 in New York City, the company serves investors, consultants, and corporations who need fast access to domain expertise.

* GLG connects clients to subject matter experts for one-on-one expert calls, surveys, and other small consulting projects. GLG offers services like 1-on-1 consultations and B2B surveys, typically priced on an hourly rate model at the client side.

* GLG offers one-on-one consultations lasting 30 to 60 minutes. The company also provides clients with transcripts and AI-generated summaries to support research workflows.

* From the client's angle: you submit a brief, GLG associates source experts, you approve profiles, then you pay per expert call or survey. Many clients operate under an annual or multi-month "seat" agreement.

* From the expert's angle: you're recruited via LinkedIn or referrals, complete compliance training, set your own hourly rate, accept or decline expert network calls and surveys, then receive payment after engagements. Experts on the GLG platform can set their own hourly rates.

* GLG sits in the expert network category alongside AlphaSights, Third Bridge, Guidepoint, Tegus, Coleman Research, and others. Decisions to use GLG typically come from research, strategy, or investment teams doing due diligence or market analysis.

## Is GLG Consulting a Scam or Just High Friction?

GLG is not a scam. It's a well-known firm with compliance controls, institutional clients, and offices around the world. But some outreach and expert experiences can feel spammy or disorganized.

* GLG consulting outreach often comes from junior associates sending high volumes of LinkedIn messages. These can look suspicious, especially when profiles seem sparse or generic. That doesn't mean the outreach is fake. It means the sourcing process is high-volume and sometimes sloppy.

* In some documented cases, experts have suspected fake recruiter profiles or phishing. Always verify email domains (e.g., @glginsights.com) and avoid sharing sensitive information or personal data outside official channels.

* Some experts worry that GLG "sells your expertise to competitors." Expert networks, including GLG, routinely pitch the same experts to multiple investment or consulting clients. That's standard for the expert network model, not a scam.

* The most frequent "scam-like" complaints relate to time wasted on screeners or unpaid cancellations, not outright theft. The company still exists, has offices, and pays many experts on schedule.

* If a message claims to be GLG consulting but uses free email domains, requests bank details before any expert call is scheduled, or won't reference GLG's proper site, treat it as impersonation rather than GLG itself.

## How GLG Projects, Expert Calls, and Consulting Rates Work

Common GLG project formats include 1:1 expert calls, online surveys, small consulting projects, and occasional site visits or events. All are centered on short, focused interactions with qualified industry practitioners.

* GLG expert calls usually run 30 to 60 minutes. GLG charges clients $1,000 to $2,000 per hour for expert consultations, while the expert receives only a portion of that hourly rate. GLG offers consulting rates that can exceed $1,000 per hour.

* Expert compensation bands vary. Early-career professionals might see $75 to $150 per hour. Experienced operators often earn $250 to $300 per hour. Narrow specialists sometimes reach $500 or more per hour. GLG is generally conservative above that range.

* GLG pays by the minute for expert calls. Overrun time is compensated. But short calls reduce earnings, which matters when consultants block out larger time windows for clients that cancel.

* GLG facilitates paid surveys that typically pay $40 to $80. Experts can earn $40 to $70 for 15-minute surveys. These capture broader views from technology, healthcare, or other industry practitioners at scale, complementing in-depth expert calls.

* From the buyer's side, pricing is often embedded in annual contracts that blend seats, expert calls, and sometimes access to transcript libraries and AI tools. This can obscure the true per-call hourly rate and make it hard to evaluate cost per consultation.

## Cost Transparency: GLG Consulting Rate vs Other Expert Networks

The biggest complaint you'll hear from mid-market funds and smaller corporates isn't that GLG is fake. It's that pricing is opaque and skewed toward large, repeat buyers.

* GLG prefers annual subscriptions or bulk-hour commitments. There's limited public disclosure of hourly rate ranges. Internal "soft caps" on expert honoraria preserve GLG's margins.

* [Informed industry commentary](https://www.nextyn.com/post/expert-network-pricing-guide-2026) often cites GLG client-side rates in the $1,000 to $2,000 per hour band. AlphaSights and Guidepoint charge clients $800 to $1,600 per hour, depending on sector and contract structure.

* Experts see only their own honorarium (e.g., $300 per hour) and not the full client rate. That's where margin and effective hourly rate gaps appear. Many experts have been paid hundreds of dollars per call without knowing the client was billed multiples of that.

* GLG offers a wide range of services including surveys and consultations, but bundling them into annual contracts can make it impossible to isolate what you're paying for each project type.
