## Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) Basics Every Research Buyer Needs To Know

The Foreign Corrupt Practices Act was enacted in 1977 to prohibit bribery of foreign officials for business purposes. It was amended in 1988 (adding two affirmative defenses) and again in 1998 to align with OECD Anti-Bribery Convention commitments. The FCPA applies to conduct worldwide for U.S. companies, and it's the core foreign corrupt practices law that affects how you pay and interact with international experts.

The corrupt practices act FCPA has two pillars:

1. Anti-bribery provisions: prohibit providing anything of value to foreign officials to influence them or gain an improper business advantage. The FCPA prohibits bribing foreign officials for retaining business, winning contracts, or securing regulatory approvals.

2. Books-and-records and accounting provisions: companies must maintain accurate books and internal controls. The securities and exchange commission and the Department of Justice co-enforce these requirements. Willful falsification or omission in records triggers liability.

"Anything of value" is interpreted broadly. It covers honoraria, travel reimbursement, meals, providing gifts, jobs, political contributions, charitable donations, and even research incentives if given with corrupt intent. Payments must be transparent and reflect fair market value.

The definition of "foreign official" is equally broad. It includes employees of governments, but also employees of "instrumentalities," meaning entities controlled by a government that perform a public function. After the [Esquenazi decision (11th Circuit, 2014)](https://natlawreview.com/article/doj-and-sec-update-fcpa-resource-guide-2020-what-s-new-and-what-does-it-mean), state-owned enterprises, government hospitals, public universities, and regulators all qualify. These people often appear as "industry experts" in research projects.

Key reference points for in-house counsel: the [DOJ and SEC Resource Guide to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/fcpa-resource-guide), second edition (July 2020), is the authoritative overview. The fifth edition of the FCPA Handbook (2022) is also widely used by compliance professionals and directors.

Under the Biden administration, anti corruption became a core national security priority. The [2021 U.S. Strategy on Countering Corruption](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2021/12/us-announces-formal-strategy-to-counter-corruption) formalized this. Recent FCPA enforcement trends focus on national security interests and individual accountability. That means research-related misconduct involving foreign officials draws real scrutiny from prosecutors and authorities.

## Why FCPA Compliance Matters In Market and Competitive Research

Your day-to-day workflows are closer to FCPA risk than you think. Expert calls, customer interviews, channel checks, and M&A due diligence across markets like China, Brazil, and the Middle East all involve people who may be foreign officials under the law. If you're conducting research on behalf of your fund, firm, or company, you're responsible for how those interactions are structured.

Common research targets that trigger risk: physicians at national health systems (UK NHS, for example), engineers at state-owned utilities in Latin America, professors at public universities, telecom regulators in APAC, or administrators at government hospitals. Under the instrumentality doctrine, even institutions that look private can qualify if they serve public functions and are controlled in part by government.

High-risk research contexts include:

* Pre-IPO commercial diligence on a state-owned telecom

* KOL interviews at government-funded hospitals

* Vendor or supplier checks where licenses depend on a regulator

* Market entry work in sectors heavy with SOEs

* Any project involved with public tenders or procurement

FCPA violations often occur through intermediaries such as agents and consultants. Enforcement examples routinely involve failures in third-party oversight, vague consulting contracts, and undocumented "marketing" payments. All of those patterns rhyme with sloppy expert calls or consulting-style surveys that lack proper documentation.

Penalties are severe. FCPA violations can lead to significant civil penalties and disgorgement. Penalties for FCPA violations can exceed 1 billion dollars for multinational enterprises. High-stakes prosecutions are a priority for FCPA enforcement under updated DOJ guidelines. Poor documentation of research honoraria and scope can be used against you in an SEC investigation or internal investigation.

A documented, repeatable compliance program for research vendors makes it much easier for a GC or CCO to sign off on your project. This is especially true during active deals, where any allegation of illicit payments can kill a transaction.

## Designing an FCPA-Safe Research Compliance Program

Compliance with the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act requires a proactive, risk-based approach. Here's how to build or tighten your compliance program around international research and expert interviews. Effective compliance programs can prevent illicit conduct before it happens, and compliance programs help detect issues at the earliest stage.

### 1. Risk Assessment

Classify every research project by three dimensions:

* Country risk: use a public index like [Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2023) to flag jurisdictions.

* Sector risk: SOE-heavy industries (energy, telecom, healthcare in many countries) carry more risk than private-sector software.

* Counterparty risk: is the person you're interviewing employed by a public institution, a state-owned enterprise, or a regulator? Flag accordingly.

This risk assessment determines how much vetting and approval you need before a call starts.

### 2. Written Policies

Establishing clear policies regarding gifts and travel helps ensure FCPA compliance. Your anti corruption policies should include:

* Clear definitions of "foreign official" and "instrumentality"

* Caps on honoraria benchmarked to fair market value in each jurisdiction

* Approval workflows for travel, meals, and any other benefits

* A ban on success-based or percentage-of-deal payments tied to foreign officials

* Rules for implementing procedures around charitable donations and political contributions that could be seen as influence

### 3. Expert Vetting

Verify identity, employer, and any government affiliations. Screen for government titles, positions at state-owned enterprises, and political exposure. Record this in a central log. Vetting foreign agents and consultants requires detailed contractual agreements to ensure compliance, even when those "agents" are research experts on a one-hour call. AI is increasingly used for third-party vetting and transaction auditing in FCPA compliance, and it's worth integrating automated screening tools where your budget allows.

### 4. Compensation Controls

Payments must reflect fair market value for time spent. Pay only the expert directly, never route money through third-party accounts. Document everything: timestamped call records, survey logs, contracts, and invoices. Companies must maintain accurate books and records under the FCPA. Mischaracterizing research honoraria as "consulting fees" or "marketing expenses" is exactly how books-and-records violations get triggered.

### 5. Content Controls

Questions cannot solicit confidential government information, non-public tender details, or commitments to "help win bids" in exchange for fees. For calls involving regulators or SOE employees, use pre-approved question sets reviewed by counsel. Keep the scope tight and avoid letting discussions drift into consulting roles or future business arrangements.

### 6. Training

Regular employee training is a best practice for FCPA compliance. Every person on your team who commissions or conducts expert calls should understand what constitutes FCPA risk. Use real case studies. Make researchers aware of red flags: requests for cash, third-party payment routing, vague scope, or resistance to documentation.

### 7. Audit and Monitoring

Periodic internal audits of research projects catch problems early. Review payments, contracts, and transcripts. Whistleblower protections and secure reporting channels are important for FCPA compliance, so give your team includes a clear way to escalate concerns without fear of retaliation. Corporate compliance programs are also integrating anti corruption efforts with data privacy regulations, so make sure your audit procedures address both.

Establishing a culture of compliance is crucial for preventing FCPA violations. Even smaller funds and growth-stage companies should codify these steps into a written research compliance memo to show investors, partners, and regulators that they take foreign corrupt practices seriously.

## FCPA Due Diligence in M&A and Investment Research

Transaction-related research is where FCPA and due diligence collide. You're probing targets in high-risk markets while also paying third parties for insights. Conducting thorough third-party due diligence is critical for preventing FCPA violations. FCPA due diligence must be thorough before acquisitions.

### Pre-Acquisition Sequence

Follow this sequence for pre-acquisition FCPA diligence tailored to research:

1. Scope the information you need: financial, operational, regulatory. Define what's in bounds for expert interviews and what isn't.

2. Screen who might be a foreign official. Anyone involved with the target's regulatory approvals, licensing, or government contracts should be flagged.

3. Select compliant research channels. A vetted vendor like FieldSignal or a comparable expert network with documented FCPA controls.

4. Document findings. Archive transcripts, call logs, payment records, and expert profiles for counsel review.

Pre-acquisition due diligence should assess third-party relationships, agent contracts, and any history of contact with foreign officials relevant to your investment thesis. Run background checks on founders and key managers, especially where government permits, concessions, or procurement contracts are critical to revenue.

Expert calls used for commercial due diligence must not drift into offering future roles, consulting contracts, or "success fees" to individuals who influence licensing, regulatory approvals, or state procurement. If an expert has decision making authority over a process your target depends on, that call needs extra scrutiny.

At a high level, your contracts should include anti corruption reps and warranties, audit rights, and remediation plans if fcpa issues are found. Companies can self disclose to DOJ and SEC consistent with the [FCPA Corporate Enforcement Policy](https://www.justice.gov/d9/pages/attachments/2020/08/20/fcpa-guide-2020_print-full-downloadable.pdf), which can lead to declination or reduced penalties. The DOJ encourages post-acquisition compliance audits as soon as practicable. Taking steps to audit a newly acquired entity fast signals good faith to prosecutors.

## Practical Checklist: Running an FCPA-Compliant Research Project This Quarter

This is a tactical, copy-pastable checklist. If you're a PE associate, corp dev analyst, or founder launching an international research sprint, follow these steps before your first call.

1. Define research scope and target countries. Write it down. If you're covering China, Brazil, or the Middle East, you're in high-risk territory.

2. Flag sectors with likely foreign officials. Oncology KOL work in UK NHS hospitals, power-grid engineers at Latin America SOEs, regulators in APAC telecom licensing, administrators at government research institutions. All require extra screening.

3. Clear the project with internal legal or compliance. If you don't have a GC, get outside counsel involved for projects touching government-linked experts.

4. Pick a vetted vendor. Use FieldSignal or a comparable expert network with documented FCPA compliance infrastructure. Don't run ad hoc outreach over LinkedIn to government-linked contacts.

5. Set written honoraria limits. Benchmark what similar experts in similar jurisdictions charge. Avoid wildly high fees that suggest payment for influence.

6. Approve question sets. For any call touching regulators, procurement, licensing, or state entities, have compliance or counsel approve the questions in advance.

7. Brief your internal team on what not to ask. No questions about non-public tender details, confidential regulatory data, or commitments to help win bids.

8. Archive transcripts and payment records. Every call should have a transcript or recording, a signed engagement letter, an invoice, and proof of payment. This is how you maintain accurate books.

9. Log any red flags for counsel review. If an expert requests payment to a different person, asks for cash, or offers to influence a government process, stop the call and escalate.

Effective fcpa compliance isn't about avoiding international research. It's about running it with procedures that hold up under securities enforcement scrutiny.
