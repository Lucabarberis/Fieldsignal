## How Tech Due Diligence Consulting Works

The technical due diligence process is structured to deliver actionable insights within tight deal timelines. Technical due diligence typically takes 2 to 6 weeks to complete, depending on scope and complexity. Here's how it works.

### Step 1: Define Scope and Timeline

Every engagement starts with scoping. You define the key areas for assessment: software architecture review, codebase analysis, security and regulatory compliance audit, technical debt quantification, scalability evaluation, and engineering team maturity.

Standard engagements run 2 to 3 weeks for mid-market deals. Lite assessments, focused on critical risks like security and infrastructure, can wrap in 1 to 2 weeks. Complexity drivers include the number of services and languages in the tech stack, the presence of regulated data (HIPAA, PCI-DSS, GDPR), AI/ML components, and how clean the target's documentation is.

A comprehensive review during technology due diligence focuses on software architecture and cybersecurity. Technical due diligence assesses architecture, codebase, and infrastructure. The scope should specify access level, deliverables, risk categories, and remediation estimates.

### Step 2: Expert Matching and Access

The right diligence partner connects you with former CTOs, engineering leads, and senior architects from companies at similar stages or in the same sector (SaaS, fintech, healthtech).

Assessing a partner's technical expertise is crucial for effective due diligence. Experts are vetted for relevant experience, compliance credentials, and conflicts of interest. They sign NDAs and IP ownership protocols before accessing the data room. Early access to code, architecture documentation, and management teams is critical.

Quality TDD partners provide actionable insights, not just checklists. You want practitioners who've built and scaled the same kinds of systems the target operates.

### Step 3: Technical Assessment and Reporting

This is where the value gets created. Experts conduct:

* **Code quality review** – Measures maintainability and technical debt. A good TDD provider uses tools like SonarQube for code analysis alongside manual review.

* **Architecture analysis** – Evaluates monolith vs. microservices, cloud setup, API dependencies, and modularity. Technology due diligence assesses the scalability of a target's technology to support growth.

* **Security posture evaluation** – Identifies vulnerabilities and compliance gaps, including OWASP Top 10 vulnerabilities, encryption standards, logging, and incident response. GDPR non-compliance can lead to significant monetary penalties. Technology due diligence examines compliance with data protection laws such as GDPR and CCPA.

* **Data governance assessment** – Checks compliance and security practices. Mismanaged data strategies can lead to lost customer insights.

* **Team execution capability** – Evaluated for engineering maturity, including CI/CD practices, testing discipline, documentation, monitoring, and turnover risk.

* **Technical debt quantification** – Consultants identify technical debt that may affect long-term technology sustainability, with cost estimates for remediation.

Deliverables include a risk-rated report, a remediation roadmap with prioritized items, and cost estimates. Consultants help adjust acquisition prices based on findings from technology assessments. The process aids in planning for post-merger integration of IT systems.

Over 40% of third-party dependencies may require major updates, a finding that routinely surfaces during code reviews and directly impacts resource allocation for post-close integration.
