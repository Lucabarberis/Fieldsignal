# Tegus vs Stream: Expert Call Platforms Compared

Tegus and Stream are expert network platforms for institutional investors and corporate strategists. Both platforms allow booking direct consultations with industry experts and offer searchable transcript libraries, but they differ in pricing, content depth, and how they fit into a broader research ecosystem. Tegus is the more established player with a massive transcript archive. Stream is designed as an AI-powered expert call transcript library that benefits from AlphaSense's AI-driven capabilities for analysis.

Here's how they compare on the key factors that matter for your research workflow.

## Tegus vs Stream: Key Expert Network Differences

Tegus and Stream both serve investment professionals who need expert insights, but their business model, scale, and technology differ meaningfully.

* Tegus is known for its extensive searchable library of expert call transcripts and live expert calls. For a quick overview, it combines qualitative primary research with quantitative financial data. Tegus was founded in 2016 in san francisco and later based in Chicago. It was acquired by AlphaSense in June 2024 for $930 million and is now part of AlphaSense's broader intelligence platform.

* Stream focuses on a vast searchable database of expert call transcripts. Stream transcripts benefit from AlphaSense's AI-driven capabilities for analysis, and streaming expert transcripts integrates with AlphaSense's financial data and filings.

* Tegus uses a transparent subscription-based model for accessing transcripts, with annual contracts starting at $20,000. Stream's pricing structure is less publicly documented but operates within AlphaSense's platform.

* Both platforms facilitate specialized knowledge transfer from subject matter experts to clients, but Tegus has the larger standalone content library and longer track record.

Expert networks generate over $2 billion in annual revenue. Competitors include GLG, AlphaSights, and Guidepoint, all competing for the same pool of investment research budgets. Tegus also expanded with Series B backing from oberndorf enterprises and willoughby capital.

## Target Audience and Use Cases

Both Tegus and Stream target professionals who need real world experience and industry knowledge delivered through expert interviews and transcripts. The overlap is significant, but the way each platform fits into your workflow depends on your research needs.

### Tegus Target Market

Tegus serves hedge fund analysts, PE firms, and institutional investors conducting due diligence on companies and specific industries. Both Tegus and Stream are heavily utilized by investment firms and private equity. Tegus offers over 200,000 expert interviews covering 25,000 companies. Typical users include:

* Investment analysts building financial models who need expert insights on competitive landscapes and market dynamics

* Corporate strategy teams doing competitive analysis before M&A transactions

* Consultants working on consulting projects who need primary qualitative data fast

* Growth equity professionals running sector scans across multiple companies

Tegus excels in sourcing niche experts for consultations. If you need to speak with a former VP of Sales at a mid-market SaaS company, Tegus's expert matching is strong.

### Stream Target Market

Stream offers expert interviews primarily with former executives, customers, and industry leaders. Its target market overlaps heavily with Tegus: investment professionals, corporate strategists, and research teams who want instant access to expert perspectives without scheduling live calls.

Stream's integration with AlphaSense makes it appealing to teams already using AlphaSense for SEC filings, broker research, and market intelligence. If your team is inside AlphaSense's platform, Stream's transcripts are accessible without switching tools.

## Business Model, Pricing Models and Cost Structure

Pricing is where these platforms diverge most, and where your budget determines which option makes sense.

### Tegus Pricing Structure

Tegus subscriptions start at $20,000 per year. Here's what the cost structure looks like:

* Annual subscription fees starting at $20,000 to $25,000 per user for basic access

* Clients pay $550 per call if published to all customers

* Tegus charges a $75 markup on expert consulting fees

* Consultant fees typically range from $100 to $500 per hour, with rates shifting at the point where niche expertise or limited expert supply drives pricing higher

* Enterprise contracts for larger teams with full access can exceed $150,000 per year

* Multi-year contracts and higher seat counts unlock volume discounts

That's a meaningful upfront commitment. For a small PE team running two to three consulting projects a year, the math gets tough to justify unless you're also using the transcript library heavily, and buyers should expect total pricing to combine transcript access with separate live-call fees.

### Stream Pricing Structure

Stream's pricing isn't publicly listed in the same detail. Because it operates within the AlphaSense platform, access likely comes bundled with an AlphaSense subscription or as an add-on module. Key points:

* Pricing is likely subscription or usage-based, tied to AlphaSense platform access

* No publicly available per-call or per-transcript pricing

* The bundled approach may offer better value if you already pay for AlphaSense

* Cost depends on your overall AlphaSense contract scope and team size

If you're comparing total cost, factor in what you're already spending on AlphaSense. If you're not an AlphaSense customer, Stream's pricing includes that platform cost too.

## Compliance and Security Requirements

Compliance isn't optional in expert network research. Both platforms address this, but the regulatory frameworks differ slightly based on use case.

### Tegus Compliance Framework

Tegus operates under financial research regulations. Its compliance team handles:

* SEC and FINRA compliance for expert calls and published transcripts

* MNPI (material non-public information) safeguards, including pre-call screening and post-call transcript review

* Expert background verification and conflict of interest disclosure

* Embargo periods for transcripts involving private companies

* Publicly available information standards enforced across the entire process

Tegus's compliance infrastructure is mature. The company has handled thousands of calls, and the process for screening experts is well-documented.

### Stream Compliance Framework

Stream's compliance requirements align with AlphaSense's broader platform standards:

* Financial research compliance through AlphaSense's existing framework

* Expert screening and MNPI protections

* Data encryption and secure transmission protocols

* Access controls and audit trails for regulatory oversight

The compliance experience is comparable. Neither platform is meaningfully weaker here. Competitive intelligence includes expert consultations and industry trend analysis, and both platforms protect against the legal risks that come with that process.

## Geographic Reach and Scalability

### Tegus Global Presence

Tegus is primarily US-focused but has a growing international expert network. The platform covers 25,000+ companies, many of them global, but expert recruitment is concentrated in major financial centers. If you need a tegus expert on a European industrials company, you'll likely find relevant transcripts. Deep research on emerging markets may require more effort.

### Stream Global Presence

Stream benefits from AlphaSense's global infrastructure. AlphaSense serves clients across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific, and Stream's transcript database reflects that reach. For teams doing cross-border diligence, the combined platform offers broader geographic coverage without needing separate contracts.

## Integration and Workflow Compatibility

### Tegus Workflow Integration

Tegus integrates with AlphaSense for broader research capabilities post-acquisition. For teams already using Tegus, the workflow looks like:

1. Search the transcript library for relevant expert calls

2. Schedule a live expert call if existing transcripts don't answer your specific questions

3. Review compliance-screened transcripts after calls

4. Export data points into your financial models or investment memos

The process requires some coordination. Expert scheduling, compliance review, and transcript turnaround add time. But the quality of insights from a well-matched expert call is hard to replicate with reports or data alone.

### Stream Workflow Integration

Stream's integration with AlphaSense places it within a professional services-oriented research workflow, so users access transcripts alongside SEC filings, broker research, and company data in one platform. The workflow:

1. Search across transcripts, filings, and reports simultaneously

2. Use AI-driven analysis to surface relevant information

3. Book direct consultations with industry experts when transcripts aren't enough, with scheduling and expert matching handled through the platform

4. Combine qualitative insights with quantitative data in the same interface

If your team already uses AlphaSense, adding Stream transcripts is straightforward. There's no separate login, no separate search, no separate payment.

## Tegus vs Stream: Which Platform Should You Choose?

Choose Tegus if you need the largest standalone transcript library, strong expert matching for niche sectors, and live expert calls as a core part of your research process. Tegus offers a library of over 35,000 client call transcripts from custom calls alone, with 100,000+ total transcripts as of April 2024. It's the stronger choice for teams that run heavy primary research programs and want control over expert selection.

Choose Stream if you're already inside AlphaSense and want expert transcripts integrated with financial data, filings, and broker research. Stream's value is in the integration, not necessarily in having a larger or different transcript library. Expert networks connect clients with industry experts for insights, and Stream does this within a platform you may already use.

Neither platform is ideal if you're a smaller firm, early-stage fund, or boutique consulting team that can't justify a $20,000+ annual commitment. Both Tegus and AlphaSense (which houses Stream) are priced for institutional buyers.
