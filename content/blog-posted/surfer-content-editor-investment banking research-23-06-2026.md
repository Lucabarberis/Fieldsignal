# Investment Banking Research: A Practical Workflow Guide

Investment banking research powers every major deal decision. If you don't define your hypothesis, gather public and private data, and validate with experts, you're flying blind. Structured workflows, proper tooling, and the right primary sources separate winning deals from costly missteps.

Master the process that turns raw data into actionable insights.

## Investment Banking Research for Every Deal Type

Different transactions demand different research approaches. Your focus, depth, and sources shift depending on whether you're running M&A due diligence, prepping an IPO, or advising on strategy.

### M&A Due Diligence Research

M&A is where investment research gets the most granular. You need industry dynamics, competitive positioning, and a clear evaluation of the target's financial performance. That means segment-level revenue, EBITDA margins, customer concentration, and unit economics.

Management team background checks and reference calls matter here. Expert consultations provide actionable insights from industry insiders, including former employees and suppliers who've worked directly with the target. Consultations can enhance due diligence for investment firms by surfacing risks that don't show up in financial statements, like customer satisfaction erosion or margin pressure from suppliers.

Research analysts evaluate industries and conduct in-depth analysis across the value chain to assess how market and operating findings affect business decision-making in the deal. For a PE firm targeting an industrial manufacturer, that includes supplier cost inputs via interviews, plant utilization metrics, and regulatory approval timelines. Analysts track economic trends and competitive landscapes to build a full picture.

### IPO, Equity Research, and Capital Markets Research

IPO prep requires growth trajectory validation: transaction volumes, regulatory licences, market sizing. Sell-side research provides actionable insights for investors and helps build institutional momentum. Analysts produce reports with recommendations on specific securities, and their coverage acts as a driver of market efficiency.

Peer company valuation comparisons, trading multiples (EV/EBITDA, P/E, PEG), and institutional investors' sentiment all feed the pricing decision. Analysts help identify potential M&A targets and build buzz for IPOs, which is a critical component of the capital markets process. Financial modeling forecasts a company's future earnings and valuation, giving underwriters and issuers the evidence they need.

### Strategic Advisory Research

Strategic advisory work covers market entry opportunities, partnership identification, and competitive response scenarios. Market entry strategies include exporting, franchising, and joint ventures, and successful market entry requires understanding local consumer behavior. Regulatory compliance is crucial for entering new markets, alongside sustainability considerations that can shape long-term attractiveness and operating requirements.

Market research can reduce risks associated with new market entry. You're looking at local regulation, competitor positioning, consumer preferences via surveys, distribution networks, and partner options. Timing is essential for successful market entry strategies, and the competitive landscape influences investment banking strategies significantly.

## Choose the Right Research Approach for Your Timeline

Research depth depends on deal urgency and budget. Here's how the three standard approaches compare:

| Dimension         | Fast-Track (1-2 weeks)                                                   | Comprehensive (4-8 weeks)                                                                           | Ongoing (Quarterly updates)                                   |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Data depth        | Mostly secondary: public filings, analyst reports, targeted expert calls | Heavy primary research: multiple expert interviews, customer/supplier surveys, private company data | Trend monitoring, peer updates, risk tracking, news flow      |
| Industry coverage | Top-level positioning, major competitors only                            | Full mapping: niche players, emerging markets threats, regulations, supply chain                    | Seasonal shifts, regulation updates, competitive moves        |
| Deliverables      | High-level decks, key risks, valuation range estimate                    | Detailed models, sensitivity analysis, full diligence report                                        | Dashboard updates, portfolio risk flags, peer-multiple shifts |
| Cost              | Low                                                                      | High                                                                                                | Moderate ongoing                                              |
### Fast-Track Research (1-2 weeks)

When the deal timeline is compressed, focus on readily available public information and databases first. Use screening tools to identify deal-breaking issues and critical success factors. Conduct targeted expert interviews for immediate industry insights. Prioritize the questions that could kill the deal.

### Comprehensive Research (4-8 weeks)

This is the standard for serious M&A and growth equity transactions. Multiple rounds of expert consultations across the value chain. Primary customer and supplier surveys for validation. Detailed competitive benchmarking with scenario analysis and sensitivity modeling across key variables like growth rate, margin erosion, discount rate, and customer churn.

### Ongoing Research (Quarterly updates)

Portfolio companies need continuous coverage. Regular expert panel calls for industry developments. Customer satisfaction tracking. Competitive intelligence gathering. This is how buy-side research teams stay ahead of trends and risks without scrambling before each board meeting.

It's strong for transaction comparables and fundamental analysis of private companies, but data on very small firms remains sparse.

## How to Structure Your Research Process

Systematic workflows prevent information gaps. Here's the process that works.

### Research Planning and Hypothesis Setting

1. Define key questions that will inform the investment or strategy decision. Example: "Is the target's revenue growth durable given regulatory headwinds?" or "What multiples do public peers trade at, and are they justified by growth and margin?"

2. Create a research timeline with milestones and deliverable checkpoints.

3. Identify must-have data points versus nice-to-have insights.

4. Set budget parameters for external research vendors and expert fees.

Investment firms often require due diligence insights for strategies, and the hypothesis stage determines whether you spend your budget on the right questions. A research team that skips this step can waste weeks chasing data that doesn't inform the decision, especially when a technical or sector-specific topic would have benefited from input from a professor to sharpen the initial hypothesis.

### Data Collection, Validation, and Risk Management

1. Start with public sources (filings, databases, industry reports) to establish baseline understanding.

2. Conduct expert interviews to validate assumptions and identify blind spots.

3. Cross-reference findings across multiple sources. For example, a supplier interview for cost structure should align with audited cost data.

4. Document source quality and potential biases. Private company figures are often unaudited. Experts' recollections carry bias. Quantitative research from public filings may be restated.

AI is reshaping equity research in investment banking, pushing analysts to work in a new world of AI-assisted research workflows. Investment banks are focusing on AI delivery over hype, using [tools like o11.ai](https://o11.ai/industries/finance/investment-banking/) to automate repetitive tasks: data pulls, comparable sheets, deck templates. But analysts remain responsible for judgment, hypothesis formation, and risk management. Research benchmarking tools like BankerToolBench and [BigFinanceBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11304) evaluate AI agents on standard IB tasks and show current models still miss key criteria.

### Fundamental Analysis and Synthesis

1. Create standardized templates for consistent data organization across deal types.

2. Build scenario models (baseline, bull, bear cases) with sensitivity analysis for key variables.

3. Identify investment risks and mitigation strategies: earn-outs, indemnities, holdbacks, warranty clauses.

4. Prepare executive summaries with actionable recommendations.

Use frameworks like Porter's 5 Forces and concentration metrics (HHI Index) for competitive structure. For valuation, run public comps, precedent transactions, and DCF for long-term forecast of private or IPO targets.

Post-MiFID II unbundling forced buyers to pay explicitly for research, reducing reliance on sell-side equity research and changing cost expectations. This means your research budget needs clear deliverables and outcome specifications.

Copper prices have been volatile due to macroeconomic risks. Demand for summer travel remains strong despite rising gas prices. The U.S. shipbuilding industry is experiencing a multi-decade decline. These are the kinds of trends across sectors, commodities research, and economics that your analysis needs to capture when they're relevant to a deal thesis.

## Execute Your Research Strategy Efficiently

Smart execution maximizes insight quality while controlling costs and timelines.

1. Begin with targeted expert screening calls to validate research direction before committing to full-scope engagement.

2. Use pay-per-project research services for flexible capacity scaling. You don't need a global team on retainer for a single deal.

3. Maintain compliance protocols for material non-public information. Expert networks must enforce eligibility vetting, conflict checks, and pre-call briefings. [Legal risks](https://legalclarity.org/what-are-expert-networks-definition-uses-and-legal-risks/) emerge when expert calls yield MNPI. 4. Build reusable research frameworks for similar future transactions. Templates for customer retention matrices, revenue mix analysis, and margin sensitivity save weeks across your deal pipeline.

5. Layer primary perspectives onto your public data models. Expert insights on customer behavior, management quality, and operational risks are the difference between a good model and a conviction call.

Your research process should develop over time. Each deal teaches you which data sources, methods, and expert types deliver the most value for your sectors and regions.
