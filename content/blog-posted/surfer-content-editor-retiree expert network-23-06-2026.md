## Taxes, Social Security, and Retirement Planning Considerations

Expert network income is treated as self-employment or miscellaneous consulting income. Retirees must set aside money for income taxes and self-employment taxes (15.3% for Social Security and Medicare combined) on net earnings.

* If you claim Social Security before full retirement age (FRA), the [2026 annual earnings limit is $24,480](https://www.ssa.gov/cola/factsheets/2026.html?utm_source=openai). For every dollar earned above that, SSA withholds $1 for every $2 over the limit.

* In the year you reach FRA, the threshold rises to $65,160, with $1 withheld for every $3 over. After FRA, there's no earnings limit at all.

* Substantial expert network income may push you into higher tax brackets or trigger Medicare IRMAA surcharges. This matters for personal finance and long term care planning.

* Coordinate expert network income with IRA withdrawals, Required Minimum Distributions, pension income, and insurance costs as part of a broader retirement planning strategy. Talk to a tax advisor once your annual consulting income exceeds $20,000.

## Compliance: Staying On The Right Side Of The Law

Expert networks enforce strict compliance to prevent insider trading. Regulation Fair Disclosure was implemented in 2000 to enhance compliance across the securities industry. Insider trading investigations around 2009 to 2011 forced the expert network industry to build rigorous, standardized compliance programs.

* Retirees cannot disclose: material non-public information, confidential customer data, trade secrets, or anything obtained under NDAs or employment agreements. Expert networks verify consultations to ensure no confidential information is shared.

* Typical compliance setup: training modules, written policies, pre-call attestations, and call monitoring or spot checks. This is similar in rigor across GLG-tier firms and boutiques like FieldSignal.

* 81% of investment professionals view expert consultations as compliant with regulatory standards. Expert networks spend millions on compliance outreach to companies whose employees or former employees participate.

* You participate in a personal capacity. Never speak on behalf of your former employer. Prepare a "cheat sheet" of topics you can and can't discuss. Use clear, direct language in your compliance documentation.
