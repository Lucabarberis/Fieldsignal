## History, structure, and relationship with FTI Consulting

Compass Lexecon traces its roots to two separate firms. [Lexecon was founded in 1977](https://www.compasslexecon.com/about/our-story) in Chicago by economists and legal scholars including Richard Posner, William Landes, and Andrew Rosenfield. Dan Fischel and Dennis Carlton joined early, establishing its strength in law, economics, and industrial organization. Competition Policy Associates, known as COMPASS, was founded in 2003 in Washington, DC by Margaret Guerin-Calvert, Janusz Ordover, Jonathan and Peter Orszag, and Robert Willig, all distinguished economists with government and academic experience.

FTI Consulting acquired Lexecon in 2003 and COMPASS in 2006. Compass Lexecon was [formed in January 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_Lexecon) as a unified economic consulting platform under the FTI umbrella. The combination brought together two firms with complementary strengths: Lexecon's securities and financial litigation expertise and COMPASS's competition economics and regulatory work. FTI also acquired the Princeton Economics Group to add further depth in valuation and financial analysis.

Within FTI's corporate structure, Compass Lexecon is positioned as the specialist economic consulting unit. It's not a generalist management consultancy or a restructuring advisory practice. FTI Consulting is a public company (NYSE: FCN) with thousands of employees and billions in annual revenue. That backing gives Compass Lexecon infrastructure, office space, and administrative support across multiple continents.

Despite this corporate relationship, clients and courts consistently treat Compass Lexecon as a standalone brand. Its reputation, leadership, and expert credibility are its own. When a judge or arbitrator evaluates testimony from a Compass Lexecon expert, they're assessing the economist's individual track record and methodology, not FTI's balance sheet. The firm is headquartered with offices in major legal and financial centers, and its brand recognition in economic consulting stands independent of its parent company.

### Bankruptcy and financial distress litigation

Compass Lexecon performs solvency analyses, valuations in restructuring, and expert work in disputes around fraudulent conveyance or preference actions. This practice covers event studies, loss causation, market efficiency, and damages modeling in securities fraud, insider trading, and market manipulation cases. Securities and financial markets work involves analyzing market manipulation and damages to determine whether alleged misrepresentations had a statistically significant effect on stock prices. ## How Compass Lexecon works with clients and law firms

A typical engagement starts when counsel identifies the need for economic consulting and approaches Compass Lexecon to scope expert testimony and analysis. The firm works almost exclusively through law firms on litigation, though it also takes direct mandates from corporations and government clients in regulatory proceedings.

The process follows four stages:

1. Initial conflict check and engagement letter. Roles, rates, scope, and confidentiality terms are established.

2. Data intake and early economic scoping memo. The team reviews data sources, defines legal questions (market definition, causation, damages), and proposes initial modeling approaches.

3. Modeling, financial analysis, and draft reports. Experts develop event studies, regression models, counterfactuals, and valuations. Draft expert reports are prepared with exhibits.

4. Deposition, trial, or hearing testimony. The expert defends their analysis under cross-examination. Models may be revised, exhibits updated, and responses to opposing expert reports prepared.

Compass Lexecon teams interact closely with litigation teams throughout. There's iterative refinement of models and hypotheses. Experts are peer-reviewed internally. Discovery demands are handled in coordination. In cross-border matters, offices in the US, Brussels, London, and other locations coordinate to handle multiple agency reviews or international arbitration proceedings across jurisdictions.

Fees are typically hourly or project-based. A senior managing director or chair-level expert bills at very high rates. Analysts and junior economists bill much lower. Costs ramp quickly as cases approach trial because of more modeling revisions, detailed exhibit prep, and potential mock trials.

Economic consulting can materially shape case strategy. It quantifies damages exposure, tests legal theories against data, and identifies economic risks regulators will focus on. In the ExxonMobil case, early price-impact work led to the exclusion of certain claims before trial, narrowing the scope of liability. That's the kind of value Compass Lexecon provides when the stakes justify the spend.
