## What is competitive intelligence? (in 1 minute)

Competitive intelligence is the legal and ethical collection and analysis of information about competitors, customers, and market intelligence to support specific decisions. A strong competitive intelligence program uses a systematic process: define the question, collect relevant information, analyze it, and distribute actionable insights to product, sales, corporate development, and leadership.

Competitive intelligence is narrower than market intelligence. Market intelligence might ask, “How big is the segment?” Competitive intelligence asks, “Which competitor is entering my segment in 2026, how are they pricing, and how should I respond?” Competitive intelligence enhances strategic planning by providing market insights, and effective CI supports informed decision-making in strategic planning.

The main components are:

* Planning questions tied to key objectives

* Collection from competitor websites, customer reviews, filings, surveys, expert calls, and proprietary databases

* Analysis of competitor moves, market trends, customer sentiment, and potential threats

* Distribution through briefs, battlecards, pricing memos, and board materials

* Regular reviews, because regular reviews maintain confidence in CI data quality

Modern CI programs blend software with primary research. Competitive intelligence tools, ci tools, and ci platforms automate competitor tracking and insights delivery. Expert networks like FieldSignal add human context when secondary research isn’t enough.

### 1. Dynamic airline pricing during demand shocks

During the 2021 to 2023 travel recovery, airlines had to adjust routes, fares, and ancillary pricing daily. Revenue teams tracked rival fares, seat load factors, aggregator search demand, and low-cost carrier promotions.

airBaltic used dynamic ancillary pricing for seat assignments and reported a 6% revenue increase in ancillary seat revenue, according to [PROS](https://pros.com/learn/case-studies-testimonials/how-airbaltic-driving-seat-revenue-with-ai-powered-dynamic-ancillary-pricing?utm_source=openai). Fetcherr’s airline pricing work has also reported more than 10% revenue lift in airline pricing use cases.

How you’d copy this in B2B:

* Track competitor pricing, discounting, and contract terms

* Watch when rivals change package limits or fees

* Give the sales team negotiation playbooks before renewal calls

* Use continuous improvement to refine pricing guardrails every month

### 2. Retail price-matching against Amazon and big box stores

From 2018 to 2025, Amazon, Walmart, Target, and marketplace sellers made algorithmic repricing normal. Smaller e-commerce brands copied the pattern with alerts for key SKUs.

The CI inputs are simple:

* Competitor product pages

* Promo emails

* Marketplace APIs

* Weekly circulars

* Review volume and stockout signals

Monitoring competitor pricing structures ensures products remain competitive and profitable. It also reduces “you’re too expensive” objections because reps know where price parity exists and where premium positioning needs proof.

### 3. Fast-food product wars: chicken sandwiches and limited-time offers

The 2019 chicken sandwich wars showed how quickly product teams react to customer demand. Popeyes, Chick-fil-A, McDonald’s, and others watched social media buzz, menu launches, sell-out patterns, and foot traffic signals.

Marketing teams used this CI to develop messaging that directly addressed competitor claims. Product teams used it to time limited-time offers and decide which flavors, bundles, or price points deserved investment.

The B2B SaaS parallel is clear:

* Watch competitor release notes

* Track customer reactions on Reddit, G2, and LinkedIn

* Compare launch timing against your product roadmap

* Decide which new feature matters now and which one can wait

### 4. Automotive feature copycats and safety standards

In the 2010s and 2020s, automakers tracked Tesla and other EV makers on battery range, ADAS features, charging access, and over-the-air updates. They gathered CI from patent filings, NHTSA and Euro NCAP documentation, supplier announcements, spec sheets, auto shows, and teardown reports.

This intelligence shaped fast-follow moves, such as adding standard driver-assistance packages or extending warranty terms. It also shaped long-term R&D bets around emerging technologies and supply chain constraints.

For B2B teams, use the same lens:

* Track integration partners

* Monitor certifications and performance benchmarks

* Watch hiring for specialist roles

* Compare competitor positioning against your roadmap

Monitoring competitor job postings predicts future product development because hiring often shows where resources are going before launches appear.

### 5. Airbnb vs hotels: mining customer reviews for unmet needs

Between 2010 and 2020, Airbnb and hotel chains learned from online reviews on Airbnb, Booking.com, TripAdvisor, and Google. Text mining helped classify complaints about noise, cleanliness, Wi-Fi, fees, workspace quality, and cancellation policies.

A study of 169,666 London reviews found that review themes changed by season and year, giving operators a clearer view of guest expectations. You can read the research summary from the [University of Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/analysing-online-reviews-to-investigate-customer-behaviour-in-the/?utm_source=openai).

This CI revealed customer preferences and market gaps for innovative product development. Hotels responded with clearer fees, better flexible cancellation, stronger web photos, and more work-friendly amenities.

For SaaS and B2B tools, the same process works on G2, Capterra, Reddit, support transcripts, and sales call notes. CI helps product teams identify customer needs and drives product development with outside-in thinking.

### 6. Streaming wars: subscription pricing and content bets

From 2019 to 2024, Netflix, Disney+, Max, and Amazon Prime Video used CI to study content slates, licensing deals, ad-supported tiers, regional pricing, and quarterly earnings disclosures.

S&P Global reported that US ad-free streaming tiers saw about 7.7% CAGR pricing growth from 2020 to 2026, while entry-level tiers rose about 4.6%. The same analysis put average entry-level monthly pricing near $10.77 by early 2026 and ad-free tiers above $16.

These signals informed ad tiers, password-sharing enforcement, family plans, and content budgets. The Harvard Business School case on Netflix pricing frames these trade-offs around churn, subscriber growth, and competitor actions.

A SaaS company can do the same by tracking:

* Free-tier limits

* Bundle changes

* Renewal language

* Add-on packaging

* Competitor price increases

CI helps companies identify emerging trends and customer preferences before those preferences show up in churn.

### 7. B2B SaaS win/loss analysis for deal-level intelligence

From 2022 to 2025, many mid-market SaaS companies formalized win/loss analysis across deals involving 3 to 5 main competitors. Win/loss analysis reviews why customers chose competitors or the company’s products during sales cycles.

Product marketing and sales ops gather competitive intelligence by:

* Interviewing closed-lost prospects

* Interviewing customers who evaluated multiple vendors

* Mining CRM notes and call recordings

* Tagging themes like price, feature gaps, security, implementation, and trust

The output is practical: battlecards, pricing guardrails, and a clear “why we win, why we lose” framework. Sales enablement tools like battlecards help sales teams handle competitor objections effectively, and CI helps sales teams improve win rates by providing competitor insights.

### 9. Enterprise product roadmap bets informed by ex-employee insights

A Fortune 1000 product team planning a 2026 roadmap might need to know which competitor priorities are real and which are slideware. Former PMs, engineers, and solution consultants can explain architecture limits, renewal tactics, implementation quality, and internal KPIs.

This isn’t asking for confidential information. Ethical CI ensures compliance with legal standards, and ethically sourced CI builds trust with executives, customers, and partners. Avoiding reputational risks is crucial in CI practices, so topic boundaries, attestations, and audit trails matter.

This type of CI shapes:

* Feature sequencing

* Integration choices

* Build-versus-partner calls

* “Must-have” items for the next 12 to 24 months

For technical buyers, CI may also connect to software delivery. Development teams compare static code analysis practices, unit tests, integration tests, automated testing, bug fixes, code changes, and release quality. They also look at whether a rival’s version control system, continuous integration, continuous delivery, and continuous deployment practices support reliable enterprise releases across existing systems.

### 10. Sales teams using CI battlecards in live deals

A 50-person sales team competing against 3 vendors in 2024 needs battlecards inside the CRM, not buried in a folder. A ci team or product marketing analyst monitors release notes, pricing updates, security docs, RFP answers, and customer complaints.

Good battlecards keep everyone on the same page. They help reps counter objections, compare implementation risk, and suggest deal structures that fit the buyer’s procurement process.

### 11. Market entry decisions using multi-source intelligence

A European SaaS company entering the US mid-market manufacturing segment in 2025 needs more than a TAM chart. It needs competitor penetration by region, channel partner strength, pricing benchmarks, compliance requirements, switching costs, and buyer language.

Teams gather this intelligence through:

* Public data and secondary research

* Localized surveys

* Distributor interviews

* Former regional sales leaders

* Customer interviews

The decision output is a go/no-go call, first ICPs, target audience definition, early packaging, and messaging. CI enables marketing teams to refine go-to-market strategies effectively, and companies using CI can adjust marketing strategies based on competitor actions.

This is where competitive intelligence work supports emerging market opportunities, market share goals, and a practical business strategy.

### 12. Tracking regulatory and policy shifts before competitors react

From 2022 to 2026, fintech and payments companies tracked PSD2, PSD3, open banking rules, regulator consultation papers, competitor compliance announcements, and bank pilot programs.

Legal, product, and business development teams turn this CI into:

* Updated contracts

* Compliance-ready sales collateral

* Partner priorities

* Product requirements

* Risk memos for leadership

Expert interviews with former regulators, compliance officers, and industry associations help stress-test the read. CI provides insights into competitor activities and industry trends, and CI enables businesses to make informed strategic decisions before regulation becomes a scramble.

A simple ci process works like this:

1. Define 3 to 5 key objectives.

2. Pick sources, including market research, competitor analysis, and expert interviews.

3. Collect for 2 to 4 weeks.

4. Synthesize into a one-page brief, battlecard, or market memo.

5. Review impact and iterate in the final phase.

CI methods include competitor analysis and market research. Analyzing competitor activities and market trends aids in strategic planning and risk mitigation. Understanding the competitive environment allows firms to streamline processes and improve efficiency. CI helps companies anticipate market shifts and capitalize on opportunities.
