## What BrainTrust is: from blockchain talent network to research tool

BrainTrust started in San Francisco in 2018 as a user-owned alternative to platforms like Upwork. It focuses on high-skill freelancers and enterprise projects. The platform enables organizations to build flexible teams for project needs across engineering, AI, UX design, and operations roles.

The core idea is shared ownership. Talent and clients jointly own the network through the BTRST cryptocurrency token, which governs incentives, fees, and some platform decisions. Professionals on BrainTrust keep 100% of their market rate with zero fees. BrainTrust eliminates traditional recruiting agency overhead by charging clients a flat 10% platform fee instead.

A [Harvard Business School case](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=62815) (Case 823-039, published August 2022, revised February 2023) documents the business model and early traction. Clients can connect with over 2 million vetted professionals. The AI talent pool exceeds 2 million vetted professionals across software, data, and creative skills. BrainTrust facilitates the compliance and onboarding of professionals for enterprises, and expert-led courses are available for professional development through BrainTrust Academy.

While BrainTrust is built primarily for project work, some people use it as an informal expert network to get short, focused calls with practitioners, especially in software, AI, and product operations. That's a meaningful distinction from how a trusted expert network operates.

## How BrainTrust's model works (and why it's different from GLG-style expert networks)

BrainTrust is structurally different from classic expert networks. It runs as a marketplace on blockchain rails with a token incentive system, not a centrally curated bench of experts. Here's how the process works:

1. Companies post projects with role, skill, and rate parameters.

2. Talent apply or are matched through algorithmic and profile-based search.

3. Both sides transact in fiat. BTRST token rewards influence the fee structure and community incentives.

4. Expert vetting includes structured interviews and skills assessments. Only about 2% of applicants are admitted.

5. Vetting processes involve automated and human scoring methods. Among accepted talent, 68% have 7+ years of experience.

AI reduces hiring bias and accelerates screening by 80%. Expert consultations can reduce hiring time by 80%, and AI can accelerate time-to-hire by 80% across similar systems. The BTRST token is an ERC-20 governance and incentive token. It introduces crypto price volatility that a traditional expert network user wouldn't normally handle.

By default, traditional expert networks like [GLG](https://glginsights.com/compliance/), AlphaSights, Guidepoint, and Third Bridge operate differently. They run request-led expert search with compliance screening before calls and standard hourly honoraria paid in fiat. Compliance infrastructure ensures regulatory adherence in expert networks at this tier.

BrainTrust doesn't pre-vet "experts" for M&A, PE/VC, or corporate strategy work. If you're trying to run due diligence through BrainTrust, you must handle your own screening, compliance checks, and conflict vetting. That's a lot of added responsibilities for your team.

## When BrainTrust is a good fit for research, and when it isn't

Treat BrainTrust as a talent marketplace first. It's only a quasi-expert network in very specific situations.

**Good fit:**

* You need an engineer or product manager to build a quick prototype and talk you through user data. User research is crucial before writing code for products.

* You want to understand how a specific SaaS tool is implemented by hands-on admins and inspect real production configurations.

* You're testing AI workflows and need someone to both implement and explain. Agile principles help teams adapt product roadmaps effectively. Data-driven insights enhance product development strategies.

* You're a founder continuing to validate a concept over weeks or months. Expert consultations help validate product-market fit for startups.

**Poor fit:**

* Pre-LOI commercial due diligence on a private company.

* Competitive intelligence where you need ex-employees of a specific target.

* Structured customer satisfaction interviews with consistency across a panel.

* Leadership assessment of target executives.

* Anything touching MNPI or regulated information.

BrainTrust talent profiles aren't curated around "ex-employee of X" or "former VP of Y." You'll spend more time manually searching, messaging, and screening to find people with the right company and role history. Businesses can innovate products to meet market demands, and continuous efficiency leads to profit growth in product planning, but that's a different problem than sourcing expert calls.

For founders and operators: BrainTrust is useful for getting hands-on advisors for longer sprints. But if you need 5 to 15 independent expert calls to support a board presentation or market entry, a dedicated expert network is faster and safer. Market entry strategies include exporting, franchising, and joint ventures. Successful market entry requires understanding local regulations and culture. This kind of work, whether you're looking at Canada or any other geography, demands a network that can source relevant agents and partners with specific experience.
