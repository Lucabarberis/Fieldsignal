## Key Concepts: Information Barriers, Ethical Walls, And Chinese Walls

These three terms overlap in practice. Here's how they differ in origin:

* **Information barriers** are the general concept of restricting two way communication and information access between specific groups. Information Barriers prevent unauthorized communication between departments.

* **Ethical wall** (sometimes called an ethics wall) is the modern, preferred term in finance and law for a formalized barrier used to avoid conflicts of interest. Ethical walls are used to maintain confidentiality in financial institutions.

* **Chinese wall** is the legacy term, still widely referenced in financial regulation and the expert network industry, though many firms now avoid it in official policies for cultural sensitivity reasons.

In expert networks, all three refer to the same practical goal: preventing misuse of confidential information or privileged information. These barriers also appear in tools like microsoft teams, where technical controls restrict communication and prevent communication between internal groups.

## Regulatory Context: Why Expert Networks Need Information Barriers

Regulators treat expert networks as potential vectors for insider trading. In the US, sharing material non public information triggers criminal and civil penalties under [Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 of the Securities Exchange Act](https://legalclarity.org/expert-networks-and-insider-trading-risks-and-penalties/). The sarbanes oxley act and the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act further strengthened the need for information barriers across financial services. In the UK, the FCA enforces the Market Abuse Regulation, and in 2026 [fined and banned an adviser for trading on a draft trading update](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-investigations/out-of-sight-not-out-of-scope-uk-fca-fines-and-bans-individual-for-insider-dealing) before public release.

Expert network compliance ensures adherence to applicable laws and regulations. Compliance frameworks prevent disclosure of material non public information and help mitigate legal risks. Regulatory audits assess expert network compliance indirectly. Information Barriers help comply with regulatory requirements in various sectors. Investment advisers and day traders alike face regulatory actions if they trade on non-public data. ## How Information Barriers Work Inside An Expert Network

A compliant expert engagement follows a controlled flow from intake to debrief. Here's how it works:

1. Client submits a project request with defined scope.

2. Compliance reviews scope, filtering out prohibited topics and potential conflicts.

3. Experts are vetted. Expert vetting verifies the credentials and employment status of industry professionals. Pre-call vetting ensures experts can legally share industry insights without disclosing confidential data.

4. Expert onboarding includes background verification and conflict assessments. Expert networks implement strict onboarding for experts to ensure compliance with confidentiality, including non disclosure agreements and contractual obligations.

5. Pre-call steps: written guidelines, conflict disclosures, explicit prohibitions against sharing material non public information.

6. During the call, engagement monitoring ensures compliance during expert consultations. Call monitoring intervenes if conversations enter prohibited topics and helps identify compliance issues in expert networks.

7. Post-call: client engagement records support rigorous audit trails and regulatory compliance. Detailed record-keeping of client-expert interactions supports compliance and auditing.

## Information Barriers Across Industries Using Expert Networks

Many industries using expert networks already operate with their own ethical wall requirements.

* **Investment management:** Portfolio managers and analysts use expert calls while remaining behind firm-level barriers that separate research from trading. Investment decisions are made only on public information.

* **Corporate strategy and M&A:** Deal teams maintain confidentiality around potential acquisitions. They handle sensitive information in virtual data rooms and ensure experts have no current conflicts before engagement.

* **Technology and healthcare:** Unreleased product roadmaps, security vulnerabilities, and clinical trial data carry severe consequences if leaked. There are ethical considerations specific to each sector's regulations.

* **Law firms and consultancies:** External advisers must comply with their own client confidentiality and conflict rules. When one school of thought favors strict separation, other schools may allow limited, controlled use, but the expert network's policies must accommodate both approaches.

## Managing Conflicts Of Interest For Both The Experts And Clients

Conflicts of interest create both legal and reputational risk. Ethical behavior is crucial for maintaining trust in expert networking.

Expert-side conflicts include working at a direct competitor, holding equity in companies being discussed, or board roles tied to the topic. Client-side conflicts include using calls to test deal rumors, gain an edge on earnings, or pressure current vendors. Users on both sides must flag potential issues early.

FieldSignal runs conflict checks through expert questionnaires, ongoing certifications, and project-level screening. Transparent communication is a best practice in expert networking. ## Designing An Ethical Wall For Your Own Use Of Expert Networks

If you're a PE associate, corporate strategy analyst, or founder, here's how to formalize your internal approach:

1. **Assign ownership.** Your legal or compliance lead should approve or block all expert network projects. Their expertise and authority are crucial.

2. **Write policies.** Define off-limits question types, expert selection criteria, and how notes or transcripts are stored. Cover what happens if material non public information is suspected.

3. **Use existing walls.** If your firm separates research from trading, or M&A from public company IR, extend those to define who sees which project materials. Even departments with limited overlap should have clear boundaries.

4. **Add technical controls.** Restricted folders, access logging, retention limits. If you use Microsoft Teams, enable information barriers between specific groups.
