# Industry Analysis: Frameworks, Examples, and How to Apply Them

Hedge fund research lives or dies on getting the right industry analysis fast, with compliance built in. Generic feeds don’t create edge. The work that matters connects public data, alternative data, and expert calls to one investment decision.

## At a Glance: How Hedge Funds Use Industry Analysis Today

Top hedge funds use industry analysis to turn commoditized data into competitive advantage. The same filings, sell-side notes, and consensus estimates reach everyone. The edge comes from sharper questions and better primary research.

Strong hedge fund research combines:

* Fundamental analysis, including financial statements, management strength, and competitor disclosures.

* Quantitative analysis, where mathematical models and alternative data find market patterns and anomalies.

* Macro analysis, covering global economic indicators, geopolitical events, rates, currencies, and demand.

* Expert networks that provide access to niche industry insights from independent industry practitioners.

FieldSignal focuses on primary expert insight for this process. You get pay-per-use access, no annual retainer, no minimum commitment, and pass-through expert honoraria.

## What “Industry Analysis” Means for Hedge Funds

Industry analysis is the structured study of market size, growth, profitability, new regulations, and competitive dynamics around a thesis. It’s not reading broker research and calling it diligence.

Hedge funds use this work differently than corporates or long-only investors. Hedge fund managers use it to underwrite shorts, special situations, event trades, and macro themes. Complexity and leverage are key characteristics of hedge fund strategies, so research must identify risks early.

Hedge funds are lightly regulated and do not disclose their asset positions or strategies publicly. That raises the value of original research. Hedge fund research involves deep investigation and analysis of financial assets, and it helps maximize returns while identifying and mitigating financial risks.

A typical team includes fund managers, analysts, a PM, and an investment committee. The analyst builds the industry deck, the PM sets decision triggers, and the committee tests sizing. Expert networks, filings, channel checks, and data vendors all sit inside the investment process.

## Core Industry Analysis Frameworks Hedge Funds Actually Use

Analysts usually combine two or three frameworks per idea.

Market sizing and growth math:

* Formula: customers × usage × price × penetration.

* Example: US telehealth estimates put the market near $43.8B in 2024, with forecasts toward $135.2B by 2034, according to [Market.us](https://market.us/report/us-telehealth-market/?utm_source=openai).

* Use it to test whether growth is already priced in.

Unit economics and value chain analysis:

* Break revenue per unit, cost per unit, gross margin, sales cost, and renewal rates.

* Map suppliers, operations, customers, and distribution.

* Use it in healthcare, software, industrials, and portfolio companies.

Competitive intensity:

* Track competitors, price discounting, CAC, churn, win/loss, and substitution risk.

* Adapt Porter’s Five Forces, but make it investment-focused.

Regulatory and policy mapping:

* Track approvals, taxes, subsidies, licensing, enforcement, and policy events.

* This is critical in financial markets, gaming, energy, and emerging markets.

## H3-level Frameworks in Practice: From Idea to Industry View

### Example Framework: US Telehealth in 2026

The big picture is normalization after COVID. Payers pushed back on reimbursement during 2024 to 2026, while listed names such as Teladoc and Amwell faced slower growth and margin pressure.

A hedge fund analyst models visits, reimbursement rates, clinician cost per visit, no-shows, churn, and platform costs. The goal is a view on sustainable margins, not just revenue growth.

Verify through expert calls:

* What reimbursement changed from 2023 to 2025?

* Are payer contract renewals tightening?

* What are cancellation and no-show rates?

* Is provider churn rising?

* What cross-state licensing risk remains?

FieldSignal can connect funds with former payer executives, clinicians, and revenue cycle leaders.

### Example Framework: European Online Sports Betting

The market is fragmented across the EU and UK. From 2018 to 2024, operators faced tighter advertising rules, KYC, AML, bonus restrictions, and tax pressure.

A hedge fund analyst maps top operators, promo spend, CAC, retention, and gross gaming revenue. An event-driven fund then asks whether pending regulation creates a rerating or derating.

Verify:

* Former heads of risk and compliance.

* Marketing leaders with CAC and promo experience.

* Local regulators or policy advisors.

* Customers and affiliates.

* License friction by country.

Primary research helps avoid broker models that underplay regulatory risks.

### Example Framework: Global Semiconductor Supply Chain

Semiconductors are a critical asset class for hedge fund investors, institutional investors, and long-only pools. AI demand made foundries, EDA, OSAT, equipment, and materials suppliers central to investing in 2024 to 2026.

A value chain view runs from fabless design to EDA, foundries, packaging, test, equipment, substrates, and chemicals. Bottlenecks in advanced nodes and specialty tools drive pricing power.

Verify:

* Former fab operations heads on yield.

* Capex planners on tool timing.

* Equipment sales leaders on order books.

* OSAT executives on capacity.

* Materials suppliers on shortages.

Funds use this research for secular longs, capex cycles, and inventory-cycle shorts.

## Hedge Fund Research Workflow: From Idea Generation to Position Sizing

Effective hedge fund research combines rigorous due diligence with specialized financial data.

1. Source an idea from earnings, events, screens, or macro shifts.

2. Triage the deal: upside, downside, timing, liquidity, and compliance.

3. Run industry analysis using the frameworks above.

4. Form the thesis with base, bull, and bear cases.

5. Test financial, legal, operational, and regulatory compliance risks.

6. Size the position using conviction, volatility, liquidity, and correlation.

7. Monitor expert feedback, surveys, alternative data, and company signals.

Risk-adjusted metrics evaluate past performance beyond absolute returns. Hedge funds aim to provide superior risk-adjusted returns with low correlation to traditional stocks, so go/no-go and sizing decisions matter.
