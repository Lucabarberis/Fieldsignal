# Coleman Research: Expert Network Profile and Alternatives

Coleman Research is one of the original expert network firms, established in 2003 in New York. It connects private equity firms, hedge funds, and consulting firms with industry professionals for one-on-one consultations. After its 2021 acquisition by VisasQ for approximately $102 million, the combined VISASQ/COLEMAN entity operates a global platform with over 750,000 experts across seven offices. It's a trusted expert service provider for large institutional clients, but its pricing model and access barriers make it a poor fit for mid-market firms and project-based research.

This page breaks down how Coleman works, what it costs, where it falls short, and when an alternative like FieldSignal makes more sense.

## Coleman Research Overview: What You Need to Know

Coleman Research was founded by Kevin Coleman in 2003, making it one of the earliest expert network firms with over 20 years of experience in connecting clients with subject matter experts. The company built its reputation serving large business investors, Fortune 500 corporations, and management consultancies that needed a direct and rapid exchange of critical insights with industry professionals.

In August 2021, Tokyo-listed VisasQ acquired Coleman Research for approximately $102 million. At the time, Coleman had annual transaction volume of roughly $43 million, more than twice VisasQ's own revenue. VISASQ/COLEMAN was established in 2021 as a partnership that combined Coleman's strong presence in US and European markets with VisasQ's deep network in Japan and Asia.

Today, the combined entity maintains a global network of over 400,000 senior-level experts within a broader pool of 750,000+ experts globally. Coleman Research connects clients with 750,000+ industry experts across offices in New York, London, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Tokyo. Clients can access expert insights 24/5 across six global offices.

The challenge? Coleman's pricing model lacks transparency. Per-call rates for technology experts run $400 to $1,000 per hour. Minimum commitment requirements aren't publicly disclosed. And the procurement process can create significant barriers for smaller funds and boutique consulting firms that need fast, affordable primary research.

## How Coleman Research Works

Coleman's core service is one-on-one expert consultations via phone and video. Project managers handle expert sourcing, scheduling, and compliance oversight. The firm provides a platform for rapid exchange of critical insights between clients and thought leaders across industries.

Here's how the process works:

1. **Submit a project request.** A centralized Client App allows clients to submit project requests easily. You describe the expertise needed, the research question, and the timeline.

2. **Expert matching and sourcing.** Coleman's team identifies relevant experts from its internal network. In-network experts can be scheduled in less than an hour. Approximately 20% of experts are custom sourced for projects, with average custom recruiting time of less than 24 hours.

3. **Consultation.** You connect with the expert via phone, video, or in person consultations. Coleman ensures consultations rely on non-material publicly available information. VISASQ/COLEMAN has a compliance framework that includes conflict screening, cooling-off periods, and a compliance app that blocks scheduling if an expert fails compliance standards.

Beyond one-on-one calls, Coleman offers several additional services:

* **Custom surveys.** Coleman conducts surveys to gather data and insights from industry specialists. Expert surveys typically require over 30 participants for quantitative data, and client surveys can involve 100 industry specialists for insights. Custom surveys can deliver accurate data you can trust, though surveys can take over 3 days to gather quantitative data.

* **Events and roundtables.** They organize webinars and roundtables featuring seasoned professionals. Coleman runs 35+ weekly events including webinars and roundtables to provide insightful connections with industry experts.

* **Custom research.** Coleman provides actionable, localized primary research combining expert interviews with reporting and analysis. Market entry research helps identify potential barriers to entry.

* **Life sciences specialization.** A dedicated US life sciences team was launched in mid-2023, adding domain expertise in healthcare, pharma, and biotech.

* **Leadership assessments.** Leadership assessments help identify key leadership qualities and can improve organizational performance. A leadership assessment framework supports compliance and transparency, and approximately 20% of experts are custom sourced for leadership assessments. Effective leadership assessment can enhance decision-making processes in organizations.

Coleman's target clients are large PE/VC firms, Fortune 500 companies, and top-tier consultancies with recurring research needs. Expert consultations help businesses make informed decisions quickly. Expert surveys and one-on-ones allow firms to validate investment hypotheses quickly. Coleman helps corporations for strategic insight.

## Coleman Research Pricing and Access Barriers

Coleman operates on a per-call pricing model. For technology experts, third-party sources report rates of [$400 to $1,000 per hour](https://prommer.net/en/tech/services/expert-consultation/networks/coleman-research/), depending on expert seniority and geography.

Here's what's not clear:

* **Annual retainer requirements.** Coleman does not publicly disclose whether it imposes mandatory annual retainers or minimum spend commitments. This is a problem if you're a mid-market firm trying to budget for a single due diligence project.

* **Markup on expert honoraria.** There's no public breakdown of how Coleman splits the hourly rate between expert compensation and service fees. Expert compensation for technology experts reportedly runs $300 to $1,000 per hour, which means the markup could be significant or minimal. You won't know unless you ask.

* **Geographic and seniority premiums.** Senior experts and experts in high-cost geographies command higher rates. These premiums aren't published.

* **Procurement friction.** Contract, legal, and compliance reviews can add weeks to onboarding, especially for mid-market or smaller clients less familiar with expert network frameworks.

For context, the broader expert network market benchmarks look like this:

* **Per-call pricing across the industry:** [$300 to $1,500+ per hour](https://www.nextyn.com/post/expert-network-pricing-guide-2026), depending on expert seniority, geography, and compliance risk.

* **Annual subscription or retainer models:** [$30,000 to $200,000+ per year](https://www.nextyn.com/post/expert-network-pricing-guide-2026), especially with high call volume or enterprise features.

If you're running project-based research (2 to 10 expert calls per year), Coleman's cost structure will likely price you out or force you into commitments that don't match your usage. The lack of transparent pricing means you may overpay unless you negotiate aggressively.

## Who Coleman Research Serves Best

Coleman is built for large institutional clients with recurring research needs and six-figure annual budgets. It's the right fit if you match these profiles:

* **Large hedge funds and mutual fund managers** running continuous primary research across multiple sectors. If you're making 50+ expert calls per quarter, Coleman's scale and compliance infrastructure justify the cost.

* **Fortune 500 corporate development teams** doing market entry research, competitive intelligence, and strategic planning. Coleman's global experience and cross-border capability (especially Japan and Asia) add value here.

* **Top-tier management consultancies** with ongoing client engagements that require access to senior industry professionals across industries. Coleman's project management team handles the sourcing and scheduling so your team can focus on analysis.

* **Institutional investors doing extensive due diligence** on targets across multiple geographies. Coleman's network spans the US, Europe, and Asia, with particular strength in Japan through the VisasQ collaboration.

VisasQ is the only publicly traded company in the expert network space, which adds a layer of financial transparency and regulatory oversight. This matters if your compliance team requires vendor audit trails.

## Coleman Research Limitations for Mid-Market Firms

Coleman wasn't built for you if you're a mid-market PE associate, a boutique consultant, or a founder doing pre-investment research. Here's why:

**High barrier to entry.** Coleman's pricing structure and procurement process favor large organizations with dedicated vendor management teams. If you're at a fund with less than $500M AUM, you'll likely find the onboarding process slow and the pricing steep relative to your research volume.

**Inflexible for project-based work.** If you need five expert calls for a single due diligence project, paying enterprise-level rates without volume discounts doesn't make sense. Coleman's service model is built around recurring, high-volume relationships.

**Procurement complexity.** For one-off projects, the contract and compliance onboarding can take longer than the research itself. This is a significant barrier for boutique firms and founders who need to discuss a market question this week, not next month.

**Financial pressure post-acquisition.** In April 2024, VisasQ recorded a JPY 14.6 billion goodwill write-off on Coleman due to revenue shortfalls. This could affect investment in platform development and service quality over time.

**Transparency gap.** Many clients report not knowing the full cost breakdown between expert honorarium, service markup, and platform fee. When you don't know what you're paying for, you can't evaluate whether you're getting quality for money.

These limitations are exactly why alternatives like FieldSignal exist.
