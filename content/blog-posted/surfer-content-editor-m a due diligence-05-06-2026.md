# M&A Due Diligence: 50+ Item Checklist Across Every Workstream

M&A due diligence is a comprehensive investigation process for target company verification before you sign, finance, and close a deal. This checklist is built for a 30 to 90 day diligence period, with the documents, questions, and workstreams you need to test the target company’s business before finalizing terms.

It’s written for PE/VC associates, corporate M&A teams, boutique consultants, and founders doing buy-side work. It’s not law school theory. It’s a working template for a virtual data room, Q&A tracker, and final due diligence report.

## Deal Timeline: 30/60/90-Day M&A Due Diligence Process

Due diligence timelines usually last 30 to 90 days for corporate buyers. M&A due diligence typically lasts 30 to 90 days, with milestones tied to financing, regulatory compliance, and the purchase and sale agreement.

The M&A due diligence process includes five key steps: scope the workstreams, request diligence materials, verify the evidence, quantify findings, and negotiate deal terms. A due diligence timeline keeps the diligence team focused and prevents scope creep.

| Phase        | Main objective                            | Workstreams                                                                                                               |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Day 0 to 30  | Build fact base and flag red issues       | Financial, legal, tax, commercial, HR, IT, real estate, ESG/compliance                                                    |
| Day 31 to 60 | Run detailed diligence review             | Financial due diligence, legal due diligence review, operational due diligence, commercial due diligence, technology, tax |
| Day 61 to 90 | Confirm findings and document protections | Disclosure schedules, stock purchase agreements, financing agreements, price adjustments, escrow, closing conditions      |
Practical constraints matter. Audited financial statements may lag. Phase I ESA or ASTM E1527-21 reports take site access and fieldwork. A Phase I environmental site assessment often takes 1 to 2 weeks, while Phase II testing can take 4 to 6 weeks. Antitrust or regulatory inquiries can add months.

For deals with material property exposure, run a separate comprehensive real estate track in parallel with core corporate diligence. This includes title, zoning, leases, property condition, capex, and any i environmental site assessment materials tied to owned or leased sites.

## Core Types of M&A Due Diligence (Across All Workstreams)

Key areas of M&A due diligence include financial, legal, operational, and human resources assessments. Most deals also include tax, commercial, technology/IP, real estate, ESG, and compliance workstreams.

Each workstream has an owner, but every diligence matter must feed into one integrated due diligence report. The central diligence lead manages requests, keeps one data room Q&A channel, and stops duplicated questions from wearing down the seller.

### Financial Due Diligence Checklist

Financial due diligence examines income statements and cash flow, plus balance sheets, working capital, revenue quality, and debt. M&A due diligence assesses the target company’s financial performance and legal standing, so the finance workstream usually runs through the full diligence process.

Common due diligence documents include financial statements and material contracts. Start with the target company’s financial statements, then test whether reported EBITDA reflects fair market value economics.

Checklist items:

* Historical financial statements for 3 to 5 years

* Audited financial statements or reviewed financial statements

* Monthly income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements

* Revenue by customer, product, geography, and channel

* Churn, retention, cohort, and renewal data

* AR aging, AP aging, bad debt, and write-off history

* Debt schedules, lease schedules, and off-balance sheet commitments

* Budget vs. actuals and current-year forecast

* Normalized working capital schedule and seasonality analysis

* Quality of earnings report and related financial metrics

* Customer concentration and margin by segment

* Financial records supporting add-backs and adjustments

Quality of earnings work tests recurring profit, owner expenses, unusual revenue, accounting policies, and cash conversion. According to Grant Thornton, quality of earnings analysis focuses on the sustainability and predictability of earnings, not just reported profit.

Former finance staff, former customers, and suppliers can help verify whether revenue is recurring or one-time. That’s where primary research tightens assumptions beyond the virtual data room.

### Legal and Regulatory Due Diligence Checklist

Legal due diligence tests whether you’re actually buying what you think you’re buying, and under which legal constraints. Legal due diligence reviews contracts and litigation history, including pending or threatened litigation, threatened litigation, contractual obligations, and legal compliance.

Core items include:

* Organizational documents and other organizational documents

* Corporate governance documents and board minutes

* Cap table, shareholder agreements, option plans, and warrants

* Material contracts, customer agreements, and vendor agreements

* Licensing agreements, permits, and approvals

* Change-of-control clauses and anti-assignment terms

* Non-compete, non-solicit, exclusivity, and termination rights

* Pending or threatened litigation summaries

* Settlement agreements and arbitration files

* Sector rules, applicable laws, and relevant regulations

Specialist counsel is needed for antitrust, export controls, financial services, healthcare approvals, employment, IP, and regulated cross-border deals. Use a tag like diligence legal in your index so legal counsel can find contracts, filings, and disclosure exceptions fast.

### Tax Due Diligence Checklist

Tax due diligence assesses compliance with tax laws and liabilities. It also tests deal structure, including asset vs. stock purchase, elections, foreign subsidiaries, transfer pricing, and tax basis outcomes.

Checklist items:

* Corporate income tax returns for the last 3 to 5 years

* VAT, GST, sales tax, and other indirect tax filings

* Payroll, withholding, and employment tax records

* Open audits and correspondence with tax authorities

* Advance rulings, tax opinions, and uncertain tax positions

* Tax loss carryforward schedules and limitation analysis

* Structure memos for stock purchase agreements or asset deals

Red flags include recurring late filings, aggressive transfer pricing, loss utilization schemes, and large reserves that weren’t reflected in the model. Tax findings influence purchase price, escrow size, indemnities, and closing conditions.

### Operational and Commercial Due Diligence Checklist

Operational due diligence evaluates efficiency and processes. Operational due diligence examines the company’s operations, while commercial due diligence tests whether the market thesis is real.

Operational assessments in due diligence evaluate supply chains and technological infrastructure. Assessments in due diligence can reveal operational or structural anomalies in target companies.

Operational items include:

* Org charts and process maps for core workflows

* Supplier SLAs, logistics contracts, and fulfillment data

* Manufacturing, service delivery, and customer support KPIs

* Site visit notes and facility-level capex plans

* ERP, CRM, billing, and workflow system dependencies

Commercial items include:

* Market size estimates and share by segment

* Win/loss reports and pricing history

* Marketing strategies and channel performance

* Pipeline quality, conversion rates, and renewal calendars

* Competitive intelligence and industry trends

* Business plans and forecast assumptions

### Technology & Intellectual Property Due Diligence Checklist

Technology due diligence assesses IT infrastructure and cybersecurity risks. Technology due diligence reviews IT infrastructure and security risks, including data security, incident history, vendor exposure, and system integration needs.

IT and IP diligence also tests value. You need to know whether the company owns the intellectual property, source code, trademarks, patents, domains, trade secrets, and licensing agreements that support the deal thesis.

Checklist items:

* Software, infrastructure, and network inventory

* Source code ownership and contractor assignment records

* Open-source software policies and usage logs

* SOC 2, ISO 27001, penetration tests, and audit reports

* Breach logs, incident response plans, and cyber insurance

* Patents, trademarks, domains, registrations, and applications

* License-in, license-out, co-ownership, and encumbrance files

* Customer DPAs, privacy policies, and retention policies

Cybersecurity reviews are essential in due diligence for assessing technology risks. For software-heavy deals, add code quality and architecture reviews from an external specialist.

### Human Capital & Culture Due Diligence Checklist

HR diligence is a risk review and a forward-looking read on cultural fit. It covers human resources, compensation, benefits, classification, claims, and whether key employees will stay.

Checklist items:

* Full employee roster by role, comp, bonus, location, and tenure

* Key person dependencies and retention risk

* Employment agreements and contractor agreements

* Equity and incentive plans, vesting, acceleration, and change-of-control terms

* Health insurance, benefit plans, and pension obligations

* Collective bargaining agreements and works council matters

* Harassment, discrimination, wage, hour, and classification claims

* Voluntary attrition and engagement survey results

Cultural differences can hinder integration during M&A. Former employee interviews help you understand management style, morale, attrition drivers, and whether retention bonuses or management rollover equity make sense.

### Real Estate & Environmental Due Diligence Checklist

Run this as a separate track when the target owns or leases plants, warehouses, data centers, labs, offices, or retail sites. Real estate diligence covers property rights, use restrictions, rent exposure, and hidden capex.

Checklist items:

* Owned property deeds, title policies, and surveys

* Lease agreements, rent rolls, options, and expiration dates

* Zoning confirmations and use restrictions

* Sale-leaseback documents and landlord consents

* Property condition assessments and deferred maintenance

* Environmental assessments, Phase I ESA reports, and Phase II reports

* Historic contamination, remediation, and violation records

* Local environmental permits and compliance files

Asset-heavy targets often need third-party inspections. Findings can change capex assumptions, fair market asset values, debt terms, and indemnities.

## Building and Managing Your Diligence Team

On a typical $50M to $250M deal, the due diligence team includes an internal deal owner, M&A counsel, tax advisor, financial diligence provider, and workstream leads for tech, HR, commercial, and real estate.

The due diligence process can involve forensic accountants and legal counsel. Insufficient expertise can lead to overlooked critical issues, especially in regulated, software-heavy, or asset-heavy deals.

Use one master due diligence checklist, one virtual data room, and one Q&A channel. Virtual data rooms enhance document sharing during due diligence, while diligence software can automate document review and data extraction. AI can streamline M&A due diligence processes, and automated alerts improve due diligence efficiency by tracking progress.

A simple weekly cadence works:

* Monday: workstream standup and risk refresh

* Wednesday: data room and Q&A cleanup

* Friday: red/yellow/green summary to investment committee

Large volumes of data can overwhelm due diligence teams. Communication gaps can lead to misunderstandings in due diligence, so assign one diligence team lead to own status, blockers, and decisions.

## Turning Diligence Findings Into a Clear Due Diligence Report

A due diligence report should include a 1 to 2 page executive summary, risk matrix by workstream, and appendices with supporting detail. Don’t just list issues. Quantify impact through price adjustments, escrow recommendations, covenants, closing conditions, or walk-away triggers.

Use this structure:

1. Deal thesis recap

2. Methodology and diligence period covered

3. Findings by diligence area

4. Red/yellow/green risk matrix

5. Financial impact and fair market assumptions

6. Recommended changes to the purchase and sale agreement

7. Conditions to close and open diligence matters

Identifying potential risks helps in negotiating deal terms based on due diligence findings. Due diligence helps identify hidden risks before finalizing a deal, and incomplete information can compromise deal value during due diligence.

Separate facts from judgment calls. Expert interview findings should appear as anonymized themes, not unsupported anecdotes, so the investment committee can see the evidence and the interpretation.

## Common M&A Due Diligence Pitfalls (And How To Avoid Them)

M&A failure rates indicate that 70% to 90% of transactions do not meet projected value, a range often cited in M&A research and discussed by [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-big-idea-the-new-ma-playbook). Poor diligence isn’t the only cause, but weak diligence increases the odds of overpaying, missing liabilities, or inheriting problems you could’ve found.

Common pitfalls include:

* Over-reliance on seller-provided data

* Insufficient commercial due diligence

* Ignoring integration risks until after signing

* Pushing hard contract questions to the last week

* Treating cybersecurity as an IT footnote

* Missing contractor misclassification and benefit liabilities

* Under-scoping regulatory compliance and applicable laws

Late red flags usually look specific: undisclosed churn from top accounts, manual billing processes that won’t scale, hidden change-of-control clauses, IP assigned to founders personally, or unresolved antitrust or regulatory inquiries.

Avoid checklist theater. Front-load critical items in the first 30 days, including revenue quality, customer concentration, material contracts, liquidity, legal standing, and key management retention. Mitigate risk by matching scope to deal size: don’t overbuild diligence for a $5M tuck-in, and don’t under-scope a $200M platform.
