## Distressed Investing Research: Quick Overview

Distressed investing research is the analytical backbone that separates profitable distressed debt investments from expensive mistakes. If you're working on a PE, VC, or corporate M&A team evaluating credits in or near default, your research process and compliance framework determine whether you capture value or inherit someone else's problem.

Distressed debt is debt securities rated CCC or lower, trading at substantial discounts to par (typically under 70 cents on the dollar), or with yields exceeding 1,000 basis points over U.S. Treasuries. Bonds with yields exceeding 1,000 basis points are classified as distressed. Distressed debt has a credit rating of CCC or less. Investors seek high returns through restructuring distressed companies, and distressed debt investing can yield returns between 15% and 25%.

## Core Concepts in Distressed Debt Investing

Before you build a research process, you need shared vocabulary. Here's what matters.

**Distressed debt vs stressed debt vs special situations:**

* Distressed debt refers to bonds, loans, or trade claims of a distressed company in or near default. Think of a retailer that filed Chapter 11 with bonds trading at 40 to 50 cents. Unsecured debt trading at 40% or less of face value indicates impending default risk.

* Stressed debt sits above distressed: deteriorating fundamentals, downgrade trajectory, but not yet in default.

* Special situations is the broader category. It includes distressed and stressed credits plus M&A-driven restructurings, liability management exchanges, and rescue financing.

**Capital structure basics:**

Distressed debt investors must understand the capital structure priority of payouts. Secured creditors are paid first in a company's capital structure priority. Below them: priority unsecured claims (taxes, wages), general unsecured, subordinated debt, then equity holders. The fulcrum security is the tranche where ownership of the reorganized company tips. Fulcrum security dictates who owns the reorganized company after restructuring. An unstable capital structure, especially one where breaching financial covenants often triggers financial distress for companies, is usually the entry point.

**Strategies and objectives:**

Distressed debt strategies include passive and active approaches. Passive strategies involve acquiring undervalued distressed debt securities and waiting for the market to recognize intrinsic value. Active strategies aim to influence restructuring or gain control of companies. Investors may use a loan-to-own strategy to gain equity control, converting debt positions into equity through the restructuring process. Other objectives include par recovery, earning high coupons, or trading around catalysts like court rulings.

**Who invests:**

Typical distressed debt investors include credit [hedge funds](/blog/hedge-fund-research-how-top-funds-build-an-edge), distressed debt funds, opportunistic private equity firms, family offices, and specialized asset managers. Hedge Fund Research reports a [14.66% one-year return for distressed investing](https://www.pfolio.io/academy/distressed-debt-investing). Distressed investing offers potential returns of 14.66% annually.

**Recent cycles:**

The 2020 to 2024 period created large pools of distressed assets. COVID dislocations hit retail and lodging. Rate hikes pressured real estate refinancing, especially office. These economic downturns and interest rates shifts opened distressed debt opportunities across sectors like office, small-cap industrials, and consumer businesses.


## Building a Distressed Investing Research Process

Here's a five-step research flow that works for distressed situations.

**1. Idea sourcing**

Screen court dockets (PACER for U.S. Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings), bond price feeds, rating agency downgrade notices, and 10-K/10-Q filings with going-concern audit opinions. In 2023, US bankruptcy filings reached a 13-year peak. In April 2023, Chapter 11 bankruptcy filings rose by 40%. 2023 saw the highest number of bankruptcies since 2010. These are the kinds of data points that signal opportunity.

**2. Desk screening**

Size the opportunity quickly. Calculate enterprise value versus the debt stack. Compare current bid levels to upcoming maturities. Example: a bond trading at 45 cents with $100 face value, enterprise value implying $60 in recovery against $80 in total claims. Investors often buy distressed debt at significant discounts like this to capture upside.

**3. Legal and claims analysis**

A restructuring attorney or internal legal team should map the [claims waterfall](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/restructuring-investment-banking/the-recovery-waterfall-absolute-priority-applied): lien perfection, priority claims under Section 507, deficiency claims, and likely recovery scenarios. Investors face legal complexities during bankruptcy proceedings, and getting claims priority wrong can cost you 10 to 20 points on recovery. Bankruptcy law governs the order, but contract details in intercreditor agreements can shift outcomes.

**4. Primary expert work**

Schedule targeted calls with former CFOs, suppliers, customers, and competitors to test your turnaround thesis. This is where you identify whether the business has franchise value or is a melting asset. Limited access to financial information complicates due diligence, so expert calls fill critical gaps.

**5. Ongoing monitoring**

Track docket events, Debtor-in-Possession financing approvals, Section 363 sale processes, and new adversary proceedings. Debtor-in-Possession financing provides new capital to bankrupt companies with super-priority repayment. Section 363 sales allow distressed companies to sell assets free of liens and claims. Timing around these court milestones can double or halve value.


## Analytical Methods for Distressed Debt Investments

**Recovery analysis:**

Build downside, base, and upside cases. Example: you buy a bond at 45 cents. Your downside recovery estimate is 30, base is 60, upside is 90. Stress test each: what if asset values drop another 20%? What if legal fees drag on for six more months? Senior secured recoveries have dropped to roughly [40 to 45 cents in recent defaults](https://deluair.com/consultancy/insights/distressed-debt-2026), down from 50 to 70 cents in prior vintages. Second lien recoveries have plunged to single digits.

**Liquidity analysis:**

Build 13-week cash flow models. Test borrowing base constraints and covenant headroom. Identify cliff events: next debt maturity, required capital expenditure, lease expirations on term loans. Market illiquidity can lead to significant position discounts, so factor in your own exit timeline.

**Operational assessment:**

Distinguish a broken business from an over-levered one. A regional healthcare chain with strong patient volumes but bad capital structure is different from a retailer whose customers have permanently migrated online. Healthcare had the second most bankruptcies in 2023.

**Sector-specific nuances (2022 to 2025):**

* Commercial real estate: office CMBS delinquencies climbed to roughly [11% by early 2026](https://deluair.com/consultancy/insights/distressed-debt-2026). Nearly $957 billion in CRE debt matured in 2025, with similar pressure expected in 2026.

* Energy: exposed to 2020 to 2021 commodity price swings and higher capital intensity post-recovery.

* Consumer and retail: post-COVID traffic shifts created distressed debt opportunities as anchor tenants closed and online migration accelerated.

* The automotive industry is expected to present distressed investment opportunities as supply chain and EV transition costs mount.

Due diligence in distressed debt must combine quantitative analysis with on-the-ground insight from people who know the company's operations, customers, and contracts.

## Due Diligence & Primary Research in Distressed Situations

Standard diligence checklists don't work for distressed debt investing. The information environment is worse, timelines are compressed, and management may not be cooperative or trustworthy.

**Core diligence angles:**

* **Financial DD:** Check quality of earnings, off-balance-sheet obligations, customer concentration, and realistic capex needed for a turnaround. Passive distressed investing expects market recognition of a company's intrinsic value, but you need to verify that intrinsic value actually exists.

* **Legal DD:** Review credit agreements, intercreditor agreements, UCC filings, and key milestones in the Chapter 11 or out-of-court process. Competition among investors can hinder control acquisition, so understand who else holds claims.

* **Operational DD:** Expert interviews with former plant managers, sales leaders, or regional GMs can validate issues like chronic under-investment or real customer churn that don't show up in financial information.

* **Market DD:** Competitor and customer calls test whether the distressed company can win back share post-restructuring or if the market has structurally moved on.

## Compliance & Information Barriers in Distressed Investing Research

Compliance risk in distressed investing research is higher than in standard equity research. Here's why.

**Public side vs private side:**

When you sign an NDA with a company or join an ad hoc group, you move from public side to private side. Once private, you can't trade on that information. The line is bright in theory, blurry in practice. Coordinate with your CCO or external counsel to set clear internal rules for when you stay public versus go private in a given special situation.

**What counts as MNPI:**

Material non-public information includes unannounced asset sales, draft restructuring support agreements, non-public financials shared in lender presentations, and unpublished details of equity conversion terms. The Securities and Exchange Commission enforces these boundaries under [Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5](https://iqnetwork.co/expert-network-compliance/). For a deeper view of [how MNPI rules apply to expert calls](/blog/mnpi-and-expert-networks-what-pe-and-hedge-fund-buyers-need-to-know), see the dedicated guide. Such securities carry high risk if you trade while holding MNPI.

**Why expert calls are sensitive:**

In distressed situations, former executives and current vendors may know details about the restructuring process that haven't been disclosed publicly. Expert calls must avoid MNPI.

## Special Situations Case Patterns & Lessons

**Loan-to-own:**

Distressed debt funds accumulate the fulcrum security, participate in DIP financing, and convert debt to equity in the plan of reorganization. This is how debt holders become equity holders and gain substantial gains through equity conversion. Distressed debt investors often face competition for control of assets in these situations.

**DIP and exit financing:**

Providing secured DIP in 2020 to 2024 retail or energy restructurings has been a consistent play. Underwriting exit facilities captures fee income and equity kickers. These are high risk, high returns positions in a portfolio.

**Busted LBOs:**

Over-levered sponsored companies from the post-2019 period, financed with cheap loans when interest rates were low, became distressed debt opportunities for secondary buyers as covenants broke and refinancing became impossible. Private equity firms that originally sponsored these deals often lost control to distressed debt funds that acquired debt investments at pennies on the dollar.

**International complexity:**

Different insolvency regimes, creditor rights, and timing make international distressed assets far more complex. U.S.-centric advice on bankruptcy law doesn't apply outside U.S. borders. Tailor your research and influence strategy to local regimes.

**Lessons:**

1. Contract details with key customers and vendors often matter more than macro views.

2. Timing around court milestones can reset recovery assumptions overnight.

3. Bidding dynamics in Section 363 sales can produce unexpected outcomes. A stalking horse bid may be blown out by a third-party bidder.

4. Expert input on operational decay, like vendor supply delays or customer cash stalls, frequently uncovers risks not visible in financial data or markets.
