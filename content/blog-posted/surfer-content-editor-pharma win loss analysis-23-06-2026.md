## What win-loss analysis means in a pharma context

Win-loss analysis in pharma is a structured approach to reviewing deal outcomes: formulary wins and losses, brand selection in treatment protocols, vendor RFP results, and strategic partnership decisions. It focuses on understanding recent formulary inclusions and prescriber adoption, going beyond what CRM reason codes capture.

"Wins" and "losses" in pharma include:

* Payer coverage decisions (tier placement, utilization management, prior authorization)

* Hospital formulary outcomes from P&T committee members

* MSA, RFP, or tender awards for services or data licensing

* HEOR or real-world evidence contracts

* Commercial services selections (patient support, digital tools, supply)

This differs from generic B2B win-loss. You're dealing with complex buying committees, medical-legal review, payer policies, and regional HTA decisions that shape the decision making process. The analysis validates the dynamics of decision makers involved in pharmaceutical purchases, not just a single buyer chose moment.

A win-loss ratio measures wins against losses. Win rate is calculated as (Won Deals / Total Opportunities) x 100. In pharma, you should track both metrics by indication, geography, and competitor to spot patterns.

Concrete deal types to analyze:

* A US PBM formulary decision in 2025 where a specialty immunology drug loses preferred tier because real-world adherence data is weak

* An EU5 oncology RFP requiring HEOR submission and cost-effectiveness analysis vs class comparators

* A hospital system formulary decision in 2024 comparing a branded product vs a biosimilar alternative on storage, administration, and safety monitoring

* A vendor services contract for patient support tied to documentation quality and turnaround speed

## Methods: how to collect pharma win-loss data safely

Effective pharma win-loss analysis combines three data sources: internal CRM data, structured internal debriefs, and external direct buyer feedback gathered through buyer interviews or surveys. The process includes data collection through interviews with stakeholders. Actionable insights from qualitative interviews can be mapped against quantitative CRM data. Effective win-loss analysis requires feedback from lost prospects and new clients.

### Using CRM and internal data for pharma win-loss

CRM data in pharma typically includes account type (payer vs provider), therapeutic indication, geography, deal size, competitor noted, and reason codes for the close. You'll find crm records tagged as closed lost with codes like "price" or "no budget."

The limitation: those codes hide detail. Incomplete discovery leads to lost sales opportunities. A reason code of "price" might mask prior-authorization burden, weak real-world evidence, or a missing safety endpoint. [Deloitte's analysis of 286 products](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/life-sciences/pharmaceutical-pricing-market-access.html) across 19 pharma firms showed that formulary tier placement correlates strongly with competitive intensity in metabolic, cardiovascular, and CNS categories, but not in oncology or immunology, where other factors dominate.

How to use CRM data well:

* Segment outcomes by geography, indication, and competitor brand

* Identify deal types with anomalous loss rates (e.g., an oncology brand losing more in hospital tenders vs outpatient in 2025)

* Use segmented CRM views to trigger external interviews: pick representative samples of wins and losses in each cohort

### Direct buyer feedback: interviews and surveys

Direct buyer feedback in pharma means structured interviews or surveys with payers, IDN pharmacy leaders, procurement managers, and sometimes KOL clinicians who participated in the buying decision. Independent interviewers get more candid responses on sensitive topics. Buyers won't tell your sales rep the real reason they said no.

Structured post-decision interviews are essential to extract actionable feedback. Interview decision makers to gather accurate feedback. Use open-ended questions to encourage detailed responses. Conduct interviews soon after the deal closes for accurate insights, ideally within 30-60 days.

Example questions tailored to pharma:

* "What evidence, clinical or economic, was most influential in your decision?"

* "How did safety monitoring, label restrictions, or REMS programs affect perceived risk?"

* "How did competing brands frame their real-world outcomes vs yours?"

* "What administrative or operational burdens, like cold chain or prior authorization, influenced your choice?"

Surveys can reach a wider audience than interviews, so consider deploying short quantitative surveys alongside qualitative calls. Limit interviews to 20-30 minutes. Focus on decisions from the last 6-12 months. Never ask about future pricing plans or coordinated competitive behavior.

## Designing a pharma win-loss program: step-by-step

Building a win loss program for pharma follows a clear sequence. It involves gathering feedback from stakeholders like healthcare providers and payers, then turning raw data into action. A weighted win rate focuses on revenue from won deals, so make sure you're tracking both volume and dollar-weighted outcomes.

**Step 1: Define objectives.** Pick what you need to learn. Example: "Why are we losing to Biosimilar X in US payer channels since Q1 2024?"

**Step 2: Pick cohorts.** Segment by indication, buyer type, geography, and competitor so patterns can be trusted.

**Step 3: Collect data.** Pull CRM data, run internal debriefs, commission external interviews.

**Step 4: Analyze patterns.** Aggregate themes. Quantify how often each driver appears. Compare across cohorts.

**Step 5: Drive action.** Share findings with commercial, access, HEOR, and product teams. Track improvements.

Success depends on picking clear cohorts. Don't mix products, regions, and buyer types. The win loss program should be co-owned by competitive intelligence and market access, not run ad hoc by a single sales leader.

### Defining pharma-specific objectives and cohorts

Set objectives like "understand why we lost 2025 EU hospital tenders for our anti-infective" or "explain declining win rates against Biosimilar X in US payers since Q1 2024." The analysis benchmarks a drug's efficacy and pricing against competitors in the market. Key themes analyzed include product fit and competitive positioning.

Research cohorts should share similar indication, buyer type, geography, and competitive set:

* US commercial payers for a specialty immunology brand vs a biosimilar competitor, Q4 2024 through Q2 2025

* EU5 oncology launch RFPs in hospital tender processes, 2024-2025

* US Medicaid MCOs for a diabetes brand vs ex-US HTA-driven markets for an oncology launch

### Structuring compliant interviews and questionnaires

Interview guides must avoid prompting for confidential contract details, future pricing intents, or anything that looks like coordinating competitive behavior. The analysis looks at pricing strategies and competitor hurdles to inform adjustments in contract negotiations, but the questions themselves stay on the right side of compliance.

Safe topics for interviews:

* Perceived value vs comparators

* Administrative friction in formulary processes

* Speed and quality of medical information response

* Clarity and relevance of real-world evidence presented

* Patient support and customer success infrastructure

## Compliance, privacy, and pharma-specific constraints

Pharma win-loss programs must respect promotional rules, anti-kickback regulations, competition law, and company-specific medical-legal-review policies. Win-loss analysis helps identify competitive gaps in the pharmaceutical market, but the way you gather that intelligence matters. You should share win-loss findings across departments for greater impact, but only through compliant channels.

Practical watchlist for your first pharma win-loss study:

* Honoraria caps for HCPs and payer experts must reflect fair-market-value, documented professionally

* Data privacy requirements: no patient-identifiable data, HIPAA in the US, GDPR in the EU

* MLR review of discussion guides, especially questions about safety, evidence, or comparative claims

* Clear separation between market research and promotional activities, never feed win-loss interviews directly into marketing materials without review

* Secure storage of transcripts, quotations, and expert identities under NDA and access restrictions

## What pharma win-loss analysis reveals: themes and deal outcomes

Win-loss analysis uncovers unmet needs regarding products and services that internal teams miss. The analysis refines the company's value proposition based on real customer understanding, not internal assumptions. Weak differentiation often results in lost sales to competitors.

Typical themes that surface:

* Clinical differentiation clarity: what seems differentiated on paper isn't perceived that way by payers unless backed by head-to-head or real-world data

* Health-economic value story: budget impact, ICER/QALY, total cost of care vs comparators

* Pricing and rebating structure: net vs list price, rebate timing, risk-sharing terms

* Ease of doing business: prior authorization burden, storage, patient support, data reporting

* Medical support quality: response speed on safety questions, post-market surveillance availability

**Example:** A national PBM contract lost in 2025, not because the competing drug was cheaper, but because its adherence-support program and digital monitoring tools reduced downstream adverse event rates. The PBM valued this more due to member retention concerns.

### Typical loss drivers in pharma deals

Common patterns across lost deals:

* Insufficient real-world evidence vs a competitor

* Slow or unresponsive medical information and safety support

* High administrative burden (cold chain, REMS, lab monitoring, prior authorization)

* Contracting misalignment: rebate value, payment timing, risk-sharing terms don't fit 2024-2026 budgets

* Not engaging the true economic decision maker at the payer early enough

* Safety perceptions from older data or label ambiguity creating feature gaps in the sales experience

Win-loss analysis improves sales execution by evaluating the sales team's effectiveness across these dimensions. It helps refine communication of health economics during formulary negotiations. When deals stall or fall through, the deeper analysis reveals whether the issue was the sales process, the evidence package, or something else entirely. These aren't one-off anecdotes. They're patterns visible across many organizations and multiple accounts.

### Using findings to refine pricing strategies and contracts

Buyer feedback clarifies whether a brand is losing on headline price, net effective price after rebates, perceived value, or non-financial friction. Real buyer feedback often reveals that product marketers and sales leaders assumed price was the blocker when it was actually operational burden or missing evidence.

Presenting findings leads to developing initiatives that capitalize on strengths and address weaknesses. Pharma teams can use win loss insights to:

* Adjust contracting approaches and value-based agreements rather than cutting list prices

* Invest in patient-support improvements if that's what tipped the buying decision
