# Privacy Law in Research Interviews: A Practical Field Guide

Conducting research interviews on privacy law requires careful preparation and techniques. This guide gives you the concrete steps to run GDPR compliant expert calls, customer interviews, and due diligence conversations without creating legal exposure for your firm.

## Quick-start checklist for GDPR-compliant research interviews

This is a printable checklist for making research interviews compliant with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). It's built for PE/VC associates, corporate strategy teams, and boutique consultants who rely on expert calls for primary research.

* **Define your legal basis** before the first call. Choose between consent, legitimate interests, or contract. Document why you chose it. You can't switch later.

* **Prepare an informed consent form** that covers data collection and usage. Keep it separate from NDAs or commercial terms. Data controllers must obtain informed consent for data processing.

* **Limit the interview data you collect.** Only collect data that is necessary for research purposes. Don't ask for information you won't use.

* **Set up secure data storage.** Store research data securely on approved devices only. Use strong passwords and encryption for data storage. Limit access to data to authorized individuals only.

* **Create a data retention policy.** Define how long you'll keep raw recordings, transcripts, and contact details. Delete participants' data when no longer needed.

* **Prepare for data subject rights requests.** Participants can request deletion of their personal data under GDPR. Have a process ready to find and remove their data within 30 days.

* **Know your breach response.** If a data breach occurs, identify the scope, notify your DPO or legal team within 24 hours, and assess whether supervisory authority notification is required within 72 hours. GDPR violations can incur fines up to €20 million or 4% of global turnover.

GDPR was implemented on May 28, 2018, and it applies to any research project involving EU or UK participants. Your research team remains the data controller even when using expert networks like FieldSignal or tools like Zoom and Notion for data processing. Data controllers are responsible for third-party data processing compliance.

FieldSignal is built to be GDPR compliant and can provide template consent language and sample retention policies as part of any research engagement. Implement clear data management procedures throughout research from day one.

## Core privacy concepts for research interviews

"Research interviews" here means expert calls, customer interviews, due diligence calls, panel discussions, and internal stakeholder interviews. Any conversation where you're collecting qualitative insights from an identifiable person.

Personal data in this context includes names, email addresses, employer, job title, phone numbers, and voice or video recordings. The combination of someone's role and geography can also identify them. Interview data is typically a mix of audio, transcripts, and notes, and all of it counts as personal data under GDPR.

In the research process, the client is usually the data controller (decides why and how data is processed). FieldSignal and transcription services act as data processors, [operating on the controller's instructions](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/gdpr-data-controller-vs-processor.html). Every operation on interview data, recording, storing, transcribing, analyzing, sharing with your deal team, counts as processing personal data.

Data privacy and data protection are parallel ideas. Privacy focuses on the rights and expectations of research participants. Protection focuses on security and governance controls you put around participant data.

Other regimes matter too. CCPA was first signed into law in 2018 and covers California residents. UK GDPR applies post-Brexit with nearly identical obligations. This article focuses on GDPR compliance because it typically sets the highest bar.

The business risk is real. Regulatory fines, deal delays in due diligence, and reputational damage can all follow if interview recordings leak or are mishandled. Familiarize yourself with fundamental privacy documents to understand terminology before you start your first research project.

## Choosing the legal basis for collecting interview data

Getting the legal basis right is the foundation of lawful interview data processing under GDPR. You need to determine this before you draft any consent form or schedule a single call.

**Informed consent** works when the participant can freely refuse without penalty, there's no power imbalance, and you can honor withdrawal requests by deleting their data. Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous.

**Legitimate interests** is often the better fit for B2B expert interviews where consent creates administrative overhead. You must document a Legitimate Interests Assessment showing a genuine interest, necessity, and a balancing test against the person's rights. Recent [EDPB Guidelines on legitimate interests](https://www.matheson.com/insights/edpb-publish-oss-case-digest-on-legitimate-interest-under-the-gdpr/) require that the interest be "clearly and precisely articulated."

**Contract** applies when you've hired an expert under terms that explicitly include certain data use. It only covers what's in the contract.

**Concrete scenario:** You're doing due diligence on a Series B SaaS company in Germany. You're interviewing former employees and customers. Legitimate interests works here if you can show necessity (you can't get equivalent insight without interviews) and proper balancing. If you're collecting sensitive data or recording calls, consent is safer.

Define clear objectives focusing on specific regulations or compliance challenges before choosing your basis. Focus questions on specific regulatory frameworks and their intersection with organizational systems. Whatever legal basis you pick must be clearly explained in participant materials and cannot be switched later to justify new uses of interview data.

FieldSignal's compliance process includes documenting the chosen legal basis for each project and mirroring that in expert onboarding and call invites.

4. Who will access recordings and transcripts

5. Retention period

### Structuring the consent form

Keep the consent form separate from NDAs or commercial terms. Write it in plain English with distinct checkboxes for:

* Permission to record audio or video recordings

* Follow-up contact for clarification

GDPR requires informed consent for all data collection activities. Participants must give informed consent for data processing. Obtain explicit consent from participants regarding recordings and observers on the line, and disclose any third-party tools like Zoom, Otter, or Notion used for transcription and notes.

Informed consent forms help maintain trust and ethics. You must keep a record of consent with the research data. Maintain secure records of consent obtained from participants.

Always secure explicit, informed consent before recording interviews. Participants can withdraw consent at any time during research, and you must honor that withdrawal promptly.

Allow participants to review transcripts to remove sensitive information before study use when practical.

## Minimizing and structuring interview data

Only collect the personal data and interview content you truly need for the research objective. Data minimization isn't just a legal requirement. It reduces your risk surface.

### Practical ways to minimize data in interviews

* Evaluate interview questions to avoid gathering unnecessary personal data. Don't ask for full names of third parties mentioned in conversation.

* Only collect necessary participant information during research. Skip demographic questions unless they're directly relevant to your research project.

* Use a structured approach to prevent participants from sharing unnecessary information. Use semi-structured interviews to stay flexible while maintaining focus on research questions.

* Review questions to ensure they provide necessary information for research goals. Cut anything that collects personally identifiable information you won't analyze.

* Minimize collection of personally identifiable information unless strictly necessary.

### Pseudonymization and separation

Use participant IDs and codenames instead of real names in shared notes, deal decks, and debriefs. Example convention: "FS-DE-Expert-001" for the first German expert on a FieldSignal project.

Separate contact details from substantive interview data. One secure file for contact info, one for transcripts and notes, linked only by participant ID. Pseudonymization replaces personal data with pseudonyms but still counts as personal data under GDPR.

Anonymize data as soon as possible in the research process. Anonymization removes personal identifiers from data. GDPR does not apply to fully anonymized data, so [proper anonymization significantly reduces your regulatory exposure](https://www.mayadataprivacy.com/post/gdpr-fines-2024-2025-top-10-penalties-anonymization). Always remove direct personal identifiers from research outputs. Anonymized extracts can be shared without privacy concerns.

Delete unnecessary information during transcription to protect participant data.

### Sensitive data

Sensitive personal data requires explicit consent under GDPR. This includes health information, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, biometric data, and information relating to a person's sex life. In commerce-focused interviews, avoid collecting sensitive personal information unless it's essential. If someone volunteers it, flag and handle it under Article 9 rules.

FieldSignal projects standardize these practices by default, so clients receive de-identified interview outputs suitable for internal sharing.

## Secure storage, retention, and deletion of interview data

Data protection obligations come down to practical daily behaviors: where files live, who has access, and how long they stay there.

### Storage best practices

* Use company-approved devices and encrypted drives. Never store files with contact details in open cloud folders or personal devices.

* Use SSO-controlled SaaS tools. If you use Microsoft OneDrive or similar platforms, ensure proper access controls.

* Store research data securely on approved devices only. Use strong passwords and encryption for data storage.

* Limit access to data to authorized individuals only. No sharing raw files via personal email.

### Setting retention periods

Set a clear data retention policy for each research project:

| Data Type                    | Suggested Retention | Notes                                   |
|------------------------------|---------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Raw recordings               | 6-12 months         | Delete after transcription review       |
| Transcripts with identifiers | 1-2 years           | Tied to deal or project lifecycle       |
| Anonymized notes             | 2-3 years or longer | No GDPR constraints if truly anonymized |
| Contact details              | Until project close | Delete or archive securely              |
Document these periods in your privacy notices.

### Deletion workflow

1. Set calendar reminders tied to project close dates.

2. Batch-delete raw recordings first.

3. Securely delete or anonymize transcripts.

4. Retain only anonymized outputs where possible.

5. Log deletions with dates if your systems support audit trails.

Research data should be deleted when no longer needed. Delete participant data when it is no longer needed. Participants can request deletion of their personal data under GDPR. You must be able to locate and delete all data linked to a participant ID, correct mis-recorded details, and respond to access requests within one month.

### Cross-border transfers

If user research data moves from the EU/UK to the US, you may need Standard Contractual Clauses or reliance on updated EU-US data transfer frameworks. Have legal validate this. Cross-border transfer violations have drawn [some of the largest GDPR fines on record](https://compliance-kit.eu/en/knowledge/gdpr-fines-tracker-2025-2026).

FieldSignal's infrastructure and project-close processes support these retention and deletion expectations, including time-boxed access to raw interview data.

## Managing recording, transcription, and third-party tools

Recording and transcription are high-risk but high-value parts of qualitative research. They require careful data protection choices at every step.

**Rule of thumb:** record only when recordings are needed for accuracy, compliance, or quote verification. If detailed notes from your research sessions suffice, skip the recording to reduce privacy exposure.

### Handling recordings

* Get explicit consent before you record audio or capture video recordings.

* Label files with participant IDs, not names.

* Restrict who can play back raw files.

* Never share raw recordings via personal email or uncontrolled channels.

### Transcription vendors

Whether you use manual or automated transcription services, you need a data processing agreement that matches GDPR standards. Check whether the vendor stores data, uses it for model training, or transfers it outside the EU/UK. Vet subprocessors.

### Anonymization in transcripts

Redact proper names, specific company identifiers, and small-location details that could re-identify niche experts. This is especially important when sharing user research data with broader teams.

## Handling data breaches and high-risk situations

Treat incidents as "when, not if." A fast, structured response protects research participants and limits regulatory exposure.

A data breach in interview terms looks like this: a lost laptop with raw recordings, a misdirected email with transcripts attached, a compromised Zoom account, or an unauthorized download of a call library containing confidential information.

### 4-step response process

1. **Contain.** Stop further exposure immediately. Revoke access, change passwords, isolate affected systems.

2. **Assess.** Determine which interview data, contact details, and data subjects were exposed.

3. **Consult.** Notify your legal team or DPO within 24 hours. Ask about areas that regulators are currently prioritizing during your assessment.

4. **Decide.** Under GDPR's 72-hour rule, assess whether the breach must be reported to the supervisory authority and whether affected participants must be informed.

### High-risk disclosures during interviews

If an interviewee discloses credible threats, evidence of serious illegality, or other sensitive information during a call, your legal or compliance team may require proportionate disclosure even where confidentiality was promised. Flag this possibility in your consent materials.

Maintain an incident log for all interview-related data protection events: date, project, expert ID, description, and remediation steps.

FieldSignal's operations include incident playbooks and secure audit trails to support clients' reporting obligations.

Maintain an incident log for all interview-related data protection events: date, project, expert ID, description, and remediation steps.
