# Technology Due Diligence: A Framework for Software and SaaS Deals

Technology due diligence tells you whether a target company's technology assets support the price, the investment thesis, and the post acquisition plan. It evaluates architecture, codebase, and infrastructure, then turns technical risks into valuation inputs, integration requirements, and risk mitigation strategies.

## Finally, a Technology Due Diligence Framework Built for PE/VC and M&A Teams

If you're evaluating a software or SaaS target company, standard due diligence isn't enough. Financial, legal, and commercial work won't show whether the target's technology can accommodate future growth, protect sensitive data, or integrate with existing systems.

Technical due diligence is different from a standard IT audit or security review. An IT audit checks controls, policies, backups, and access management. A security assessment checks vulnerabilities and data protection practices. Tech due diligence assesses a company's technology and product delivery system, including software architecture, code quality, data architecture, technology infrastructure, intellectual property, scalability, and the target company's team.

That matters because the company's technology is often the core asset. Technical debt assessment is essential for understanding future viability. Tech DD identifies risks in architecture, security, and scalability before they disrupt operations post acquisition.

You get a clear understanding of:

* Whether the target company's technology stack is scalable and secure

* Whether the target company's security protocols match relevant regulations

* Whether technical debt will slow future development or increase infrastructure costs

* Whether the acquired technology fits the acquirer's existing operations

* Whether IP ownership, licensing agreements, and proprietary software rights are clean

A full technology due diligence checklist helps you identify risks early, compare technical findings against business objectives, and make informed decisions before signing.

## Why Technology Due Diligence Works

Here's why tech due diligence changes the quality of the deal decision:

* **It connects technology risk to valuation**. Technology due diligence can uncover hidden risks affecting valuation. Identifying technical debt can lower a company's valuation, while a strong tech infrastructure can enhance a target's worth.

* **It goes beyond surface-level metrics**. Due diligence evaluates a company's technology stack for scalability and security, not just uptime claims or product demos.

* **It catches hidden costs early**. It assesses technological risks, scalability, and hidden costs before an acquisition. Accurate valuation reflects hidden costs and capital expenditure needs found during assessments.

* **It supports integration planning**. Post-merger integration highlights compatibility issues between acquired and existing technologies. Technical due diligence helps you see where integration challenges will appear before closing.

* **It assesses the team behind the system**. The diligence process reviews technical talent, engineering practices, key person risk, and whether the target company's team can execute the technology roadmap.

Investors use technology due diligence to prevent overpaying for outdated software. Due diligence lowers risks by identifying outdated technology or unresolved security issues. Technology risks can impact M&A deal conditions or price, including purchase price reductions, escrows, seller warranties, and retention packages for key engineers.

A reported 97% of CIOs have seen Tech Due Diligence uncover significant issues. That's why private equity firms, VC investors, corporate development teams, and strategic buyers treat technology due diligence as a core part of the due diligence process for tech companies.

### Step 1: Technical Infrastructure Assessment

Start with the target's technology foundation.

Review architecture documentation, system diagrams, deployment models, and product roadmaps. Collect essential documentation including architecture diagrams and roadmaps. Prepare a product demo account for reviewers so technical reviewers can test claims against actual product behavior.

Assess the technology infrastructure across:

1. Cloud architecture and deployment model

2. Database design and data management practices

3. Data architecture, redundancy, and backup procedures

4. Third-party dependencies and vendor contracts

5. Performance metrics, uptime, and capacity planning

Assessing cloud strategy involves reviewing hosting reliability on platforms like AWS or Azure. Evaluating vendor contracts helps identify potential risks in technology due diligence, including vendor lock in, price escalation risk, data access restrictions, and business continuity concerns.

Assemble key performance indicators like uptime and test coverage. Current performance metrics show whether the platform can support strategic goals, sustainable growth, and future growth without major rework.

### Step 3: Scalability and Team Evaluation

Finally, test whether the target company can execute its future plan.

Review system performance under projected growth scenarios. Look at load handling, database growth, cloud cost trajectory, observability, incident history, and whether the software architecture can accommodate future growth. This step should produce actionable insights, not vague warnings. A strong Tech DD report includes prioritized recommendations for action. The final due diligence assessment should connect findings to resource allocation, integration planning, business strategy, and strategic alignment.

## Technology Due Diligence Framework Components

A strong diligence assessment should cover the technical areas that affect valuation, risk, and integration. Use this framework as a practical technology due diligence checklist.

### Core Infrastructure Review

Core infrastructure review tests whether the target company's technology can support current revenue and future growth.

Review:

* Cloud architecture and deployment strategy

* Hosting reliability on AWS, Azure, or other platforms

* Database design and data management practices

* Data architecture and data access controls

* Third-party integrations and vendor dependencies

* Infrastructure costs and scaling assumptions

* Disaster recovery and business continuity planning

This review identifies whether the technology infrastructure can scale, whether vendor contracts create potential risks, and whether cloud spend will rise faster than revenue.

### Security and Compliance Analysis

Security and compliance analysis checks whether the target company's security protocols match its customer promises and regulatory duties.

Review:

* Cybersecurity protocols and incident response procedures

* Data privacy compliance, including GDPR, CCPA, and HIPAA

* Access controls and authentication systems

* Penetration testing results and vulnerability management

* Data breaches, incident history, and remediation quality

* Encryption for sensitive data at rest and in transit

* Security measures across development, infrastructure, and operations

Assessing cybersecurity posture is crucial during technology due diligence because unresolved security issues can create financial penalties, customer churn, and deal condition changes.

### Development Operations Assessment

Development operations assessment shows whether the team can keep shipping product after the deal closes.

Review:

* Engineering team structure and technical leadership

* Software development lifecycle and quality assurance processes

* GitHub, JIRA, CI/CD, and release management access

* Code repository analysis and technical debt measurement

* Test coverage, uptime, deployment frequency, and defect trends

* Product roadmap alignment with technical capabilities

* Key person risk around critical systems

Ensure access to systems like GitHub and JIRA for auditors. Without source control, ticket history, and release data, the due diligence assessment depends too heavily on management interviews.

## Common Technology Due Diligence Red Flags

These are the issues that should slow a deal, change deal terms, or trigger deeper diligence:

* **Monolithic architecture that can't scale with business growth**. A tightly coupled architecture limits future development and increases integration challenges.

* **Heavy reliance on legacy systems or outdated technology stacks**. Outdated technology can increase infrastructure costs, security exposure, and maintenance burden.

* **Key person risk with single developers maintaining critical systems**. If one engineer owns core production systems, business continuity depends on retention.

* **Insufficient security measures or compliance gaps**. Compliance issues can lead to financial penalties impacting valuation.

* **Technical debt exceeding 30% of engineering capacity**. High technical debt signals slower delivery, more rework, and reduced operational efficiency.

* **Vendor lock in situations that limit future flexibility**. Vendor lock in can restrict data access, increase costs, and complicate successful integration.

* **Unclear IP ownership or open source license risk**. Intellectual property disputes can reduce valuation and create future litigation risk.

* **Mismanaged data strategies**. Mismanaged data strategies can lead to lost customer insights and weaker strategic alignment.

* **Weak documentation and missing runbooks**. Missing documentation makes it harder to maintain existing operations or integrate acquired technology.

A red flag doesn't always kill a deal. It gives you a reason to adjust valuation, require remediation, modify deal conditions, or build risk mitigation into the post-close plan.
