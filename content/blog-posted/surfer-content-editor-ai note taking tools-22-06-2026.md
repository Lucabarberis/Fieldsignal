# AI Note-Taking for Customer Interviews: 8 Tools Tested

AI note takers let you stop scribbling and start paying attention. For customer interviews, that shift matters more than in any other type of meeting. You need accurate quotes, clear speaker attribution, and a record you can trust when building strategy decks or due diligence memos.

We tested eight tools across real customer interviews and research calls. This article breaks down what works, what doesn't, and which tool fits your specific workflow. The evaluation covers transcription accuracy, privacy features, insight extraction, and how each tool behaves during customer-facing calls.

AI tools can automate note taking and follow-up tasks, which means you spend more time listening to what your customer actually says instead of typing. AI note-taking tools allow users to focus on discussions without typing, and that changes the quality of every interview you run.

## How We Chose the Best AI Note-Taking Tools for Customer Interviews

Customer interviews aren't regular meetings. The person across from you is sharing opinions about your product, your competitor, or your market. Mistakes in transcription, privacy violations, or clunky recording setups can kill the conversation before it starts.

AI note-taking tools enhance productivity by eliminating manual transcription. But not all tools handle interviews equally well. Effective AI note-taking tools specialize in either meeting transcription or personal knowledge management. We focused on the transcription side.

Here's what we evaluated:

1. **Transcription accuracy.** How well does the tool handle varied speaking styles, accents, and industry terminology? Recording and transcribing meetings prevents missing valuable points, but only if the transcript is reliable.

2. **Privacy and confidentiality.** Does the tool send a bot joining the call? Does it notify participants? Is meeting data encrypted and protected? For regulated industries, compliance certifications (SOC2, GDPR, HIPAA) matter.

3. **Insight extraction.** AI meeting assistants transcribe audio into searchable text, but can they also pull out key points, themes, and customer pain points? AI tools can generate actionable summaries identifying decisions and action items.

4. **Bot-free recording options.** Many customers freeze up when they see a recording bot in the participant list. Bot free capture changes the dynamic entirely.

5. **Integration with research workflows.** Workflow integrations push organized notes into project management systems. Export formats, CRM compatibility, and searchable transcripts help review missed meetings or verify decisions.

6. **Real-time vs. post-meeting processing.** Some researchers want live transcription during the call. Others prefer to review ai meeting notes after.

7. **Pricing accessibility.** AI note-taking tools automate transcription and summarization processes, but costs vary wildly from free to $70+/user/month.

## 8 Best AI Note-Taking Tools for Customer Interviews

### 1. Granola

Granola is a desktop app that runs locally on your device, capturing meeting audio without sending a bot into the call. It records system audio, transcribes it, and combines the output with any notes you take manually. After the meeting ends, Granola automatically transcribes and summarizes meetings into structured output.

#### Why It Stands Out

Granola captures notes without sending a bot into meetings. That's the core differentiator. Nobody on the call knows you're recording unless you tell them. For customer discovery calls or sensitive interviews, this changes how openly people talk.

Granola automatically formats meeting notes based on context, meaning your customer interview notes look different from your internal standup notes. You pick a template (customer call, product review, etc.) and the output adjusts.

#### Best For

Researchers running customer discovery calls, founders doing pre-launch validation, and anyone conducting interviews where a visible recorder would reduce candor.

#### Key Strengths

* No bot visibility during virtual calls or in person meetings

* Clean, readable meeting summaries

* Privacy-focused approach with audio deleted after transcription

* Business plans start at roughly $14/user/month, [billed annually](https://vantaige.io/ai-tool/granola)

* Integrates with Notion, Slack, HubSpot

#### Possible Limitations

* Speaker labels are generic (Speaker A, Speaker B) rather than named

* No audio playback to verify meeting transcripts after the fact

* Limited multi-meeting analytics compared to other tools

* Windows version is newer, so feature parity may lag behind Mac

### 2. Fireflies.ai

Fireflies.ai is a meeting assistant that joins your calls as a visible "notetaker" bot, records audio, transcribes it, and builds a searchable archive across all your interviews.

#### Why It Stands Out

The real strength is the archive. Fireflies lets you search across your entire meeting history using natural language queries through its "AskFred" feature, which functions as an ai chat for your interview data. Fireflies offers [sentiment analysis](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/fireflies_ai) on meeting transcriptions, so you can flag interviews where customer frustration was highest.

#### Best For

Research teams conducting dozens of customer interviews who need a searchable database. Sales teams tracking recurring conversations across accounts also get value here.

#### Key Strengths

* Ai powered search across all recorded interviews using AskFred

* Fireflies can transcribe meetings and assign speaker labels with speaker recognition

* Multi language support covering [69+ languages](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/fireflies_ai)

* SOC2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA compliance for enterprise plans

* Collaboration features for team sharing, with CRM integrations

* Meeting content is never used to train models by default

#### Possible Limitations

* Bot joining calls as a visible participant may affect customer comfort

* Paid plans required for advanced ai features like sentiment analysis

* Can feel overwhelming for simple interview needs

* Transcription accuracy drops with overlapping speech or fast talkers

### 3. Otter.ai

Otter.ai provides real time transcription with live collaboration, letting your team follow along as the interview happens.

#### Why It Stands Out

Live transcription means you can review what a customer just said, word for word, while the interview is still running. Real-time transcription ensures you never miss vital points, and it allows active participation in discussions because you can adjust follow ups on the fly instead of relying on memory.

Otter can transcribe uploaded audio and video files too, which is super helpful when you have recorded interviews from other tools or even youtube videos you want to reference. Otter allows transcription of uploaded audio and video files in multiple formats.

#### Best For

Teams who want to review transcripts during interviews to ask better follow-up questions. Also useful when a second team member monitors the live transcript remotely and feeds questions to the interviewer.

#### Key Strengths

* Real time transcription accuracy with speaker identification

* Team can follow along remotely during the call

* Starting price around [$8.33/user/month](https://www.spotsaas.com/compare/otter-ai-vs-avoma) for paid version

* Works across google meet, Zoom, and microsoft teams

* Calendar integration for upcoming meetings

#### Possible Limitations

* May distract from natural conversation flow if you're watching the transcript

* Requires internet connectivity for live features

* Analytics depth falls short of dedicated research platforms

* Free tier has limited monthly minutes

### 4. tl;dv

tl;dv is an ai meeting recorder that captures calls on Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, then lets you create timestamped clips and highlights from the recording.

#### Why It Stands Out

The clip creation is the good stuff. After an interview, you can mark key moments, pull specific customer quotes, and share them as short video clips with your product team or investors. tl;dv provides ai powered meeting search capabilities, so finding a specific statement across dozens of interviews takes seconds instead of hours.

#### Best For

Teams who need to share specific customer feedback with people who weren't on the call. Product managers building feature roadmaps from customer input. Consultants building deliverables from primary research.

#### Key Strengths

* Easy clip creation and sharing of meeting recaps

* Multi language support across [30+ languages](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/tldv)

* Generous free plan with unlimited recordings and transcripts

* tl;dv ensures [GDPR compliance](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/tldv) and no AI training on user data

* Multi-meeting trend detection for themes across interviews

* EU-first infrastructure with data residency options

#### Possible Limitations

* Bot visible in participant list during calls

* Learning curve for advanced features like CRM sync

* Transcript accuracy drops with accented or overlapping speech

* Advanced analytics locked behind paid plans

### 5. Krisp

Krisp started as a noise cancellation tool and evolved into an ai meeting assistant. It filters audio in real time to remove background noise, then transcribes and summarizes the cleaned-up audio.

#### Why It Stands Out

Audio quality determines transcription accuracy. Krisp improves audio quality during meetings by removing background noise, echo, and interference before the transcription engine processes the audio. If you're transcribing calls with customers in noisy environments, field settings, or on poor connections, this makes a measurable difference.

Krisp filters background noise without requiring a bot, keeping the call participant list clean.

#### Best For

Customer interviews conducted over unreliable connections, in hybrid settings, or with participants who have heavy accents. Also strong for in person transcription when ambient noise is a factor.

#### Key Strengths

* Noise cancellation that directly improves transcript accuracy

* Custom vocabulary support (up to 750 terms per workspace) for industry jargon

* Works across all meeting platforms, including google meet, microsoft teams, and Zoom

* On-device transcription for English improves privacy

* [16+ languages](https://krisp.ai/meeting-transcription) supported

#### Possible Limitations

* Requires software installation on each device

* Transcription and summarization features less mature than dedicated transcription tools

* Some languages only available via server-based processing

* Mobile support limited for certain features

* In person meetings may require specific microphone setup

### 6. Avoma

Avoma is a conversation analytics platform built for customer-facing teams. It goes beyond transcription into conversation intelligence, tracking talk-time ratios, competitive mentions, and customer sentiment.

#### Why It Stands Out

Where most tools stop at ai summaries and transcripts, Avoma extracts structured insights. It tracks competitor mentions automatically, flags objections, and maps conversation themes across multiple interviews. AI note takers track action items and highlight key takeaways from meetings, and Avoma does this more systematically than most tools.

Many ai note takers provide a chat interface for interactive Q&A with your meeting data. Avoma's version is built around customer journey mapping and competitive analysis.

#### Best For

Product teams and founders doing systematic customer research across 20+ interviews. Strategy teams comparing what different customer segments say about pricing, features, or competitors.

#### Key Strengths

* Conversation analytics including sentiment analysis, talk-time ratios, and coaching insights

* Tracks competitive mentions automatically across interviews

* CRM integration for linking interview data to customer records

* Ability to create tasks and follow up emails from meeting insights

* Higher satisfaction scores ([\~4.8/5 on G2](https://www.spotsaas.com/compare/otter-ai-vs-avoma)) than most competitors

#### Possible Limitations

* Starting price around $70/user/month, significantly higher than basic tools

* Sales-focused features may not suit pure research workflows

* Overkill for teams running fewer than 10 interviews per month

* Pro plan and team plan pricing adds up fast for larger groups

### 7. Fathom

Fathom is a free ai note taking tool that offers unlimited transcription and recording for individual users. No catch, no time limits on the free tier.

#### Why It Stands Out

Fathom offers unlimited free transcription for meetings. That's unlimited recordings and transcripts for free on the individual plan. For budget-constrained researchers, this is the best entry point into ai meeting tools. Fathom allows users to create short clips of meetings for sharing, which helps you pull important details from interviews and send them directly to teammates.

Summaries generate in under 30 seconds after the call ends.

#### Best For

Early-stage startups, solo consultants, and anyone who needs to capture notes from customer interviews without adding another line item to the budget. Automatic transcriptions support individuals with hearing impairments too, making interviews more accessible.

#### Key Strengths

* Unlimited transcription on the free plan

* Fast ai summaries (under 30 seconds)

* Easy sharing of interview highlights and clips

* [SOC2 Type II, HIPAA, GDPR compliance](https://fathom.video/alt)

* Works with Salesforce, HubSpot, and Zapier

* Supports 25-38 languages depending on plan

#### Possible Limitations

* Free plan limits advanced analytics and team features

* Visible bot in calls may reduce customer openness

* Summary quality drops on long, multi-topic interviews

* Speaker attribution less reliable on the free tier

* Paid version required for full ai credits and enterprise plans

### 8. NotebookLM

NotebookLM is Google's AI research assistant. It doesn't record meetings live. Instead, you upload meeting transcripts from other tools, and NotebookLM synthesizes them with your other research documents, market reports, and competitor analysis.

#### Why It Stands Out

Most tools on this list capture and transcribe. NotebookLM does something different: it connects interview insights with broader research. Upload ten interview transcripts alongside your market sizing doc and competitor teardown, then ask questions across all of them. AI note takers can automatically organize long lectures into structured summaries, and NotebookLM applies the same logic to interview transcripts.

Automated study materials transform text into summaries, flashcards, and quizzes, which can be useful for teams onboarding new analysts to an ongoing research project.

#### Best For

Strategy teams who want to synthesize customer interviews with market research. M&A analysts building a thesis from multiple data sources. Anyone who already has transcripts and wants to turn meetings into structured analysis.

#### Key Strengths

* Connects interviews with market research, product docs, and competitor data

* Advanced AI analysis across multiple uploaded sources

* Free with a Google account

* Natural language querying across all your documents

* Good for summarize conversations across an entire research project

#### Possible Limitations

* Not designed for live interview capture, requires transcripts from other tools

* No calendar integration or ability to record meetings directly

* No speaker recognition or real-time features

* Works best when paired with a live capture tool from this list

## How to Choose the Right Tool for Customer Interviews

### Choose Based on Interview Privacy Needs

If your interviews involve sensitive topics, competitive intelligence, pricing discussions, or expert consultations, bot visibility matters. A recording bot in the participant list signals surveillance. Customers speak differently when they know they're being recorded by a visible third party versus having a private conversation.

For maximum privacy, Granola's bot free capture is the clear winner. Krisp also avoids bot visibility. For most users running sales calls or general customer feedback sessions, a visible bot with proper disclosure is fine.

Check your jurisdiction's consent laws. Some U.S. states require two-party consent. GDPR requires explicit opt-in. Most tools let you configure notifications, but the default settings vary. Don't assume the tool handles disclosure for you.

### Choose Based on Analysis Depth

If you just need meeting transcripts and basic meeting recaps, Fathom or Otter.ai will do the job. If you need to summarize meetings, extract themes across 30+ interviews, and track how customer sentiment shifts over time, you need Fireflies, tl;dv, or Avoma.

The depth of analysis you need depends on what you're building:

* **Investor memo or due diligence report**: You need searchable transcripts, tagged quotes, and cross-interview themes. Fireflies or tl;dv.

* **Product roadmap input**: You need clips, feature request tracking, and the ability to share key moments with product managers. tl;dv or Fathom.

* **Market validation**: You need fast meeting summaries and the ability to spot patterns. Granola or Fathom, then NotebookLM for synthesis.

### Choose Based on Team Size and Budget

Solo researchers and early-stage founders should start with Fathom's free tier. It's the best ai note tool for zero-budget research. The generous free plan covers unlimited recordings and transcription.

Small teams (2-10 people) doing regular customer interviews should look at tl;dv or Granola. Both offer reasonable per-seat pricing and enough collaboration features for shared research libraries.

Larger teams with budget for enterprise plans and a need for CRM integration, compliance certifications, and conversation analytics should evaluate Fireflies or Avoma. Avoma's price is steep, but the conversation intelligence justifies it if you're running 50+ interviews per month.

AI note-taking tools transcribe meetings into searchable text regardless of which tier you pick. The difference between free and paid is how much you can do with that text afterward.
