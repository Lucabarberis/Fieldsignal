# Sales and Commercial Due Diligence: A Buyer's Field Guide

Sales due diligence is a comprehensive investigation during a business sale. It pressure-tests the target company's revenue engine, customer base, and competitive position so you can decide within 30 to 60 days whether to proceed, re-price, or walk away. If you skip it, you're betting your capital on a slide deck.

## Sales Due Diligence: The Fast Answer for Buyers

You won't read every file in the data room. Tight timelines mean you need focused sales and commercial due diligence to avoid overpaying or backing a revenue story that falls apart six months after close. Sales due diligence validates value before finalizing a transaction, and it's the most practical front line of any commercial due diligence effort.

Due diligence preparation typically takes three to six months. Junior PE, VC, and corp dev teams should treat sales due diligence as the working core of the commercial due diligence process, not a separate exercise bolted on at the end.

## What Sales and Commercial Due Diligence Actually Cover

Sales due diligence focuses on the pipeline, win rates, rep productivity, and GTM execution. It confirms the accuracy of information and assesses risks tied directly to how money comes in the door. Sales due diligence reviews financial records and market position alongside the selling motion itself.

Commercial due diligence is broader. It assesses a target company's market position, the competitive dynamics around it, and whether the business model can sustain future growth. A commercial due diligence report includes market demand and competitive dynamics, plus an assessment of the company's business model viability through 2030 and beyond. For the full framework, see our [commercial due diligence guide for PE/VC and corporate buyers](/blog/commercial-due-diligence-a-practical-guide-for-pe-vc-and-corporate-buyers).

Here's a quick comparison:

| Dimension     | Commercial Due Diligence                         | Financial Due Diligence                                               |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Core question | Is this revenue repeatable and defensible?       | Are the reported numbers accurate?                                    |
| Focus         | Market, competition, customer base, sales engine | Financial statements, net income, revenue margins, EBITDA adjustments |
| Time horizon  | Forward-looking (3-5 years)                      | Backward-looking (3-5 years of history)                               |
| Output        | Commercial due diligence report                  | Quality of earnings report                                            |
In a typical 2026 mid-market deal (EV $50M to $500M), sales and commercial workstreams run in parallel with financial due diligence and legal work, starting at LOI and running 60 to 90 days. Due diligence involves analyzing financial records and company operations across all streams simultaneously. Due diligence can be categorized as hard or soft. Hard due diligence focuses on financial records and compliance. Soft due diligence examines corporate culture and employee dynamics, including how target employees interact and how the management structure supports performance.

The output feeds directly into valuation, debt sizing, covenants, and the first-year operating plan.

## Types of Commercial Due Diligence You'll Actually Use

In practice, you'll mix several diligence types depending on deal size, sector, and where you are in the sale process. Your sales diligence questions should flex based on type, with different depth for each.

### Buyer-Initiated Commercial Due Diligence

This is the standard case. A private equity fund, corporate acquirer, or venture capital backed growth equity buyer commissions a full buyer initiated commercial due diligence after LOI and NDA but before signing.

Core focus areas:

1. Addressable market and future growth potential through 2030

2. The target company's commercial activity and competitive advantages in concrete terms

3. Quality of revenue and customer-level economics

4. Sales engine health: pipeline analysis, cohort analysis, win/loss patterns

Buyer-initiated due diligence provides an overview of the target company across all these dimensions. Sales due diligence involves analyzing historical sales data and customer metrics, and it includes examining the current sales pipeline and customer relationships. The output is usually a 50 to 80 page [commercial due diligence report](https://altrioniq.com/insights/due-diligence-checklist) plus a commercial due diligence checklist appendix that can be repurposed for lenders.

### Vendor-Initiated or Sell-Side Commercial Due Diligence

Sellers, especially those running processes with investment banks, often commission their own diligence commercial report 6 to 12 months before a formal sale process. Sellers should prepare documentation before receiving buyer requests.

The selling company's goal is to anticipate prospective buyer questions, quantify upside in the business plan, and address obvious red flags in reporting, contracts, and customer concentration. Sell-side CDD often includes anonymized customer interviews and NPS surveys. Thorough documentation in sales due diligence builds trust between parties and shortens the buy-side timeline. Due diligence involves sharing sensitive information with potential buyers, so getting ahead of that process protects the seller's position.

Sellers who commission their own quality of earnings before LOI often [close 15 to 25 days faster](https://ctacquisitions.com/how-to-prepare-for-pe-due-diligence/) and see re-trade adjustments that are 5 to 10% smaller.

### Red Flag Commercial Due Diligence

Red flag due diligence identifies significant risks before in depth analysis. This is a 10 to 15 day, high-level scan used early when you're screening multiple targets or when deal timing is compressed. Due diligence can uncover red flags in target companies before you commit serious resources.

Typical scope: headline market growth, any obvious regulatory or technology disruption risk by 2028, and basic tests of customer concentration and churn. Sales due diligence analyzes retention rates and churn at this stage with a narrow question set: "Is the revenue real, recurring, and reasonably diversified?" A handful of fast expert or customer calls through FieldSignal can answer this in days.

The output is a short slide deck focused on go or no-go plus 3 to 5 substantial market risk indicators and issues that would need full work later. This is where red flag commercial diligence serves its purpose.

### Top-Up Commercial Due Diligence

Top-up CDD happens when a deal is already advanced and you find specific blind spots, like a new 2025 product line or a region driving most of the forward plan. Complex operations increase due diligence challenges, making targeted follow-up work necessary.

Common triggers: a data cut showing heavy dependence on one channel partner, or a late-stage question from lenders about enterprise vs SMB mix. Sales due diligence evaluates technology and infrastructure for integration capabilities during this phase. This is highly targeted, often 1 to 2 weeks, relying on short expert calls and customer interviews instead of broad market scans.

A clear top-up memo should summarize findings in a few pages that tie directly to price, structure, and covenants.


## Step-by-Step Commercial Due Diligence Process

Here's a practical framework for a 4 to 8 week commercial due diligence process, tailored to buyers running deals without a Bain or McKinsey budget. Due diligence can take three to six months for the full process, but the commercial workstream itself compresses into a shorter window.

The flow: define your investment thesis and key questions in week 1, design your diligence checklist and begin data collection in weeks 1 to 2, run primary research in weeks 2 to 4, synthesize into a commercial due diligence report in weeks 4 to 5, and brief the IC. Lock dates into your deal timetable. A due diligence checklist organizes necessary company information and keeps your diligence team on track.

Risk mitigation identifies potential financial, legal, or operational liabilities before transactions. Due diligence identifies potential legal and financial risks that could destroy value post-close. Failure to exercise due diligence may result in liability for the acquiring company.

### Scoping and Building Your Diligence Checklist

Turn your one-page investment thesis into a concrete commercial due diligence checklist of 30 to 60 specific questions across market, customer base, competition, and sales engine. A due diligence checklist includes financial ratios and management structure alongside commercial questions. Sellers must understand their financial performance metrics like EBITDA before the diligence process begins.

Segment questions by data source:

* **Management Q&A:** Strategy, market view, business plan assumptions

* **Data room exports:** Revenue by segment, contracts, financial statements, employee agreements

* **Expert calls:** Competitive dynamics, win themes, market dynamics

* **Customer interviews:** Satisfaction, switching costs, renewal intent

Include "killer questions" that can end the deal quickly, like dependency on one OEM contract expiring in 2027 or a key intellectual property challenge filed in 2025. Sales due diligence includes assessment of compliance with regulations and contracts. Timebox scoping to 2 to 3 days so analysis starts within the first week.

### Collecting Core Data from the Target

Request these items early in your diligence request list:

1. 3 to 5 years of company's revenue by product, segment, and region

2. Customer cohorts showing retention and expansion by vintage year

3. Win/loss data with reasons and stage outcomes

4. Churn by logo and by ARR

5. Current-year pipeline with stages and probabilities

6. Sales org structure, quota attainment, and rep turnover

7. Financial records including profit margins, financial performance, and the company's financial position

Sales due diligence involves assessing sales forecasts and conversion rates against reality. It validates the pricing strategy and customer contracts. Investors should analyze profit margins over several quarters to spot trends. Sales due diligence assesses customer acquisition cost and lifetime value as part of this data pull.

Structure this as a data request sent via email or uploaded into a virtual data room. Push back hard when metrics are too custom or opaque. Ask for definitions: "How do you define churn?" and "What counts as qualified pipeline?" If key data is missing by end of week 2, flag it in the diligence report as a signal about reporting maturity.

### Primary Research: Experts, Customers, and Competitors

Secondary research alone rarely gives you a better understanding of real commercial risk. Plan 10 to 25 interviews depending on deal size. Sales due diligence examines operational structure, including human resources and IT infrastructure. Evaluating sales team structure is a key part of sales due diligence.

Three interview streams:

1. **Former sales leaders and reps:** Ask about quota attainment in 2024, discounting behavior, CRM accuracy, and why they left

2. **Key accounts and recently churned customers:** Ask about renewal decisions in early 2026, switching costs, and satisfaction

3. **Competitors or channel partners:** Ask about the target's pricing power, win themes, and supply chain operations

## Inside a Commercial Due Diligence Report

Your 40 to 80 slide commercial due diligence report goes to partners, IC, and lenders. Sales due diligence helps prevent post-sale disputes and maximizes company value when the report is thorough. Each section should answer 3 to 5 non-negotiable questions tied to deal value.

Core sections:

1. Executive summary

2. Market analysis and competitive positioning

3. Sales engine and go-to-market assessment

4. Customer base quality and retention

5. Business plan and investment case assessment

Appendices house the detailed diligence checklist, interview guides, and anonymized expert call notes.

### Company and Sales Engine Overview

Start with a concise overview of the target's history, ownership, and product lines, then move quickly into how revenue is actually generated. Operational due diligence reviews a company's operational efficiency, and this section is where you show how the business operates day to day.

Include charts of revenue by product, channel, and geography from 2021 to 2025. Highlight inflection points like a 2023 product launch or a 2024 channel pivot. Map the go-to-market model: direct vs indirect, inside vs field sales, online vs partner-driven. Show the current sales organization, including number of quota-carrying reps, territories, and typical ramp time.

### Market, Competitive Landscape, and Positioning

Open with a 2021 to 2030 market sizing and growth chart using concrete years and growth ranges. Tax due diligence assesses a target's tax exposures and obligations, and intellectual property due diligence evaluates ownership and licensing issues. Both should inform how you view the target's competitive advantages.

Profile 3 to 7 direct competitors with a short comparison of pricing, core features, and target segments. Use customer insights from expert calls, not just desktop research. Take a frank view of the target's positioning: premium vs low-cost, niche vs broad platform. Expert calls through FieldSignal often surface practical details on competitor win themes and reasons buyers switch vendors.

### Customer Base Quality and Retention

Analyze customer concentration: top 10 accounts as a percentage of 2025 revenue. Any single customer above [20 to 25% of revenue](https://altrioniq.com/insights/due-diligence-checklist) is a significant risk. Assess the mix of enterprise, mid-market, and SMB.

Include cohort charts showing retention and expansion by vintage year. Use customer interviews to validate management's claimed NPS and switching costs.

Key metrics to cover:

* Logo churn and net revenue retention

* Average contract length and renewal behavior over 3 years

* Customer relationships quality and depth

* Revenue growth trajectory by segment

### Sales Productivity, Pipeline, and Forecasting

Analyze sales productivity: quota coverage, attainment percentage, pipeline coverage by quarter, and sales cycle length by segment. [Typical B2B win rates](https://cufinder.io/blog/wiki/marketing-metrics/sales-win-rate/) from full pipelines are 15 to 25%, rising to 40 to 50% for qualified opportunities.

Evaluate the current 12 to 24 month forecast. Check whether pipeline stages, win rates, and hiring plans realistically support the business plan. Compare a claimed 35% win rate to what former reps and customers report in interviews. Call out any aggressive sales compensation plans or discounting patterns that distort reported ARR or financial health.

### Business Plan and Investment Case Assessment

This section bridges commercial findings and the deal model used in IC and lender discussions. Reconcile management's plan with your own base, downside, and upside cases. Show which commercial assumptions differ.

Tie major findings to model levers: revenue growth by product, gross margin assumptions, and sales and marketing spend as a percentage of revenue. The conclusion should clearly state what would have to be true about market demand, competitive response, and sales execution for the investment to meet target returns. This is where your diligence aims to confirm or challenge future performance assumptions.


## Practical Commercial Due Diligence Checklist (Sales-Focused)

This is a working due diligence checklist you can adapt for software, B2B services, or industrials targets in 2026. Conducting diligence with a structured checklist keeps the diligence team focused and prevents drift. This diligence serves as the foundation for informed decisions about deal terms.

| Category                 | Key Questions                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Market & Competitive** | What's the addressable market size and growth rate through 2030? Who are the top 3-5 competitors and what are their win themes? Are there regulatory or technology disruptions expected by 2028?      |
| **Customer Base**        | What's customer concentration (top 5 and top 10)? What's logo churn and net revenue retention by year? Are there contracts expiring in the next 12 months without auto-renewal?                       |
| **Sales Engine**         | What's quota attainment by rep tier? What's the average sales cycle by segment? How does the CRM pipeline compare to actual bookings?                                                                 |
| **Unit Economics**       | What's CAC by channel? What's LTV to CAC ratio? How have revenue margins and gross margins trended over the last 3 years?                                                                             |
| **Risks & Red Flags**    | Are there hidden risks in pending legal issues, IP disputes, or regulatory exposure? Is any single rep generating more than 30% of bookings? Are there organizational risks in key-person dependency? |
This checklist can double as the starting point for an RFP to external advisors or to FieldSignal for a custom interview and survey program.

### Sales and GTM Diligence Questions to Prioritize

These questions directly test the health of the sales funnel. The diligence focuses on what's happening in the field versus what appears in the CRM.

For former reps (our [win/loss interview playbook](/blog/win-loss-interviews-for-b2b-saas-a-founder-s-playbook) goes deeper on structuring these):

* What was your actual quota attainment in 2024 and 2025?

* How often did you discount beyond approved levels?

* What were the top 3 reasons you lost deals?

For customers (our [customer reference call playbook](/blog/customer-reference-calls-for-due-diligence-a-buyer-s-playbook) covers the full structure):

* What drove your renewal decision in early 2026?

* Have you evaluated competitors in the last 12 months?

* What would make you switch?

For partners:

* How does this company's product compare to alternatives you also sell?

* Has their channel program improved or declined since 2024?

Include at least one question around internal sales enablement and training, as this predicts whether the team can sustain higher quotas post-acquisition. A potential buyer should use these to document review findings and test the company's financial health narrative. Each question is designed to plug directly into your own diligence process.
