# Off-List Management References

The general advice on management reference work is simple: don't bother with on-list references (the candidate or target picked them); commission off-list references (former direct reports, peers, customers, partners they didn't put forward).

That advice is correct but incomplete. Off-list references only produce useful signal if you ask the right questions. Most reference programmes ask the wrong questions and get the same anodyne signal that on-list references produce.

The three questions below have, in my experience, the highest signal-to-effort ratio of any management reference questions I've used.

## Question 1: "Walk me through a specific situation where you saw [subject] under pressure."

Reference programmes that ask "how does the subject handle pressure?" get useless generic answers ("they're calm under pressure"). Reference programmes that ask for a specific situation get behavioural narrative — and behavioural narrative is where the real signal lives.

The narrative will be 5-15 minutes long. The interviewer doesn't interrupt. By minute 8, the reference is describing things they didn't plan to disclose.

A version of this question generates more new signal per minute than any other reference question I've used.

## Question 2: "Who else from [subject]'s career should I talk to that they probably wouldn't recommend?"

This is the meta-question. The reference, having warmed up over the first 30 minutes of the call, knows who in the subject's network would speak more critically — and they're now psychologically halfway to telling you.

About one in three references answer this candidly. The names you get are reliably the highest-value next references to source. They almost never appear in on-list reference packages.

## Question 3: "If you were hiring [subject] for a role tomorrow, what role would you NOT put them in, and why?"

This question reframes "what are the weaknesses?" — which generates rehearsed answers — into a constructive forward-looking framing. References will articulate specific role-fit limits that they wouldn't articulate as "weaknesses".

Common answers I've heard: "I wouldn't put them in a turnaround", "I wouldn't put them in a public-company CFO role", "I wouldn't put them in a role with a co-founder dynamic", "I wouldn't put them in a heavy enterprise sales motion".

Each of these is potentially deal-relevant in pre-IC or pre-hire diligence.

## How to use them

These three sit inside a 30-45 minute structured reference call. Open with situation context (relationship, dates, scope), warm up with a few neutral background questions, then deploy these three across the middle of the call.

Don't use them in the first 5 minutes. The reference needs to have committed to the conversation before they'll engage at this depth.

## What to expect

A well-run off-list reference programme — 5-8 references per executive, the three questions above plus a structured guide — produces three to five concrete insights about the subject that the on-list reference package will not surface.

In a pre-IC VC context, those three to five insights are reliably what saves or kills the deal in the post-reference IC discussion.
