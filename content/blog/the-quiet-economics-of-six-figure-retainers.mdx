# The Quiet Economics of Six-Figure Retainers

I've now had this conversation with enough emerging-manager hedge funds to write it down once.

A fund's COO will tell me: "We have a $120k retainer with [major network]. We used about $50k of it last year. We renewed because the rate would have gone up otherwise."

This is the quiet economics of the expert-network industry.

## How retainers work

The major networks operate on annual minimums. Standard contract: a per-call rate published nominally (say €800/hour) and an annual minimum that's typically 6-12x what a similar boutique-tier client would actually consume.

The fund signs because the alternative — ad-hoc per-call pricing — comes at a 40-50% premium per call, plus pricing discretion that varies engagement-to-engagement.

Result: predictable cost to the fund, predictable revenue to the network, and 60% of the retainer paying for capacity the fund never uses.

## Why this is rational for the major networks

Capacity allocation is the major networks' single hardest problem. They have a global senior-researcher bench staffed for the top 200 institutional accounts. Predictable annual retainers let them staff sensibly.

If every client paid per-call only, the network would lose 80% of its predictability and bench utilisation would crater in low-demand quarters. The retainer model isn't a customer rip-off — it's how the global service model works at scale.

## Why this is irrational for emerging-manager funds

The emerging-manager problem: you don't have $50k of annual research need yet, let alone $120k. But the retainer-network won't price below their minimum. So you either:

1. Sign a retainer you can't use efficiently (60% waste)
2. Pay 40-50% per-call premium on ad-hoc engagement (still expensive)
3. Don't run expert work at all (lose the alpha)

Most emerging managers default to option 3 — running far less primary research than the strategy justifies.

## The boutique-tier alternative

This is the structural opportunity that boutiques (FieldSignal included) exist to fill. No annual minimum. Per-call pricing matched to actual cheque-size economics. Senior researcher direct, not via account-management triage.

The tradeoff: smaller bench, slower turnaround on edge-case requests, less polish on deliverables. For most emerging-manager and mid-market use cases, these are fine.

## What to do if you're stuck in option 1

If you're at a fund that signed a retainer you're not using efficiently:

- **Audit utilisation honestly.** What was the actual per-call cost last year, including the retainer waste?
- **Compare against ad-hoc per-call at a boutique tier.** Net of the per-call premium, what's the breakeven point in annual calls?
- **Make the decision at renewal.** Most retainers escalate year-over-year; the year-2 renewal is usually a worse deal than year 1.

The math usually works for boutique-tier at sub-50 annual calls. Above that, the retainer is reasonable.
