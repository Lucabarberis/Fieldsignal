# The 90-Day Question

I've watched more than a dozen Voice of Customer programmes die in year 2.

The same pattern, every time: launched with energy, runs strongly through quarter 1 and 2, slowly fades over the next 6-9 months, formally cancelled or quietly defunded somewhere between months 14 and 18.

Programmes that survive year 2 do one specific thing. The dying programmes don't.

## The 90-day executive review

The surviving programmes run a 60-minute executive review every 90 days. Four to five senior leaders in the room. Quarterly findings on the table. Action assigned with named owners.

That's it. Sixty minutes per quarter.

The discipline sounds trivial. It isn't. Every dying VoC programme I've seen failed at this exact discipline.

## Why this specifically matters

VoC findings have one fundamental quality: they're uncomfortable.

A well-run VoC programme surfaces things like "your top-tier customers think your product is good enough but boring, and they're checking out alternatives quietly." Or "your renewal language is interpreted by buyers as bait-and-switch, and they're warning peers."

These findings, by their nature, ask for action from product, sales, marketing or leadership.

Without a forcing function — the quarterly review — the findings get circulated, nodded at, and quietly de-prioritised. The third quarter where this happens, the team running VoC loses faith in the programme. By the fourth, the budget conversation goes the wrong way.

## What the review looks like in practice

The companies whose VoC programmes survive run the review like this:

- **5 minutes:** rolling-12-month sentiment trend
- **20 minutes:** quarter's specific findings, with verbatim quotes
- **20 minutes:** discussion — what action does each finding require?
- **10 minutes:** named owners and action commitments
- **5 minutes:** kill list — what's not worth doing right now?

The named owners are the critical part. Without owners, findings drift. With owners, the team running VoC has someone to follow up with in 60 days.

## The dying-programme alternative

The dying programmes circulate quarterly findings via email. The findings are read. Nothing happens. Three quarters in, the team running VoC starts to feel like they're filing reports rather than influencing the business.

By month 12, the team is openly questioning whether the programme is worth the effort. By month 15, leadership agrees and the budget gets cut.

I've watched this pattern play out at companies ranging from €30M revenue startups to public companies. The pattern is invariant.

## What to do if you're starting a VoC programme

Two things, in order:

1. **Get the quarterly executive review on the calendar BEFORE the first findings are due.** Four 60-minute slots, 90 days apart, on the calendars of 4-5 named senior leaders.
2. **Build the findings format around the review.** Five-page deliverable, structured for the conversation. Not a 40-page slide deck.

Skip (1) and the programme will not survive year 2. I've never seen otherwise.
