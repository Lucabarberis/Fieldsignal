# Why Most Expert Calls Underperform

After 200+ structured expert engagements I can tell you the single highest predictor of call quality: **how well the brief was written**.

Not the network's bench depth. Not the expert's seniority. The brief.

A weak brief produces weak experts no matter how good the network is. A strong brief lifts even a mid-tier expert into useful territory.

## What a weak brief looks like

> "We're looking at the European cybersecurity market and want to talk to operators."

This is what most first-time buyers send. It produces three predictable failures:

1. The network sources broadly — too many expert profiles, none of them tightly matched
2. The matched expert can't tell what decision the buyer is making, so they default to category-level platitudes
3. The buyer wastes 60 minutes confirming what they already knew from analyst reports

## What a strong brief looks like

> "We're evaluating a Series B investment in [category-leader X]. The thesis hinges on whether mid-market CISOs are consolidating EDR vendors rather than running best-of-breed. We need 5-7 conversations with CISOs at €100M-€1B revenue companies who have made or are making that decision in the last 18 months. Avoid: anyone currently at X, anyone within 6 months of leaving X, and the obvious published analysts."

This produces three predictable wins:

1. The network knows exactly who to source — and who to exclude
2. The matched expert can articulate their direct experience in the first 90 seconds
3. The buyer extracts thesis-relevant signal in the first 30 minutes, not the last 5

## The 30-minute exercise

Spend 30 minutes on the brief before the first call. Include:

- **The decision being made.** Not the topic. The specific decision.
- **Five to seven specific questions.** Open-ended is fine; topics are not.
- **The expert profile.** Role, seniority, geography, recent context.
- **The experts to AVOID.** Former employer X, named competitors, anyone too recently departed.
- **A kill-criterion.** What would make you end the project early?

The exercise feels disproportionate. It isn't. The 30 minutes you spend on the brief saves you 5+ calls of wasted time across the project — and a much more useful project deliverable at the end.

## Why this is rarely done

Most buyers come from organisations that brief vaguely because the work has historically been done internally. When the work moves to an external network, the implicit context that made vague briefs work disappears.

Networks won't push back on a weak brief in the way an internal team would, because pushing back on a paying client feels rude. So you get the dud calls and never quite know why.

The fix is on the buyer side. Spend the 30 minutes. It's the most leveraged time in the entire engagement.
