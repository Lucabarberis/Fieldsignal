# The Channel Partner Conversation

For B2B companies with material channel-partner share, the single most underused intelligence source is the partner network itself.

Your channel partners are talking to your competitors' channel partners. They're moving product across categories. They see margin shifts, programme changes, and end-customer demand patterns that your sales team can't see.

And nine out of ten B2B companies don't systematically interview their channel partners about any of this.

## What partners actually see

Channel partners sit at a uniquely informed vantage point. In a quarterly conversation with a well-chosen distributor, you can surface:

- **Margin pressure across the category** — who's discounting, who's holding price, who's running margin-eroding promotions
- **Product-line health** — which competitor SKUs are gaining floor space, which are being de-prioritised
- **End-customer signal** — which buyer cohorts are buying more, which are pulling back
- **Programme-level changes** — competitor partner programmes that are gaining or losing partner enthusiasm
- **Personnel moves** — competitor channel-account-manager changes that signal strategic shifts

None of this shows up in your internal CRM. Most of it doesn't appear in analyst reports until 6-12 months after the channel sees it.

## Why companies don't do this

Three reasons, in roughly descending order:

1. **Worry about partner relationships.** Most B2B companies fear that asking partners structured questions will damage the commercial relationship.
2. **Internal capability.** Channel marketing teams typically lack the structured-interview discipline. Sales teams have it but introduce bias.
3. **It's not anyone's job.** Channel-partner intelligence falls between sales, marketing and competitive intelligence — and ends up being done by nobody.

The first concern is solvable: third-party interviewers, professional framing, partner opt-out. The second and third are organisational, not methodological.

## How to start

If you're a B2B company with channel-partner share and you don't currently do this:

1. **Pick 8-12 partners** across distribution tiers and geographies. Mix of strong and lukewarm partners.
2. **Commission third-party interviews.** Independent interviewers, structured guide. 30-45 minute conversations.
3. **Frame the value to the partner.** "We want to understand how the category is evolving and where our programme is working or not working." Most partners participate.
4. **Run quarterly.** Same partners. Track change over time.

Per-cycle cost: €5-15k for 8-12 interviews depending on partner seniority.

## What you'll find in the first cycle

You'll find three to five things you didn't know, including at least one competitive shift that's material to next-quarter planning. I've seen this hold across more than 20 B2B companies running this kind of programme for the first time.

The second and third cycles compound the value. By cycle 4, you have longitudinal pattern recognition that no other intelligence source produces.
