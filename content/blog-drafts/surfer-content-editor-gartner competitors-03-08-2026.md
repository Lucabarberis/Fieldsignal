# Gartner Competitors: 10 Alternatives Compared

Gartner, founded in 1972, is traditionally seen as a leader in IT research and consulting. It provides advisory services, syndicated reports (Magic Quadrants, Hype Cycles), and events for technology and business leaders across various industries. But a Gartner subscription runs roughly $25,000 to $60,000 per year, and that pricing locks out a lot of mid-market teams, smaller funds, and firms that only need research for a single project.

This comparison covers 10 alternatives that competitors to Gartner provide: market research, data-driven insights, and business strategy consulting, each with a different pricing model, methodology, or specialization. If you're a strategy analyst, consultant, PE/VC associate, or CIO evaluating your options, this is the list.

Gartner competes in the research and advisory space against various direct peers and consultancies. Niche operational firms like Everest Group offer tailored competitive intelligence in specific areas, such as IT sourcing and outsourcing strategies. Crowd-sourced vendor-shortlisting platforms like G2.com and PeerSpot challenge traditional advisory gates for buyers. The right alternative depends on what you actually need.

## How We Chose the Best Gartner Alternatives

We evaluated each provider on six criteria that matter to deal teams, strategy analysts, and leaders making procurement decisions:

1. **Research quality and analyst expertise.** Does the firm employ analysts with direct industry experience, or rely on secondary aggregation?

2. **Pricing models and accessibility.** Annual retainer, credit-based, pay-per-use, or project-based? What's the real cost?

3. **Speed of insights delivery.** Can you get usable data in days, or does it take weeks?

4. **Industry coverage and specialization.** Does the provider cover your sector with depth, or spread thin?

5. **Primary vs. secondary research capabilities.** Do they connect you with experts for calls, or only publish reports?

6. **Client service and support quality.** Do you get a dedicated analyst, or a login and a help desk?

Enterprises often subscribe to multiple analyst firms to gain a full view of a topic. This list helps you compare which ones earn that spend.

## Top 10 Gartner Alternatives for Research and Advisory Services

### 1. Forrester Research

Forrester Research is a direct competitor to Gartner. It offers market research reports and advisory services, focusing on technology strategy and customer experience, while Gartner emphasizes IT operations. Forrester publishes its own evaluation frameworks (Forrester Wave) and runs events for technology and business leaders.

**Why It Stands Out:** Forrester offers business impact advice on technology, with particular depth in customer experience research. Its analysts produce actionable guidance tied to revenue outcomes rather than pure technology assessment.

**Best For:** Companies prioritizing customer experience measurement and business technology impact analysis.

**Key Strengths:**

* Proprietary customer experience index that benchmarks performance across industries

* Strong advisory quality with analysts who collaborate directly with clients on strategy projects

* Event portfolio covering B2B marketing, CX, and technology leaders

**Possible Limitations:**

* Pricing is still premium, comparable to Gartner for full access

* Coverage thinner outside of North American and European markets

### 2. IDC (International Data Corporation)

IDC, or International Data Corporation, was founded in 1964 and focuses on IT market intelligence. It provides market intelligence for IT and telecommunications sectors, with forecasting models that track technology vendors, market sizing, and spending trends across 110+ countries.

**Why It Stands Out:** IDC's data on technology spending and market share is among the most cited in the information technology industry. Its tools let you size markets by geography, vertical, and vendor.

**Best For:** Organizations needing detailed market sizing, technology vendor analysis, and spend forecasting across telecommunications, manufacturing, and finance.

**Key Strengths:**

* Market data covering thousands of technology categories with quarterly updates

* Geographic reach across 110+ countries, strong in Asia-Pacific and EMEA

* Vendor tracking reports that buyers and investors use to compare players in a given field

**Possible Limitations:**

* Strategic advisory depth is lighter than firms like McKinsey or Deloitte

* Full platform access pricing can rival Gartner's subscription costs

### 3. McKinsey & Company

McKinsey & Company is one of the "Big Three" management consulting firms. McKinsey provides high-level strategic advisory services, published research through the McKinsey Global Institute, and industry-specific analysis used by executives and boards.

**Why It Stands Out:** McKinsey's frameworks for corporate strategy, digital transformation, and organizational performance are used by Fortune 500 companies and governments. Its research sits at the intersection of strategy and implementation.

**Best For:** Large enterprises seeking strategic transformation and implementation guidance at the board level.

**Key Strengths:**

* Strategic depth unmatched by pure research firms, covering everything from growth strategy to operational redesign

* Industry expertise spanning healthcare, financial services, energy, and technology

* Global reach with 130+ offices, enabling local market insights

**Possible Limitations:**

* Engagement costs typically start in the six figures, making it inaccessible for mid-market firms

* Research outputs are tied to consulting engagements, not available as standalone subscriptions

### 4. Deloitte Research

Deloitte is a major competitor in digital transformation consulting. Deloitte offers consulting services in digital transformation and publishes industry-specific research through Deloitte Insights, covering trends in AI, cloud, cybersecurity, and industry regulation.

**Why It Stands Out:** Deloitte integrates research directly with implementation consulting. You get analysis and a team that can execute on recommendations in the same engagement.

**Best For:** Organizations wanting research tied to actionable consulting services, particularly in regulated industries.

**Key Strengths:**

* Implementation capability that spans technology, risk, audit, and assurance practices

* Industry depth in financial services, government, healthcare, and energy

* Technology focus with dedicated AI and cloud research teams

**Possible Limitations:**

* Research objectivity can be questioned when the same advisory firm sells implementation services

* Pricing reflects the full consulting model, not a standalone research subscription

### 5. Frost & Sullivan

Frost & Sullivan provides growth opportunity analytics since 1961. The firm focuses on identifying growth trends, market entry strategies, and technology innovation across 50+ industries.

**Why It Stands Out:** Frost & Sullivan's entire model is built around growth consulting. Its research identifies where markets are heading and what it takes to enter them.

**Best For:** Companies planning market expansion, evaluating new verticals, or seeking data on emerging market growth.

**Key Strengths:**

* Growth-focused research with frameworks for market entry, competitive positioning, and innovation scoring

* Emerging market expertise, particularly in Latin America, Middle East, and Southeast Asia

* Custom research projects available alongside syndicated reports

**Possible Limitations:**

* Brand recognition is lower than Gartner or Forrester among North American IT leaders

* Research depth on specific technology vendors is lighter than IDC or 451 Research

### 6. Info-Tech Research Group

Info-Tech Research Group provides practical IT research aimed at mid-market enterprises. Its model delivers blueprints, toolkits, and step-by-step guidance for IT leaders and CIOs implementing technology solutions.

**Best For:** IT leaders and CIOs at mid-market companies who need actionable tools rather than high-level analysis.

**Key Strengths:**

* Practical focus with downloadable blueprints and templates that IT teams use directly

* Blueprint methodology breaks complex decisions (vendor selection, cloud migration) into step-by-step processes

* Cost-effective compared to Gartner, with pricing designed for mid-market budgets

**Possible Limitations:**

* Scope is limited to IT operations and technology decisions; doesn't cover broader business strategy

* Brand recognition outside North America is limited compared to global players

### 7. PwC Research

PwC publishes industry research through its Strategy& unit and PwC Global, covering risk management, regulatory shifts, and market trends across finance, healthcare, and energy.

**Why It Stands Out:** PwC's strength is the intersection of regulatory expertise and market research. If your business operates in heavily regulated industries, PwC's research reflects that context.

**Best For:** Organizations making informed decisions around regulatory changes, tax implications, and risk management.

**Key Strengths:**

* Regulatory expertise grounded in PwC's audit, tax, and assurance practices

* Global network spanning 150+ countries with local regulatory knowledge

* Industry specialization in financial services, healthcare, and energy

**Possible Limitations:**

* Research is often tied to PwC's consulting pipeline, not sold as standalone data products

* Accessibility for smaller firms is limited; PwC's research relationships favor enterprise clients

### 8. 451 Research (S&P Global Market Intelligence)

[451 Research](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/solutions/451-research), now part of S&P Global Market Intelligence, covers emerging technology markets, vendor analysis, and competitive intelligence for technology companies and investors. Omdia, another firm in this space, focuses on technology, media, and telecommunications research and serves a similar audience.

**Why It Stands Out:** 451 Research goes deeper on individual technology vendors than most advisory firms. Its analysts track startups, M&A activity, and technology adoption with granular vendor-level detail.

**Best For:** Technology companies, investors, and strategy teams tracking competitive positioning in fast-moving tech categories.

**Key Strengths:**

* Technology specialization covering cloud, security, AI, and data infrastructure

* Vendor-level insights with detailed competitive profiles and M&A analysis

* Direct analyst access for clients needing to discuss specific market dynamics

**Possible Limitations:**

* Scope is narrow compared to Gartner; 451 doesn't cover non-tech industries

* Brand awareness is lower among general business leaders who default to Gartner

### 9. Accenture Research

Accenture publishes research through Accenture Research, covering digital transformation, AI adoption, cloud migration, and workforce trends. TSIA, another provider in this space, focuses on technology and services industry insights and provides actionable benchmarks for technology businesses.

**Why It Stands Out:** Accenture's research is backed by implementation data from thousands of client engagements. Its insights on digital transformation reflect what actually works at scale, not just theory.

**Best For:** Large enterprises planning digital transformation and needing a provider that combines research with execution.

**Key Strengths:**

* Digital expertise informed by Accenture's work with 75%+ of the Fortune 500

* Implementation experience means research recommendations are tested, not hypothetical

* Global scale with operations in 120+ countries

**Possible Limitations:**

* Objectivity concerns exist, since Accenture sells implementation services alongside its research

* Pricing for advisory access reflects consulting-firm economics, not research-platform economics

## How to Choose the Right Gartner Alternative

### Choose Based on Research Type

If you need published reports, market sizing, and analyst frameworks, choose Forrester, IDC, or 451 Research. Direct analyst firms publish evaluation matrices and advise enterprise leaders on vendor selection. These are secondary research providers.

If you need primary research (talking to people who've actually done the job), choose FieldSignal. You'll get direct access to insiders via calls, surveys, and custom projects. Major professional services firms compete with Gartner in advisory and implementation spaces, but they don't offer the same operator-level depth that expert networks provide.

### Choose Based on Budget and Pricing Model

Gartner stock trades at a premium because the business model is built on annual retainers. Most of its competitors follow the same models.

If your budget supports $25,000+ annually, Forrester, IDC, and Info-Tech all offer subscription access. If you need flexibility, FieldSignal's pay-per-use model works for teams that run 3 to 10 expert calls per project without committing to a year-long contract. Management consultancies like McKinsey provide high-level digital and corporate strategy work, but at project costs that start well into six figures.

### Choose Based on Implementation Needs

If you want a firm that will both research a topic and help you execute on findings, Deloitte, Accenture, McKinsey, or PwC are the right fit. The trade-off: objectivity. When the same company sells you the analysis and the implementation, you should question whether the recommendations are shaped by what they can deliver.

If you want research that's independent of any implementation agenda, choose a pure research firm (Forrester, IDC) or an expert network (FieldSignal) where the experts have no commercial relationship with your decision.

## Which Option Is Best for You?

Choosing comes down to three questions: what type of research you need, what you can spend, and whether you need implementation support.

* Choose Forrester if you need customer-focused business research with analyst collaboration

* Choose IDC if you need detailed technology market data and vendor-level performance tracking

* Choose McKinsey if you're a large enterprise needing strategic transformation at the board level

* Choose FieldSignal if you need primary research from industry insiders without annual commitments or inflated per-call pricing
