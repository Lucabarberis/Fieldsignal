## What Similarweb is (and why it's priced like this)

Similarweb is a digital intelligence platform that models website traffic, app usage, audience behavior, and channel performance across millions of domains and apps globally. It doesn't just tell you how a site ranks for keywords. It estimates the full picture of how traffic flows into and through a competitor's digital presence.

Core categories of similarweb data include:

* Web traffic estimates and engagement metrics by device, country, and channel

* App intelligence covering installs, DAU/MAU, retention, and sentiment analysis

* Search intelligence and ai search intelligence for tracking organic search and AI-generated citations

* Shopper and retail intelligence for marketplace and eCommerce analytics

* Stock intelligence and investor-grade data feeds tied to public-company digital signals

This differs from basic SEO analytics tools. Similarweb tracks entire markets and competitors by channel, not just keyword research rankings or backlink analysis in isolation. Pricing reflects that breadth (web, app, stock, shopper), depth (up to 37 months of historical data, 100+ countries), and delivery options (UI, exports, api access, data feeds). PE/VC, corp dev, and strategy teams typically justify the cost by folding it into a bigger digital marketing, GTM, or investment research stack.

## Web Intelligence pricing tiers

Web intelligence is the core product most people mean when they say "Similarweb." Marketing teams, SEO practitioners, and market research analysts use it for competitor traffic analysis, channel breakdowns, and audience data.

Each tier in 2026:

**Starter**: $199/month (month-to-month) or roughly $125/month with annual billing. One user. About 3 months of historical data. You get basic traffic and competitor data, demographics, marketing channel splits, and competitor alerts. Good for solos and very small teams doing occasional competitive research.

**Team**: From around $14,000/year. Multiple users. More data depth with granular engagement data by country and subdomain. Collaboration features and some export capacity. This is where most mid-market digital marketing and strategy teams land.

**Business**: From around $35,000/year. Broader history (often 12+ months), larger export limits, and api access becomes possible. Used by enterprise digital teams and research organizations that need reliable traffic insights across dozens of domains. Data hub access opens up at this level for some contracts.

**Enterprise**: Custom pricing. Multi-region coverage, enterprise grade security, SSO, dedicated support, and often part of six-figure budgets that bundle multiple modules. Enterprise plans typically include the full 37 months of historical data.

The [7-day free trial](https://www.similarweb.com/packages/web/) sits on top of Web Intelligence. It caps you at about 15 actions per day, offers no API, and excludes premium modules. It's enough to test a few workflows, not enough for a full M&A or digital marketing project.

Research buyers typically use web intelligence for market sizing, share-of-traffic analysis, competitive benchmarking, and validating channel-mix narratives in a CIM.

## AI Search Intelligence (AEO) and SEO / Ads bundles

Similarweb's ai search intelligence, often branded around AEO and "AI brand visibility," is a separate, more self-serve pricing track. It's oriented around search and content visibility rather than full-market traffic data.

Three common self-serve bundles with 2026 pricing from [Similarweb's packages page](https://www.similarweb.com/packages/ai-search/):

**AEO Intelligence**: The AEO Intelligence plan starts at $99 per month (annual billing) or $129/month monthly. One seat, roughly 150 prompts, about 3 months of history. Focused on tracking how your brand appears in AI-generated answers, citation analysis, and AI traffic measurement.

**AEO + SEO + Competitive Intel**: Around $333/month annually or $399/month monthly. Adds keyword research, rank tracking, site audit, backlink analysis, and broader competitive intelligence. This is where organic search visibility and traditional SEO monitoring come in.

**AEO + SEO + Ads + Competitive Intel**: Roughly $542/month annually or $649/month monthly. Adds paid search, display, and social ad intelligence with creative and spend signals. Covers both organic and paid channels.

The main differences between bundles come down to prompt volume, channel coverage (organic only vs. organic + paid), historical windows, and how many domains or keywords you can track.

These bundles suit marketing teams and agencies doing ongoing SEO and performance marketing. They're less relevant for one-off M&A or investment use cases. Compared with the higher-ticket web intelligence or shopper intelligence plans, these AI-driven packages have clearer pricing and shorter commitment options, which some smaller teams prefer for tracking market trends without a large annual contract.

## Other key modules: App, Sales, Shopper, and Stock Intelligence

These modules turn Similarweb from a single analytics tool into a broader platform covering apps, B2B sales, retail, and public-market digital signals.

**App Intelligence**: Custom pricing only. Used by mobile product and growth teams. Tracks installs, MAU/DAU, retention, audience profiles, and app data across app stores. Covers mobile growth patterns and competitive app rankings. History depth in enterprise contracts goes up to 37 months.

**Sales Intelligence**: [Individual plan](https://account.similarweb.com/packages/sales/) at about $129/month or $1,200/year. Business tier from roughly $16,000/year with multiple users, CRM integrations, buyer intent signals, and larger contact data quotas. Sales teams use it for prospecting and enrichment. Enterprise is custom.

**Shopper Intelligence**: Starter around $15,000/year, Business around $25,000/year, Enterprise custom. Built for retail and eCommerce teams analyzing category performance, retailer share, and shopper behavior across marketplaces. Conversion analysis and long term trend analysis on ASIN-level data are core features here.

**Stock Intelligence**: Custom pricing. Oriented toward investors. Includes premium stocks coverage and stock feeds that tie traffic data, app data, and stock performance to public-company tickers. Investors or corporate development teams sometimes buy only stock or web intelligence data feeds and skip UI seats entirely, which changes the pricing conversation.

Each module adds meaningfully to total annual cost. Add-on modules can increase total subscription costs by $20,000-$60,000/year per module. When your research scope crosses multiple domains (app + shopper + web), price bundles explicitly rather than accepting a large "all-in" quote.

## Similarweb API access, pricing, and documentation

API access is where Similarweb becomes a data infrastructure product, not just an analytics tool. Engineering teams, data scientists, and systematic investors use the similarweb api to feed traffic data directly into dashboards, models, and CRM systems.

API pricing is quote-based. The main cost drivers are data credits consumed, number of endpoints accessed, refresh frequency, and whether you use the REST API or batch api for larger pulls. API access may incur separate charges beyond the base plan. Api pricing details aren't published, so you'll need a sales conversation.

API can be purchased in three ways:

* Included or discounted with business and enterprise tiers of Web Intelligence

* As a standalone product for engineering and quant teams

* As OEM licensing for embedding digital intelligence into third-party tools

The data credit model works roughly like this: you pay for data returned, not raw api calls. Monthly credit resets and rate limits vary by similarweb plan. This matters if you're pulling historical or high-frequency data.

Api documentation is publicly available and covers web, app, keyword, stock, and technology data endpoints. Many teams trial the free DigitalRank API first to validate data quality before committing to a paid plan. Concrete use cases include feeding digital insights into BI tools, custom digital marketing dashboards, and quant factor models. For the PE/VC and corporate strategy audience, this is where data driven decision making meets raw data infrastructure.

## Free version, free trial, and what you actually get for free

Similarweb offers three levels of free access:

* A limited free version on Similarweb.com for quick checks

* A 7-day free trial with constrained daily usage

* A free DigitalRank API for global ranking data

The free version shows high-level website traffic estimates, a small history window, a few top traffic sources and keywords, and sample app rankings. It's fine for ad-hoc curiosity. It won't support structured competitive research or client deliverables.

The similarweb free trial works like this in 2026: users can perform around 15 actions per day during the trial. You get partial access to web intelligence features but no app, shopper, or stock modules, and no full API data. A credit card is required to activate the free trial. The trial automatically renews to a paid plan if not canceled. Set a calendar reminder.

The free trial includes limited access to Web Intelligence features. The free plan is not viable for professional use. Serious use cases (board memos, IC memos, client deliverables) require a paid plan because you'll hit caps quickly and need reliable exports, audience data, and history.

Use the trial phase tactically: test critical workflows, validate data accuracy on your own domains or portfolio companies, and collect questions for your negotiation with sales.

## Hidden costs, add-ons, and renewal surprises to watch for

Headline plan prices rarely equal total similarweb cost once add-ons and renewals are in play. Here's what to watch for.

Typical extra costs beyond the base subscription:

* Add-on intelligence modules (App, Shopper, Stock, conversion analysis, extra AI prompts)

* Additional user seats beyond the initial bundle, often priced higher mid-contract than pre-negotiated

* API and data credit overages when volume exceeds initial assumptions

* Foreign transaction fees that may apply for international payments

* Onboarding and implementation services, sometimes $2,000-$10,000 depending on scope

Annual plans typically lock users in for a full year. Most enterprise contracts are 12 months or longer with renewal uplifts [baked into the terms](https://blog.contentforce.ai/similarweb-pricing/). Renewal costs can include additional fees not disclosed upfront. Without caps, renewal increases of 5-15% are common.

Push for these in negotiation:

* Written caps on renewal price increases

* Clear definitions of included modules vs. paid add-ons

* Transparency on data credit tiers, rate limits, and overage pricing

* Annual billing terms that allow mid-year module adjustments

Include legal or procurement in contract review, especially if your company is sensitive to data-processing and compliance clauses around digital intelligence data sources. These hidden costs can turn a $35,000/year plan into $50,000+ if you're not careful.

Similarweb is objectively expensive. Semrush's Pro plan starts at $139.95 monthly. Ahrefs offers plans starting at $129 per month. The question isn't "is it expensive" but "does it pay for itself in your workflow."

Where Similarweb usually is worth it:

* Enterprise digital marketing teams managing multi-country spend and needing attribution context across traffic sources and channels

* PE/VC and public-market investors modeling revenue or channel mix using web/app traffic and shopper intelligence as leading indicators. Investors require due diligence insights to make informed decisions. Strategic insights are crucial for corporate development and M&A teams.

Where it may be overkill:

* Small sites under roughly 100K monthly visits, where traffic estimates get noisy

* Teams that only need SEO data and get better price/performance from Semrush or Ahrefs

* Occasional one-off research questions that don't justify enterprise contracts or even annual plans
