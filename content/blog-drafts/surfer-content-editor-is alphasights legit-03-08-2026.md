## What is AlphaSights and how does an expert network work?

AlphaSights is a global information services firm focused on expert networks. It's a "knowledge on demand" service [founded in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaSights) that connects clients with subject-matter experts for short, paid knowledge-sharing engagements. Expert networks connect organizations seeking specialized knowledge with industry professionals.

The business model works in four steps:

1. A client has a research question (e.g., "How does vendor X price its logistics platform in Southeast Asia?").

2. The client service team sources and screens experts who can answer that question.

3. Expert networks arrange one-on-one consultations between clients and industry experts, typically via phone or video.

4. The client uses the insights for due diligence, strategy, or market research.

AlphaSights has over 2,000 employees worldwide, spread across nine offices including New York and San Francisco. AlphaSights reports having facilitated millions of expert interactions globally. Clients include management consulting firms, private equity firms, and hedge funds. AlphaSights clients also include hedge funds and investment banks.

Expert networks aren't job-placement agencies or coaching platforms. If you got a LinkedIn message from an AlphaSights associate, they're not trying to hire you or sell you a course. They're asking if you'd be willing to talk to one of their clients for pay.

This model is now standard across major expert networks like GLG and Third Bridge, and also used by smaller boutique firms like FieldSignal.

## Is AlphaSights legit for experts who get contacted on LinkedIn?

Here's the common scenario: you're a mid-career professionals in finance or tech, and you get a LinkedIn message from an AlphaSights associate in New York or London offering a few hundred dollars for a 30 to 60 minute call. You've never heard of them. You want to know if it's real.

In most cases, these invitations are a legitimate opportunity. Clients can engage experts through phone consultations lasting 30-60 minutes. AlphaSights associates aren't trying to sell you anything or extract confidential employer data.

Here's how the entire process typically works:

1. AlphaSights finds you through desktop research or LinkedIn search.

2. You get a message with a short project synopsis, target topics, proposed hourly rate, and expected timing.

3. If you're interested, you do a quick screening call and fill out a compliance questionnaire.

4. You're booked for the consultation, join a call, and share your expertise.

AlphaSights pays experts per minute of consultation. Payment is typically received within a few days after a call. There's no cost to join, and you're never asked to recruit friends or colleagues.

You should still verify basics: check that the email comes from an @alphasights.com domain, look up the associate on LinkedIn, and get written confirmation of your rate and scope. If someone asks you for an upfront fee or pushes you to disclose confidential information, that's not standard practice. Decline and move on.

## Who qualifies as an AlphaSights expert and what projects look like

You don't need to be a C-level executive. Many projects value middle managers, former employees, customers, and operators with recent, on-the-ground knowledge. Industries covered by AlphaSights include technology and healthcare, though the range is much broader.

Typical qualifications include:

* 2 to 5+ years in a specific niche (e.g., revenue operations at a B2B SaaS company).

* Buyer-side experience, like choosing a logistics vendor or evaluating a software platform.

* A recent role at a company a client is researching.

* Regional knowledge for market entry questions in the Middle East, Asia, or other target geographies.

The bar is usually this: could you walk a sophisticated investor through a 30-minute overview of how a product, process, or market really works?

Common project formats:

* 1:1 phone or video calls (the core format).

* Company-focused research where multiple industry experts are called on the same target.

* Industry overviews comparing multiple competitors.

* Occasional paid surveys or written Q&A.

The client service team often runs several calls with different experts on the same topic. No single person is expected to have every answer. Most experts spend 5 to 20 minutes prepping and 30 to 60 minutes on the call. There's no ongoing obligation.

If you're a PE/VC associate or corporate strategist, these are the same types of profiles you'll want to target for your own projects, whether through AlphaSights or a boutique like FieldSignal.

## What types of consulting work and pay rates does AlphaSights offer?

AlphaSights is primarily a "calls business." Most revenue and expert earnings come from short 1:1 consultations, not long-term advisory roles. Here's what the alphasights offer looks like in practice:

* **Ad hoc 1:1 expert calls.** The bread and butter. A client talks to an expert for 30 to 60 minutes on a focused topic.

* **Multi-expert panels.** Separate calls with several experts, same client, same research question.

* **In person meetings and full day workshops.** Arranged occasionally for larger engagements or deeper investigations.

* **Surveys.** AlphaSights offers bespoke surveys to quickly aggregate market data and market opinions from experts.

On the pay side, experts can earn $250 to $500 per hour depending on seniority and domain. Senior executives can charge over $1,000 per hour for niche or high-demand topics. [Pay is calculated per minute](https://mycrolance.com/resources/alphasights), so a 45-minute call pays three-quarters of the agreed hourly rate.

On the client side, AlphaSights charges clients $1,000 per hour or more for expert consultations. The markup between what an expert earns and what the client pays is standard across expert networks. It covers sourcing, project management, compliance, and scheduling.

Scheduling is handled by the client service team, which coordinates time zones and sends calendar invites. Experts join a dedicated conference line or platform.

## Compliance, confidentiality, and how AlphaSights manages risk

Expert networks operate in highly regulated environments regarding information handling. AlphaSights, like GLG and Third Bridge, invests heavily in compliance because most of its clients can't afford regulatory mistakes.

The core rules are straightforward:

* No material non-public information (MNPI).

* No confidential information covered by NDAs or employment contracts.

* No selective disclosure that would breach securities rules or data privacy laws.

AlphaSights has a strong compliance program to protect client confidentiality. Experts must complete compliance training and legal agreements before consultations. They receive a "no-go topics" list and must confirm they won't discuss restricted information.

The [compliance team includes over 60 legal and compliance professionals](https://www.alphasights.com/compliance/) across its global offices. They screen experts for current vs. former employment status, exclude current employees of target companies in regulated contexts, and can cut off a call if the discussion drifts into prohibited areas.

Some users reported unpaid consultations after calls ended. In most cases, this links back to compliance stops, where a call was flagged because it veered into restricted territory. It's frustrating, but it's part of the risk control structure.

For clients, working through a reputable expert network is safer than sourcing experts directly without a compliance framework. Boutique networks such as FieldSignal follow equivalent compliance standards while offering more transparent pricing, making them accessible to smaller funds and mid-market companies.

## Common misconceptions: spam, scams, and "pyramid scheme" worries

Aggressive outreach, cold LinkedIn messages, and repeat emails sometimes lead people to wonder if AlphaSights is spammy or a scam. Let's break this down.

AlphaSights is not a pyramid scheme:

* AlphaSights associates are salaried employees, not commission-based recruiters.

* Experts aren't asked to pay to join or recruit others.

* Revenue comes from clients that pay for market research, not from expert recruitment.

Common misconceptions:

1. **"If they're paying so much for 1 hour, it must be a scam."** Clients pay a premium because time-sensitive insights from the right person can inform a multimillion-dollar deal. The demand for this expertise is real.

2. **"It's a data harvesting ploy."** The company operates under strict compliance boundaries. Calls are focused on professional expertise, not personal financial data.

3. **"It's multi-level marketing."** There are no downlines, no commissions for bringing in friends, and no schedule of tiered rewards.

Outreach feels spammy because associates have large call targets and use template-style messages. That's a volume-driven sales operation, not fraud.

To verify a real outreach: check the email domain, look up the person on the company website, and confirm scope in writing. "Annoying outreach" and "fraudulent business model" are very different things. AlphaSights sits firmly in the former for some people, not the latter.
